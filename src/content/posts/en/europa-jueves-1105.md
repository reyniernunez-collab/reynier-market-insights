> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 5, 2026, 08:12 CT:** Thursday's Europe session had one clear headliner: the Bank of England. It raised rates as expected, but the vote split came out tougher than the market had anticipated. At the same time, the dollar recovered much of what it gave up in the Asia session. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was Korea dragging chips lower on regulatory fears, Chinese tech rebounding and the dollar touching another low. **The Europe session turned the dollar around**: the index climbed from its overnight low to the 101.6 area, with US short-term rates rising again.

My read: **the political premium of the Democratic sweep is still in the background, but it no longer drives the day's move**. The dollar returned to the top of the short-term range many analysts had in mind after the election (approx. 101.00–101.50) and edged above it. From here, US data will decide whether the rebound holds: jobless claims today and payrolls tomorrow. In London, the key for December is not today's hike but the guidance.

## Analysis

### The Bank of England: expected hike, tougher vote

- **The decision:** a 25 basis point hike to **4.00%**, fully priced.
- **The vote:** **8–1**, with the lone dissenter calling for **50 basis points**. Calendars expected two votes to hold; there were none. (⚠️ An early summary reported 9–0; the official statement says 8–1.)
- **The tone:** two committee members see a case for **another near-term hike** (⚠️ single-source). The governor stressed this is not the start of an automatic cycle, but said it need not be the last if the Middle East conflict keeps up the pressure; inflation is seen heading toward approx. 5% by mid-2027.
- **December:** the odds of another hike rose, but sources disagree: approx. **30%** on a prediction market versus approx. **50%** in a traders' summary (⚠️ conflicting).

### Dollar, sterling and euro

- **Dollar index:** recovers from approx. **101.37 to approx. 101.6** during the Europe session, from the Asia session low near 101.2. The **US 2-year** rises to approx. **4.61%** (⚠️ single-source).
- **GBP/USD:** slips ahead of the announcement and sits at approx. **1.346** (low near 1.344 mid-morning). The **UK construction PMI sinks to 43.6** (approx. 46.5 expected), but the central bank's tone outweighed the data.
- **EUR/USD:** the weakest of the morning, from a high near 1.125 to approx. **1.122**. **Euro area producer prices** rise **11.1% year on year**, above expectations (⚠️ consensus figures range from 10.6% to 10.9%).
- **USD/JPY:** climbs to approx. **158.4**, once again without the yen benefiting from the tech slide.
- **Stocks:** Nasdaq 100 futures are down approx. 0.6%, while European shares rise except for tech.

### Oil and gold

After plunging more than 8% in Wednesday's New York session, **Brent rebounds** from a Europe session low of approx. $94.7 to near **$97** (approx. +2.4%), and **WTI** is back at approx. **$89.5**. The market still bets the new Congress will speed up an end to the Iran conflict, but the previous day's drop was so large that it left room for a bounce. **Gold** hovers around **$4,136**.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Claims in line (approx. 190–210K) (most likely):** the dollar trades inside the range Europe left and the market waits for tomorrow's jobs data.
2. **Very low claims:** would reinforce a December Fed hike, push the 2-year higher and extend the dollar's rebound, with the euro most exposed.
3. **Soft claims plus hawkish BoE and ECB speakers:** would cap the dollar and give sterling and the euro some room.
4. **Iran:** verified progress in mediation would weigh on crude again; a verified escalation would send it back up and favor the franc and gold.
