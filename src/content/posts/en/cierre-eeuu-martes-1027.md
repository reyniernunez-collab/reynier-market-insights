> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 27, 2026, 3:44 PM CT:** One day before the Fed, and with earnings from companies worth more than $10 trillion combined just around the corner, Wall Street chose optimism: chips pulled higher again, the US consumer surprised to the upside and the dollar finished the day stronger than it started. Oil, by contrast, kept shedding premium. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 rose **0.6%** to **7,722.60**, the Nasdaq Composite **1.2%** to **26,477.81** and the Dow Jones just **0.03%** to **52,377.57**. The gap between indices tells the story: the gain was tech-driven, while the Dow was held back by Boeing's slide. The VIX eased to the **17.5** area.

The data point of the day was consumer confidence, which jumped to **91.7** from a revised **84.2**, when the market expected around 81. Together with solid durable goods orders, the message is that the economy heads into the Fed stronger than expected, and that showed up at the front end of the curve and in the dollar.

Oil kept easing: the claimed attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery during the Europe session did not affect supply, and the market no longer buys every Gulf headline.

## Analysis

### Stocks: the AI trade back in front

The semiconductor index rose **3.6%** and the Nasdaq 100 **1.3%**, the sector's second straight session of recovery. Two pieces of news fed the mood. First, Meta's **$25 billion** share sale ended with demand about five times the offering, a sign that money is still willing to fund the AI race (Meta's stock still slipped approx. **1.6%** on dilution). Second, Oracle expanded its partnership with OpenAI and announced cloud services running on Cerebras chips, handing that chipmaker another big customer in its challenge to Nvidia. Oracle rose approx. **3.6%**.

Elsewhere, UPS gained approx. **5.8%** on a better-than-expected outlook, while Boeing plunged approx. **11%** after lowering its delivery target, the biggest drag on the Dow.

The real test comes tomorrow after the New York close with Microsoft and Alphabet, and Thursday with Amazon. What they say about data-center spending and how they pay for it will decide whether this bounce has legs or last week's selloff repeats.

### Data: a more upbeat consumer than expected

Consumer confidence rose **7.5 points** to **91.7**, its best level since June. Both the view of current conditions (**120.4**) and expectations (**72.6**) improved, although expectations only recover part of three straight monthly declines. The relief matches the drop in gasoline prices from September to October, but households still complain about the cost of living and plan to spend less on durable goods and services in the coming months. In other words: better mood, but not necessarily more spending.

September durable goods orders rose **1.8%**, driven by aircraft. Excluding transportation, they rose **0.5%**, and capital goods excluding defense and aircraft (the business-investment gauge the market watches most) also rose **0.5%**, somewhat below expectations (⚠️ forecasts for this component varied across sources).

### Bonds and the dollar: the data gives the dollar the support it lacked

In the morning the dollar was rising without help from yields; after consumer confidence, the front end joined in. The 2-year finished in the **4.74%** area, about 2–3 bp above Monday's New York close (⚠️ readings vary by time), and the 10-year was roughly flat around **5.0%**. The 5-year auction cleared at **4.845%**, essentially in line with the pre-auction level and with somewhat better demand than last time.

The dollar index rose approx. **0.4%** to the **102.4** area, a weekly high. EUR/USD fell to approx. **1.113**, its fifth straight down day, with the euro paying for its own problems (France and energy) on top of dollar strength. GBP/USD slipped to approx. **1.330** ahead of tomorrow's UK budget, and USD/JPY climbed to nearly **158**, with the yen once again ignoring Tokyo's warnings. Gold rose approx. **0.8%** to the **$4,070** area (December futures).

### Oil: the Gulf makes noise, but supply isn't moving

December Brent fell **$1.93** to settle at **$113.88**; WTI dropped approx. **$1.9** to settle around **$99.9**, below $100 for the first time this week. The Ras Tanura attack lifted crude during the Europe session, but the move deflated once it was confirmed there was no supply impact. In the afternoon, Washington insisted oil is still flowing through the Strait of Hormuz. Another Houthi claim, this time against Riyadh, had no Saudi confirmation.

⚠️ Contract reminder: December Brent expires this week and January trades well below it, so price references change a lot depending on which contract you look at.

### Israel: an election without a clear winner

The first exit polls in Israel's election give the opposition bloc an edge, but without a majority of its own; projections differ and the count is just starting (⚠️ preliminary results). A change of government could shift the read on regional risk, but for now it is not a price driver.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If Australian inflation (tonight) comes in strong on its core measure:** the RBA's Nov. 3 meeting, currently the least settled, would look almost done and the Australian dollar would gain support; a moderate print would put it back in doubt.
- **If the Reserve Bank of New Zealand hikes and leaves the door open to more:** the kiwi would find support; if it hikes but softens guidance, the market could unwind part of what is priced for December. Views on what it will do are split (⚠️).
- **If tomorrow's Fed confirms December is still on the table:** the dollar would consolidate today's gains; a more cautious tone would hit a market heavily long dollars, with the risk of a quick correction.
- **If Microsoft and Alphabet disappoint on spending or financing:** the chip rebound could unwind like last week. If they convince, tech would keep holding up the index even if the rest of the market hesitates.
- **If real damage to Gulf infrastructure or Hormuz is confirmed:** oil would become the driver again, pressuring the euro and the yen.
