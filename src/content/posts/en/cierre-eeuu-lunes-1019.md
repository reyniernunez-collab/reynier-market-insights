> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 19, 2026, 3:44 PM CT:** The New York session opens the week with what the Asia session already hinted at: buying in semiconductors after last week's beating. But the bounce was narrow, Europe closed in the red on France, and Canada's inflation print undid part of the hawkish bet that had been built ahead of its release.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 finished at **7,690.73** (+0.4%), the Nasdaq Composite at **26,430.89** (+1.1%) and the Dow at **51,966.03** (−0.1%). The Nasdaq 100 rose approx. **1.2%** and the semiconductor index approx. **1.6%**, snapping the eight-session losing streak it carried into Friday's close.

The key nuance is breadth: the rebound was concentrated in tech and consumer discretionary, while most S&P 500 sectors finished in the red and the Dow couldn't hold gains. At the same time, US bonds didn't join in: the 10-year yield rose to approx. **5.13–5.14%** from approx. **5.10%** on Friday, with oil back near $110. The dollar leaned on that rise in yields and on a weak euro: the DXY closed near **102**.

## Analysis

### Europe: the Europe session closes in the red on France

The Europe session was the weak spot of the day. At the Europe close, the Stoxx 600 lost approx. **0.8%**, the CAC 40 approx. **1.1%**, the DAX approx. **1.2%** and the FTSE 100 approx. **1.1%**. Violent weekend protests over the cost of living put French debt under pressure again: the French 10-year premium over Germany moved in the approx. **145–148 bp** area (⚠️ the exact figure depends on the measurement), near the highs of the eurozone debt crisis. With little fiscal room and snap elections in Spain next month, political noise in the region won't fade quickly.

The euro touched **1.1185** in the Europe session, recovered during the morning thanks to a soft dollar, and slipped again in New York to approx. **1.121**. My read: as long as the French premium doesn't calm down, the euro struggles to get back to Friday's levels (approx. 1.124 at the New York close), even though short-rate differentials make it look rather cheap on longer horizons.

Oil had its moment in Europe too: Brent dropped to approx. **$106.8** after the waiver of Russia-linked sanctions and a cut to OPEC's demand forecast, then recovered all of that ground.

### Tech: a chip bounce, not a market bounce

After a week in which the Nasdaq lost approx. **4.5%** and chips chained eight sessions lower, the market bought back into the thesis that spending on artificial-intelligence infrastructure will keep supporting earnings. Talks emerged over a possible compute contract worth about **$45 billion** for a major AI developer (⚠️ unconfirmed reports), a reminder of the scale of investment at stake.

The real test comes this week with Meta and Tesla results: the market needs to see that this spending turns into sustainable growth and justifies demanding valuations. Today was a positioning bounce; there's still no answer to the financing question that dominated last week.

### Canada: CPI deflates part of the hawkish bet

Ahead of the print, the market had sharply raised the odds of a Bank of Canada hike on **October 28** and the loonie was at nearly one-month highs. September CPI came in at **3.1%** year over year (from 3.0%; 3.2% expected) and **0.1%** month over month (0.2% expected). The headline rises on gasoline, but what the central bank watches cooled: the median eased to **1.9%** and the trim to **1.8%**, an average of **1.85%** versus the approx. **1.95%** expected. Excluding gasoline, inflation slowed to **2.2%**.

That's the scenario the pre-release thesis flagged as weakening the case for an October hike: the market had bought a hot print and got a soft one in the core measures. USD/CAD went from approx. **1.408** in the morning to approx. **1.411** at the New York close, still below Friday's **1.4137**. The energy-driven inflation risk is still there, but spillover into non-energy prices isn't showing up in these numbers yet.

### Bonds, dollar and crude

A US 10-year back above **5.1%** signals that expensive oil keeps capping relief in bonds, even as tech stocks recover. The Fed remains in its blackout period: the odds of a hike on October 28 stay in the approx. **12–18%** area depending on the metric (⚠️ readings diverge), and December remains the meeting most likely to bring a move.

In FX, USD/JPY rose to approx. **158.5** with yields, and AUD/USD ended near **0.698**, still supported by China's data from the Asia session. Brent closed the New York session at approx. **$109.3–109.6**, almost unchanged versus Friday: the Russia-driven drop in Europe was offset by ongoing attacks on ships in the Gulf. I'm not quoting WTI: the November contract expires tomorrow and prices across contracts aren't comparable today.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If the Asia session confirms the chip rebound (Seoul, Taiwan):** tech buys time until this week's earnings; if not, today's bounce stays an isolated one-session relief.
- **If big-tech results justify the AI spending:** the rebound could broaden beyond chips; if they disappoint, last week's financing doubt returns.
- **If the French premium keeps widening:** Europe and the euro remain the weak side of the map; calm in the streets and in parliament would be the first relief.
- **If oil holds near $110:** US yields have little room to fall and the dollar keeps support; a clear drop in crude would change that read.
- **Canada:** with core measures easing, an October hike now needs another justification (energy or inflation expectations); without it, the loonie loses part of the support it gained ahead of the print.
