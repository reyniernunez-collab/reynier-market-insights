> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 12, 2026, 11:15 CT:** The Europe session undoes the overnight relief. Headlines out of Saudi Arabia and doubts over the Russia–Ukraine energy truce push Brent back into the ~$106 area, the dollar firms and the euro gives ground. European stocks keep the gains they built early, while Wall Street futures wait, flat and near record highs, for the banks and CPI.

## Executive Summary

In our Asia session post, the read was containment: crude hadn't bought the weekend's fear. The Europe session changes that picture. Riyadh extends the suspension of its airport, the Saudi-led coalition reports intercepting drones and missiles, Kyiv accuses Moscow of breaching the “energy truce,” and Aramco's chief executive warns that inventory pressure will persist until Hormuz fully reopens. Brent moves from ~~$103 to **~$106–107** and WTI to **~~$93–94**.

The market isn't treating this as a flight to safety but as an **inflation shock**: the dollar rises across the board (DXY ~**102.4–102.6**), EUR/USD drops from ~1.121 to **~1.116–1.118**, gold slips below $4,200, and neither the franc nor the yen acts as a haven. European stocks close green (Stoxx 600 ~**+0.6%**, preliminary single-source figure), backed by the first half of the session. S&P futures are essentially flat near all-time highs; the Nasdaq has already lost Friday's New York close reference. With US cash Treasuries shut for Columbus Day, the oil-to-rates transmission only shows up in futures: what rises is the probability for **December**, not October.

## Analysis

### Oil: from Asia's “contained” to Europe's repricing

The price path tells the story. Brent touched ~$105.6 at Sunday's reopen, fell toward ~$102.4 during the Asia session and began recovering late in that session. In Europe, the rebound took hold around the Saudi and Aramco headlines: by about 6:30 CT Brent was near **$106.1** and WTI **$93.1** (two references agree), and by mid-morning CT they were trading near **$106.9** and **$93.9**. Important: the day **hasn't settled**; any figure presented as today's oil “close” is intraday.

A word on causality: the recovery started **before** the toughest headlines. The news consolidated the move, but didn't necessarily start it. That matters for judging how fragile it is: there's a geopolitical premium, but also a tight physical market. The bottleneck is now less about missing barrels and more about **shipping** (a nearly fully booked tanker fleet, longer routes, expensive freight), which raises the cost of crude delivered to Europe more than WTI. A Brent–WTI spread near ~$13 reflects that.

One relevant institutional read this morning: a major bank raised its December Brent forecast to **$90** from $85, with upside risks. With spot near ~$106, price trades well above that base case. Translation: there's a premium that could unwind on verified de-escalation, but nobody expects it to vanish quickly.

### Dollar and euro: oil as a tax on Europe

The cleanest move of the Europe session is in currencies. DXY rises from ~102.2 overnight to **~102.4–102.6**, near session highs. EUR/USD loses some 30–50 pips from ~1.121 to **~1.116–1.118**: Europe imports its energy and pays the most expensive crude in the chain. Sterling gives up a little less; USD/JPY and USD/CHF rise, a sign the market isn't seeking shelter but hedging against more inflation and higher US rates.

In France, yields rise on political uncertainty over fiscal policy, with parliament debating the budget's revenue side this week. That's another drag on the euro: expensive energy on one side, a fiscal premium on the other.

Gold loses the **$4,200** area and prints session lows (~$4,190 in futures). Geopolitics supports it, but a strong dollar and expectations of higher rates weigh more today.

### Equities: Europe keeps its early gains; Wall Street waits

The first half of the Europe session was clearly positive: banks and autos led, Spain's IBEX gained around 1% and the Stoxx 600 climbed while crude was still soft and on the EU–China hybrid deal (a quota of **350,000** cars a year with a 6% annual increase). When oil turned higher, that momentum stalled but didn't vanish: the Stoxx 600 ends the Europe session around **635.7**, ~**+0.6%** (preliminary, single-source figure).

In the US, S&P futures are essentially flat (ES ~7,864 vs 7,859.75 at Friday's New York close) and the index remains near records. The nuance is the Nasdaq: NQ falls to ~**30,960**, below Friday's New York close reference (~31,109). That was the signal we flagged in Asia: if the Nasdaq lost that level, Sunday's risk-on push would look more like a liquidity gap than real demand.

The market is leaning on one idea: that **earnings** can support valuations after weeks of high oil and global bond yields at multi-decade highs. Wall Street's biggest banks kick off on Tuesday (JPMorgan and Citigroup among them), with the group expected to post about **$19 billion** in quarterly equity-trading revenue (single-source estimate). AI stays front and center: ASML and TSMC report later in the week.

In crypto, bitcoin is holding much of its three-session rebound, and crypto-linked stocks are outperforming in premarket trading.

### Bonds and rates: December absorbs the oil move

With no US cash Treasury market for Columbus Day, the 10-year stays at its Friday New York close reference (~**5.24%**); bond futures edge lower. The transmission shows up in fed funds futures:

- **Fed Oct 28:** ~**80–83.5%** hold / ~**16.5–20%** hike, depending on the reading (a range, not a single figure). No alert (no move of 20 pp or more).
- **Fed December:** ~**87.6%** odds of at least one cumulative hike in the futures reading, **+4 pp** from overnight. That's the part of the curve absorbing the oil move.
- **ECB Oct 29:** ~**91.5%** hold.
- **BoE Nov 5:** ~**89.5%** hike.
- **BoC Oct 28:** ~15% hike in one reading vs ~25–32% in others from last week (conflicting figures and different metrics; low confidence).
- **RBA Nov 3:** ~**28%** hike in futures; another reading is lower (~20%).

Key read: Wednesday's September CPI **doesn't capture** today's oil move. The spike affects October and November inflation expectations, which is why December moves and the October 28 meeting doesn't.

### Scenarios for the New York session and the week

- **If Brent holds above ~$105.5** (and more so if it breaks today's highs), the oil → inflation expectations → Fed December → dollar chain stays active; the euro and the Nasdaq would be the most exposed.
- **If Brent drops back below Friday's New York settle ($104.72)**, the Asia session's “contained” scenario regains ground and the dollar's Europe-session move loses steam. With liquidity this thin, it's best not to extrapolate it.
- **Between ~$104.7 and ~$107**, the most likely outcome is a sideways holiday Monday waiting for the banks and CPI.
- **Wednesday's CPI:** the headline is already expected hot on energy (+0.6% m/m, the biggest rise in five months). **What can move October is core:** ≥0.4% m/m would push October hike odds toward ~30–35%; in line (0.2%) would keep them around ~17–22%; ≤0.1% would send them back toward ~10%.
