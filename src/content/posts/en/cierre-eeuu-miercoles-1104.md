> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 4, 2026, 15:45 CT:** The market had a full day to digest the Democratic sweep of Congress, and its answer was clear: more stocks, less oil and stronger bonds. The reading that won out in the New York session was not regulatory risk but a divided government that spends less and a Washington with less room to prolong the war with Iran. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the **Dow Jones** jumped **3.0%** (+1,569 points) to **53,356.12**. The **S&P 500** rose **1.6%** to **7,688.62**, its best day in about three months, and the **Nasdaq Composite** gained **1.4%** to **25,736.51**.

It is the mirror image of Tuesday. Yesterday chipmakers propped up an index in which most companies fell; today **almost everything rose except chips and energy**. The market went into election night hedged against uncertainty and, with the result now clear, it is unwinding those hedges.

In the Europe session, the story was a dollar falling on a political premium rather than on the Fed. **New York confirmed it**: the day's data did not soften the Fed, yet the dollar stayed below its Tuesday close.

## Analysis

### Stocks: broad relief, AI split in two

The rally was broad: sectors tied to domestic consumption, healthcare and financials led, and consumer discretionary names were among the strongest. Energy was the big exception, dragged down by the oil slump.

Within tech, the market clearly separated two groups. **Cash-rich megacaps** rose sharply: Microsoft approx. **7%**, Amazon approx. **6–7%**, and Apple also among the strongest. By contrast, **chipmakers and data center operators fell**: Democrats won in states that are key for data center construction, such as Texas and Georgia, where local opposition to that expansion is growing. The semiconductor index lost approx. **3%** to a six-month low (single-source figure) and Nvidia fell approx. **2%**.

The logic runs like this: if the AI infrastructure buildout slows, the giants need to spend less cash and compete better, while suppliers whose growth depends on that construction continuing at full speed become more exposed. Meanwhile, Meta placed **€9 billion** in its first euro-denominated bond sale, with record demand of approx. **€53 billion**: the appetite to finance the AI heavyweights remains intact.

### Data: solid hiring, slightly calmer services

**ADP** private payrolls added **86,000** jobs in October, above expectations (approx. 65,000–69,000 in surveys; ⚠️ estimates did not match), with base pay up **3.2%** year over year. The **ISM services index** fell to **54.5** from 54.9 (approx. 54.8 expected): new orders cooled (**55.9** from 59.8), services employment improved (**51.4** from 50.1) and delivery times lengthened on the supply problems tied to Hormuz. The prices-paid component eased to approx. **73** from 74, still very high (single-source figure).

This morning we saw the ISM as the only release able to move the Fed today. It landed in the middle, without the price-and-employment combination that would have reinforced a December hike or the weakness that would have called it into question. That is why **the probability of a December hike remains around approx. 79–82%**, measured in the morning.

### Bonds: divided government makes debt cheaper

Treasuries rallied across the curve. The 2-year yield fell to approx. **4.58%** and the 10-year ended in the approx. **4.88–4.93%** area (⚠️ sources do not agree on the exact closing level). The underlying idea is fiscal: a Congress controlled by the opposition limits new tax cuts and extra spending, easing pressure on debt issuance. The oil collapse added to that by lowering inflation expectations.

### Dollar: lower, but well off the morning low

The dollar index touched a low of approx. **101.2** in the morning, its weakest level in a month, and then recovered part of the ground: it ends in the approx. **101.5–101.6** area, still below Tuesday's close. The **euro** remained the currency best placed to benefit from dollar weakness after euro-area inflation hit 4%, though it gave back part of its Europe session gains (EUR/USD approx. **1.122–1.124**, from approx. 1.127 in the morning). **Sterling** holds at approx. **1.347** ahead of the Bank of England.

The **yen** barely moved (USD/JPY approx. **158.4–158.5**): it still fails to benefit from lower US yields, and the explanation circulating since the morning, structural Japanese capital outflows, gains weight. The **Swiss franc** did gain ground as a safe haven.

### Oil: the war premium deflates at once

January Brent settled at **$94.62** (−$8.98, approx. −8.7%) and WTI at **$88.12** (−$7.97, approx. −8.3%), their lowest closes since August 31. It is the third straight decline, and Brent is now approx. **$12** below the $107 it touched on Tuesday in the Asia session.

The market is betting that a Democratic Congress will limit Washington's military options and speed up a negotiated exit from the Iran conflict. Trump himself acknowledged the war weighed on the election defeat, and his vice president openly distanced himself from that decision. But the risk has not gone away: Iran insists US hostility does not depend on who governs, Qatar is stepping up mediation to avoid a new escalation, and attacks on ships in the Gulf are still there. What has gone is much of the **geopolitical premium**, not the physical supply risk.

### Gold: safe haven and weak dollar at once

Gold rose more than **3%** and ended in the approx. **$4,045–4,075** area depending on the contract. Lower yields and a weaker dollar pushed it more than physical demand did.

### Scenarios for the next few hours

- **If the Asia session extends the relief:** the Nikkei and regional stocks could follow Wall Street's rebound, with the question mark on Asian chipmakers, which share the sector's hit in New York.
- **If oil keeps falling without verified progress in mediation:** the market would be pricing peace too early; a confirmed escalation headline could quickly reverse part of the drop.
- **If the Bank of England hikes with a split vote or hints at a pause:** sterling could give back this week's gains; a unanimous vote with guidance for more hikes would support it.
- **If the dollar fails to recover even with the Fed still priced for December:** it would confirm that the political premium outweighs rates; Friday's jobs report is the next test.
