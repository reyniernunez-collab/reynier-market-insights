> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 1, 2026, 15:40 CT:** After a week of five central banks comes a week of data and politics. The two G10 decisions, the **RBA** on Monday night and the **BoE** on Thursday morning, are already priced as hikes, so what gets tested is the guidance: whether each bank says "one and pause" or leaves the door open to more. The dollar arrives in an awkward spot: the tech selloff over the cost of AI spending sought shelter in Treasuries, the yen and the franc, **not the dollar**, and Fed odds for December fell to their most uncertain level in weeks. Tuesday brings the US midterm elections, more a tail risk than a catalyst, and on Friday **payrolls** decide which way December leans. In between, euro-area inflation tests the ECB and oil keeps its Hormuz premium.

## RBA: the hike is done, the guidance is not

A hike to 4.85% is the base case for both markets and economists. What is **not** priced is a second one: December trades at only approx. 12–24%, and 27 of 29 economists see 4.85% as the year's peak. Bullock has already signalled that inflation will overshoot the August forecasts. And the RBNZ just reminded everyone that "priced" does not mean "done".

- **Hike with peak or pause guidance** (what economists expect): the AUD could weaken even with the hike.
- **Hike with a Statement that lifts the inflation forecast and leaves the door open:** December or February above approx. 40% and a firm AUD.
- **Hold:** the surprise of the week, with roughly 20 bp of immediate repricing.

**Components:** the guidance sentence ("further tightening may be needed" vs "policy is now sufficiently restrictive"), the trimmed-mean forecast for 2026–27 and the cash rate assumed in the projections. If the RBA hikes and leaves the door open while the RBNZ stays put, **AUD/NZD** concentrates the reaction.

## UK: a priced hike with downside asymmetry

The hike to 4.00% is at approx. 95–100%, but markets price roughly **108 bp** of hikes in total across the coming meetings, while a narrow majority of economists see nothing more after November and Pill opposes "a series of hikes". That gap leaves the asymmetry on the side of a weaker pound if the bank avoids committing.

- **Vote:** 7-2 is expected. **Three or more votes to hold** would be a dovish signal; 9-0 would be hawkish.
- **Guidance and Report:** with CPI at 3.5% and services at 3.7% in September, a higher inflation projection and a "further hikes likely needed" would take December (approx. 28–41% ⚠️) above 50%. A "wait and assess" would leave it below 20%.

The decision lands in the Europe session, before the New York open, and sterling comes in after being last week's strongest currency.

## Fed: hawkish talk, a market that doesn't buy it

Last week brought tough messages: two votes for a hike, Warsh describing a "strong" economy and no discomfort with tighter financial conditions, and several regional presidents talking about more hikes. Even so, December odds **fell**, because the tech selloff and the Treasury rally weighed more than the rhetoric. An in-line ECI (0.8% q/q) added no wage pressure.

- **Hawkish:** payrolls of 100K or more with wages at 0.3% m/m or more and unemployment at 4.2% or lower. December back to 80% or more, the 2-year above 4.65%, and the dollar would get the bounce the risk-off did not give it.
- **Dovish:** payrolls of 30K or less, net negative revisions of 30K or more, or unemployment at 4.3% or higher. December below approx. 55%, the 2-year below 4.50% and a weaker dollar against the yen, franc and euro.
- **In between** (50–80K, 4.2% unemployment, 0.3% wages): that is what is priced.
- **Components over headline:** private payrolls, revisions (September was 29K), participation (61.8%) and wages. Wednesday's **ISM services** is the second test: prices paid of 76 or more with employment above 51 supports the hawks; employment below 49 would foreshadow a weak Friday. In Monday's **ISM manufacturing**, prices paid of 78 or more point the same way; below 75 with stable new orders would be disinflation without a slump, which would pull long-end yields lower.
- **Michigan:** 5-year inflation expectations of 3.6% or more would be the de-anchoring signal that would worry the Fed most.

## Midterms and the Treasury: the long end in play

A Democratic sweep is what is priced (approx. 70%). That outcome is associated with doubts over corporate taxes and regulation: somewhat negative for stocks and supportive for bonds, with little immediate FX impact. A split Congress would be "nothing changes". The surprise would be a Republican sweep (approx. 5–6%), with stocks, yields and the dollar higher. Results arrive in Wednesday's Asia session.

That same Wednesday, the Treasury refunding announcement is the risk for the long end: any hint of **larger** issuance would pressure the 10-year from 4.933% and, with tech fragile, stocks too. Bigger buybacks or less long-dated issuance would ease the curve.

## ECB and the euro area: the core decides December

Lagarde acknowledged a hike was discussed in October, and the dominant reading is "December and pause". German CPI already cooled the picture (2.8%; core 2.1%), while Italy and France ran hot. Energy explains the headline; the ECB watches **core and services**.

- **Hawkish:** core at 2.6% or more and services at 3.3% or more. December to 85–90% or higher.
- **Dovish:** core at 2.4% or less. December toward approx. 70%, and the euro loses support with the French spread over Bunds at approx. 165 bp.
- A 4.0% headline with core at 2.5% is what is expected and would give no direction.

## Canada, New Zealand and Japan

- **Canada:** the BoC hiked "cautiously", but flat August GDP (and a −0.1% flash for September) supports a pause. Macklem speaks Tuesday and Thursday, the **federal Budget** lands Thursday (big stimulus would argue for a second hike; an austere one pushes it away) and **jobs** come Friday: a third negative month with unemployment at 6.7% or more would take December below approx. 25%; +40K with positive full-time jobs, above 50%. High oil and a possible deal with the US support the CAD.
- **New Zealand:** the MPC is split over December (approx. 75%). Unemployment at 5.4% or lower with labour costs at 0.6% or more would lift it above 85%; unemployment at 5.7% or higher, below 60%.
- **Japan:** the BoJ declared underlying inflation around 2% and December sits at approx. 80–87% excluding the lowest measure ⚠️. September wages on Thursday (3.8% expected) and Ueda on Friday. Tuesday is a holiday.

## Earnings: does the AI selloff spread?

After Alphabet, Amazon and Intel were punished for cash flow consumed by AI, **Apple and Palantir** (Monday), **AMD** (Tuesday) and **Qualcomm and Arm** (Wednesday) will show whether the selloff spreads to chipmakers. Solid results would break that dynamic. There are no big US banks reporting this week.
