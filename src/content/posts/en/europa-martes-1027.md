> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 27, 2026, 08:19 CT:** Tuesday's Europe session delivered a number that changes the euro's tone: French households are in their worst mood in more than a decade. Meanwhile, the dollar kept rising without bonds pushing it. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the theme was a dollar rising without help from bonds and a yen ignoring Tokyo's warnings. The Europe session kept both and added a third: **the euro is now falling on its own problems**. French consumer confidence slumped and the French bond spread widened again.

My read: **the market heads into central bank week long dollars, with the euro as the month's worst-hit currency**. With an ECB hike in December nearly priced and the euro falling anyway, what weighs is the economic cost of energy, not rates.

## Analysis

### France: the missing data point

**French consumer confidence for October fell to 80**, versus approx. 85 expected and a prior month also revised to 85. It's the lowest since June 2013. The detail matters most: households' financial outlook worsens, fear of unemployment rises and **the share of households seeing sharp price increases jumps**, for a second month in a row. It's the worst mix for the ECB: higher perceived inflation and weaker spending.

**The French 10-year spread over Germany widened back to approx. 161 bp**, from about 153 bp at Monday's New York session close (⚠️ approximate calculation from 10-year yields; it got to around 163 bp). The French 10-year rises to approx. 4.95% and the German one eases to approx. 3.34%: money is moving from Paris to Frankfurt.

### Stocks and companies

European stocks edge higher (Germany and the euro area index approx. +0.2–0.3%), but London drops approx. 0.7% as a major British bank fell after revenue missed expectations. Wall Street futures stall after Monday's rally: enthusiasm over Anthropic's IPO filing cools and the focus shifts to earnings (Microsoft and Alphabet Wednesday after the close, Amazon Thursday) and the Fed.

### The dollar rises without bonds

- **EUR/USD:** ends the Europe session at approx. **1.1145** (about −26 pips vs Monday's New York session close), with a low around 1.1134. It's the fifth straight daily decline.
- **Dollar:** the index marks approx. **102.3**, a weekly high, while the **US 2-year eases to approx. 4.69–4.70%** (−2/−3 bp). December Fed pricing hasn't changed (approx. 75–80%), so the move is positioning, not expectations.
- **Sterling:** falls less than the euro (GBP/USD approx. 1.332) and EUR/GBP slips to approx. 0.8366. The weakness is the euro's, not Europe's as a bloc. The UK Budget arrives tomorrow.
- **Yen:** still among the weakest; USD/JPY hovers around approx. **157.7**. The market treats the Japanese government's verbal warnings as routine until there are more concrete signals.
- **Antipodeans:** the Australian and New Zealand dollars are the firmest of the Europe session (AUD/USD approx. 0.703; NZD/USD approx. 0.570), with positioning ahead of tonight's events.

### Oil: an attack the market priced out

Houthi rebels said they attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery. Saudi Arabia reported a limited fire from debris of intercepted drones, already under control and **with no impact on supply**. **December Brent** rose to near **$117** and then gave the gain back: at the Europe session close it was around approx. $114.7–114.9. The read: today the bar for oil to move currencies is high. Regional diplomacy remains open, with several countries trying to revive talks with Iran, and Israel's elections add to tensions. Gold is defending $4,000 (approx. $4,024).

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Continuation:** if consumer confidence comes in line and the US 2-year holds around approx. 4.68–4.72%, the euro would keep paying for France and energy, and the dollar would reach the Fed firm.
2. **Strong data:** consumer confidence with rising inflation expectations and the 2-year above approx. 4.72% would give the dollar rate support for the first time this week and reinforce a December Fed hike.
3. **Weak data:** with the 2-year below approx. 4.66%, the market could unwind part of its dollar positioning before the Fed, and the euro would recover some ground after five days of losses.
4. **France and the Gulf:** a French spread above approx. 170 bp would reopen the debate on a December ECB hike; real damage to Gulf infrastructure would send crude back to highs, with the euro and the yen among the most exposed.
