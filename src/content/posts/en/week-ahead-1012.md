## How we got here: what is priced

At the New York close on Friday October 9, Canada resolved its question in a dovish direction: employment **−68.3k** (consensus +7k to +9.2k ⚠️), unemployment at **6.5%** only because participation fell to **64.8%**, and USD/CAD touched 18-month highs (~1.4293–1.4302). Bank of Canada October hike odds moved from ~32% to a range of **~15–25%** depending on the source ⚠️. Michigan came in line on inflation expectations (1-year **4.7%**, 5-year **3.5%**), so the Fed barely moved.

Per my data, Fed October sits around **~17–20%** hike odds ⚠️ (readings vary slightly across sources) and December still carries **~80–85%** odds of at least one hike ⚠️. ECB October remains a low-probability move (~8–11% ⚠️); Bank of England November stays high (~86–89%); RBA November is around ~28–31%. The US 10-year held near **5.24%** at the New York close. In Europe, the OAT–Bund closed at **132.5 bp**, tactical relief from the early-October peak near ~147 bp, but no fiscal resolution.

Brent settled at **$104.72** and WTI at **$91.85**. The DXY closed at **102.23** and the dollar posted its longest weekly winning streak since early 2025. Treasury cash trading is closed Monday for Columbus Day; Canada is on Thanksgiving.

## US CPI: composition over headline

This is the release with the most capacity to move Fed expectations this week. The base risk is a hot headline driven by energy (Brent >$100) with core near 0.2%: a headline reaction that fades if services and shelter cooperate.

- **Restrictive scenario (Fed October → ≥30–35%; 10-year >5.30%):** core **≥0.4% m/m** or ≥0.3% with hot services/shelter. Headline ≥0.7% alone is not enough.
- **In-line scenario (core 0.2%, headline 0.5–0.6% on energy):** October stays ~17–22%; December ~80–85%. Focus shifts to Thursday.
- **Dovish scenario (core ≤0.1%; headline driven only by energy):** October toward ~10%; December eases toward ~75%.

The threshold to change the October 28 meeting is **core m/m**, not the headline. A 0.4%+ print with services is the shortest path to reopen October. Michigan (1-year 4.7%, 5-year 3.5%) did not move it on Friday.

## Canada and Macklem after −68.3k

Friday’s jobs print already did the work: BoC October moves to improbable but not ruled out (~15–25% ⚠️). What remains is communication. Macklem speaks ~1:05 AM CT Wednesday and ~11:15 PM CT Thursday.

- **Base case:** he talks labor slack and consolidates the October pause under ~15%.
- **What would change the script:** he leans on permanent wages (2.3% y/y) or inflation → that supports December. A strong Canadian activity print this week alone is not enough to reopen October. Monday, with CAD on holiday, liquidity is thin: do not force breakouts in USD/CAD.

## UK GDP and BoE November

Thursday at 1:00 AM CT brings August GDP: consensus **−0.1% m/m** (prior +0.4%). Services and manufacturing matter more than the headline. Per my data, BoE November still prices ~86–89% odds of a hike. Bailey speaks Wednesday and Thursday.

- **Base case:** near −0.1%; November holds.
- **What would change the script:** m/m ≤−0.3% with weak services can cut November toward ~75–80%. A 0.0%/+0.1% print keeps it near 86–90%.

## RBA minutes and Australia jobs

Monday night, RBA minutes are what can move AUD most: are they still open to November (~28–31% per my data)? Wednesday night, Australia employment (consensus ~+16k to +20k ⚠️, UR 4.6%) is the second test.

- **Restrictive:** minutes that do not close the door on November + jobs clearly above +20k with UR lower → November toward ≥40%.
- **Dovish:** minutes that stress slack or weak jobs → November toward ~20%.
