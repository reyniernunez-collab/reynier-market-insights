> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 2, 2026, 01:56 CT:** Monday's Asia session opened the week with some relief. Oil was the driver: the sense that Washington wants to give diplomacy with Iran more time before deciding anything after Tuesday's midterm elections pushed crude lower, and that, together with a technical bounce in tech, steadied risk appetite.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves a calmer market than Friday's, but not a fully convinced one. **Crude is down approx. 3%**, **Wall Street futures recover part of their losses** and the **dollar is softer across the board**, with the **Swiss franc and the euro** as the strongest currencies. The Australian and Canadian dollars and the yen, by contrast, lag.

My read: the relief depends almost entirely on the Gulf. As long as the diplomatic track stays open, crude faces downward pressure and the dollar loses appeal; but attacks on ships have not stopped and Iran's conditions remain demanding, so a single headline can turn the session around quickly. That is why the European open matters more for oil than for the final PMIs.

## Analysis

### Oil: diplomacy outweighs the attacks

**January Brent** is down approx. 3% from Friday's settlement at around **$110**, after an overnight low of approx. $109.4; quotes range from approx. $109.75 to $110.20 depending on the time. **December WTI** is at approx. **$100.6**, with a low of approx. $100.15, very close to $100.

Two factors explain the drop: a third day of Qatar-mediated talks on Iran's plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and the sense that Trump is in no hurry and wants to give diplomacy room before considering renewed strikes after Tuesday's elections. OPEC+ also kept its quotas unchanged for December.

The risk has not gone away. Overnight **a tanker was hit by two projectiles** off the Gulf coast, and late in the session a headline, not yet confirmed elsewhere, said Iran would not reopen Hormuz before the US lifts its sanctions. If confirmed, crude could win back part of its losses in Europe.

### Stocks: a bounce after Friday's blow

On Friday the **Nasdaq 100 fell approx. 3.5%**, its worst session since June, with chipmakers hammered. This Monday **Nasdaq 100 futures are up approx. 0.8%** and **S&P 500 futures between approx. 0.4% and 0.5%**. European futures are also higher.

- **Korea** led the region: the Kospi closed up approx. 2.2%, after gaining close to 3% intraday.
- **China** rose approx. 1%, helped by news that financial regulators meet banks and brokerages today to discuss measures to support the market. The private manufacturing PMI came in at 52.0, no surprise.
- **Japan** slipped slightly (the Nikkei between approx. −0.1% and −0.3%) and **Australia** fell approx. 0.6%.

### Dollar: the euro and the franc take the lead

- **EUR/USD**: up to approx. **1.119**, 32 to 36 pips above Friday's New York session close, depending on the reference. It did so despite very weak German retail sales (single-source figure; see below), a sign of euro demand that is ignoring the data for now.
- **USD/CHF**: down to approx. **0.822**, about 28–31 pips; the franc is the strongest currency of the night.
- **GBP/USD**: up to approx. **1.341**, about 23–27 pips, although sterling loses some ground against the euro.
- **USD/JPY**: up to approx. **157.7** (high approx. 157.99). Japan's finance minister repeated she will watch for disorderly moves, but the message was mild and the yen stayed weak, especially against the euro and sterling. Japan is on holiday tomorrow, so Asia liquidity will be thinner.
- **USD/CAD**: approx. **1.398**, a bit higher: weaker crude weighs on the Canadian dollar.
- **AUD/USD**: down to approx. **0.702** (13 to 16 pips lower) despite firmer stocks, hours before its central bank decides.
- **Dollar index**: approx. **101.74**, about two tenths lower.

The **2-year Treasury** yield barely moves (approx. 4.59%) and the **10-year** is down one basis point at approx. 4.92%.

### Gold and crypto

**Gold** rebounds between approx. 0.4% and 0.8% and trades in the approx. $3,900–3,915 area, depending on spot or futures, after Friday's lowest close in more than a year. **Bitcoin** rises for a third day after last week's drop on a hack at a major crypto exchange.

### Overnight data

- **Germany**: real retail sales reportedly fell approx. 2.0% month on month, against an expected rise of approx. 0.4%. It is a single-source figure, still to be confirmed, and it did not stop the euro.
- **Switzerland**: retail sales reportedly grew approx. 1.6% year on year, against 3.0% expected (also single-source). It did not stop the franc either.
- **China**: private manufacturing PMI at **52.0**, no surprise after the weak official figure over the weekend.

### Central banks: what the market is pricing

I give ranges because the measures differ. The drop in crude **has not moved** central bank expectations:

- **Reserve Bank of Australia (decides tonight):** a hike to 4.85% at approx. **80–91%**. With it so well priced, the Aussie's room will depend more on the message than on the decision.
- **Bank of England (Thursday, Nov 5):** a hike at approx. **97–99%**. For December, measures range from approx. 28% to 41%, too dispersed to read much into.
- **ECB (Dec 17):** a hike at approx. **80–84%**.
- **Fed (Dec 9):** a hike at approx. **71–74%**.
- **Bank of Japan (December):** a hike at approx. **80–87%**.
- **Bank of Canada (Dec 9):** between approx. 37% and 48%, again widely dispersed.

As a volatility reference, the 30-day expected move implied by options is approx. 1.75% in EUR/USD and approx. 2.1% in USD/JPY; in crude it is above 14%.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If the diplomatic track with Iran holds** and no serious new incidents arrive, crude would stay under pressure near $110 Brent and $100 WTI, which tends to favor the euro and weigh on the Canadian dollar.
- **If it is confirmed that Iran ties reopening Hormuz to sanctions relief**, or new attacks occur, crude could recover quickly and the dollar could win back some of its losses.
- **If the US ISM manufacturing index (9:00 AM CT) is firm**, especially on prices, it would reinforce bets on a December Fed hike and could slow the dollar's slide.
- **If Australia's central bank hikes and leaves the door open to more**, the impact could be limited because it is so well priced; a pause would be a big surprise for the Aussie.
