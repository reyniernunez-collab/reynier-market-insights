> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 29, 2026, 3:48 PM CT:** The day brought three tests, and the one that mattered most was not a macro number. PCE came in line and the ECB held no surprises, but Alphabet's historic drop reopened the big question of the year: whether the money the tech giants are pouring into AI will pay off. The result was a split New York session: the Nasdaq down and, at the same time, most stocks up, with money rotating into the rest of the market and into bonds. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 fell **0.30%** to **7,720.39**, its first loss in four sessions; the Nasdaq Composite dropped **1.38%** to **26,184.72** and the Dow Jones **0.25%** to **52,342.56**. Behind those numbers is a healthier market than it looks: the vast majority of S&P 500 stocks rose and the small-cap Russell 2000 gained **1.0%**. The VIX edged up to the **17.2** area.

September PCE confirmed what the market already expected: inflation high because of energy, but no surprises in the core measure. Q3 GDP showed the economy growing at its fastest pace in a year. In Europe, the ECB held rates with a tone that leaves December wide open. And after the close, Amazon and Intel added pressure on the tech sector.

## Analysis

### AI is no longer bought blindly

Alphabet fell approx. **12.6%**, its biggest drop on record, after sales came in below expectations and its AI spending burned much more cash than expected. The Nasdaq 100 lost **1.4%** and the semiconductor index **1.4%**. The message continues what we saw on Wednesday: the market rewards companies that turn AI investment into cash and punishes those still burning it.

What matters is that the selling did not spread. Healthcare and small caps rose, and Microsoft (practically flat on the day) placed a debt offering of approx. **$25 billion** with strong demand. This is a rotation, not a flight from risk: money is leaving the most expensive AI-linked names and moving into the rest of the market and into bonds.

After the New York close, the pressure returned: Amazon was down approx. **5%** after hours as spending to meet AI demand keeps rising, and Intel was sliding approx. **16%** after a revenue forecast below expectations (⚠️ after-hours prices, preliminary). The Asia session will open with that weight on tech indexes.

### US data: inflation in line, strong growth

The PCE price index rose **0.5%** in September, its biggest monthly increase since March, and the annual rate climbed to **3.6%**, with gasoline as the main driver. Core rose **0.3%** m/m and **3.1%** y/y, exactly as forecast. Q3 GDP grew at a **3.4%** annualized pace, above expectations and the **2.2%** of Q2, driven by AI-linked investment and consumer spending. September household spending (**0.5%**) and income (**0.2%**) came in slightly below forecasts.

**What's priced:** the data did not change the Fed scenario, but it did not reinforce it either. The probability of a December hike slipped to approx. **77%** in prediction markets, from the **79–82%** area in the morning. An in-line print, with tech falling and oil slightly lower, was enough for the market to ease the bet a little.

### Bonds: safe haven and relief

Treasuries rallied strongly. The 10-year yield fell approx. **10 bp** to the **4.94%** area, back below **5%**, and the 2-year dropped to the **4.60%** area (⚠️ single-source closing level). The 7-year note auction cleared at **4.894%**, well below the previous **5.085%**, with solid demand. Three forces pushed in the same direction: the tech selloff, a PCE without surprises and slightly cheaper crude.

### ECB: a pause with the door open

The ECB held its deposit rate at **2.50%**, as expected. The key nuance is that the Governing Council debated a hike, although it unanimously decided to wait. Lagarde insisted they decide meeting by meeting, but made it clear that, barring major changes over these six weeks, the direction is known. The bank sees upside risks to inflation from energy and downside risks to growth, and does not yet see second-round effects in wages.

The market read it as a pause with a hiking bias. The probability of a December hike sits between approx. **67%** and **80%** depending on the measure (⚠️ sources disagree). On the balance sheet, Lagarde announced no change to the pace of bond run-off.

### Dollar: the euro ends the day as a winner

The dollar index fell approx. **0.3%** to **101.9**. EUR/USD rose to approx. **1.118**, far from the **1.1124** low it hit during the Europe session, before Lagarde's departure was announced. GBP/USD gained approx. **0.4%** to **1.338** and the Swiss franc was among the strongest (USD/CHF approx. **0.824**). USD/JPY slipped to the **157.7** area ahead of the Bank of Japan. The Australian dollar was the weakest (approx. **−0.4%**, to **0.703**), hit by the tech selloff, and USD/CAD ended flat around **1.399**. Gold slipped approx. **0.3%** to the **$4,040** area.

### Oil and the Gulf: down on the day, heating up again in the afternoon

December Brent settled at approx. **$114.9**, about **$0.4** lower, and December WTI at **$102.0**, about **$0.8** lower (⚠️ single-source settlement figures), on a day dominated by indirect US–Iran contacts. Iran has still not replied to Washington's letter and insists on its conditions. After settlement, two signals pointed the other way: a report of a tanker hit by a projectile while leaving the Strait of Hormuz, and a Houthi statement claiming missile attacks on Saudi oil facilities, including Ras Tanura and Abqaiq (⚠️ no Saudi confirmation). WTI was recovering some ground after those headlines.

⚠️ Contract reminder: December Brent expires this week and January trades well below it, so price references change a lot depending on which contract you look at.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If Amazon and Intel drag Asian tech in the Asia session:** today's rotation could turn into broader selling; if the rest of the market holds again, it would remain an adjustment focused on the most AI-linked names.
- **If the Bank of Japan holds and Ueda validates December:** the yen could gain ground with the dollar already weaker; if he sounds cautious, USD/JPY would look at **158** again.
- **If the attacks on Saudi facilities are confirmed with damage:** crude would regain the premium it lost and the curve could pressure bonds again; if they remain claims with no effect on output, the focus would return to diplomacy.
- **If tomorrow's labor costs come in strong:** the market would regain confidence in the December hike that cooled slightly today; a soft print would extend the bond rally.
