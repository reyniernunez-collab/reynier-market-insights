> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 29, 2026, 02:02 CT:** Thursday's Asia session was about earnings, not central banks. Results from Alphabet and Samsung revived doubts that the huge spending on artificial intelligence will pay off soon, and that mattered more than any regional data. Meanwhile, the dollar won back part of the ground it lost after the Fed.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves a more cautious equity market and a slightly firmer dollar. **Alphabet sank approx. 9–10% after hours**, and Nasdaq 100 futures point to the end of a three-day winning streak. The drop was not across the board: Microsoft rose on cloud strength, and in Asia the hit was concentrated in tech, especially in Korea.

My read: after a Fed meeting that barely changed December pricing, the focus is back on earnings and data. Today brings **US third-quarter GDP and core PCE**, the **ECB** and, tonight in CT, the **Bank of Japan**. It is the day with the most potential to move the dollar since Tuesday.

## Analysis

### Tech: the market wants proof

Alphabet reported sales below forecasts and fell sharply after the New York close, reviving a familiar question: whether the hundreds of billions committed to AI will turn into profits fast enough to justify valuations. **Samsung Electronics** fell in Seoul after disappointing net income and no new shareholder-return plan.

There were offsets: **Microsoft** rose after hours on cloud sales, and **Oracle** had jumped during the day after issuing a long-term free-cash-flow outlook. The message is selective: the market is separating companies that show returns from those that only show spending. **Amazon** reports today after the close and **Apple** next week; those are the next tests.

### Stocks: Korea suffers, Australia holds up

**Korea** was the hardest-hit market, down between approx. 1.4% and 2.8% depending on when it was measured, dragged by chipmakers. **Australia** rose approx. 0.5%. In **Japan** the readings disagree (from flat to a moderate gain), so I'm not giving a figure. **S&P 500** futures are barely lower, and European stocks look set for a slightly positive open.

### Dollar: a partial rebound without a clear trigger

After the Fed, the dollar lost the support of short-term Treasury yields. During Asia it recovered part of that with no data behind it, which is common ahead of a busy day:

- **Dollar index** approx. **102.2**, up from approx. 102.1 at the New York session close.
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.114**, about 15 pips below the New York close, hours before the ECB.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.331**, also a bit lower.
- **USD/JPY** steady at approx. **157.8**, below the high it set on the Fed (approx. 158.3), ahead of the Bank of Japan.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.704** and **USD/CAD** approx. **1.399**, with little movement.

Treasury yields dipped slightly: the **10-year** at approx. **5.01%** and the **2-year** at approx. **4.69%**.

### Regional data: no market-moving surprises

**Australian import prices** fell **1.1% on the quarter**, versus an expected 1.3% rise, a low-impact figure that did not change bets on an Australian rate hike on November 3. **Japanese consumer confidence** came in at 35.0, essentially in line with forecasts.

### Oil and gold: a bit of diplomacy

Crude is down approx. **$1**: December **Brent** around **$114** and **WTI** at approx. **$101.6**. Signs that **Washington is trying to restart talks with Iran** are weighing, though there is no concrete progress yet. Watch the expiry of the December Brent contract on Friday, which can distort quotes. **Gold** is down approx. **1%** to the **$4,040** area, held back by the dollar's rebound.

### Central banks: what the market is pricing

I'm giving ranges because the measures differ:

- **ECB (today, 8:15 AM CT):** a hold is all but certain; a December hike at approx. **66–79%**, a wide range because sources disagree.
- **Bank of Japan (tonight, CT):** a hold is all but certain; December at approx. **80–86%**.
- **Fed (December 9):** a hike at approx. **80–87%**, nearly unchanged from before yesterday's meeting.
- **Reserve Bank of Australia (November 3):** a hike at approx. **80–87%**.
- **Bank of England (November 5):** a hike at approx. **97–98%**.
- **Bank of Canada (December):** readings range from approx. 20% to 52% after yesterday's hike, too wide a spread to draw conclusions.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If core PCE comes in at 0.4% month on month or higher**, the market would firm up a December Fed hike and the dollar would have grounds to recover what it lost after yesterday's press conference. A reading of 0.2% or lower would do the opposite.
- **If the ECB holds and leaves the door open to December**, the euro could find support; if it cools that expectation, the euro would be exposed, especially if PCE is strong.
- **If Alphabet's drop spreads to the rest of tech** at the New York open, risk-sensitive currencies such as the Australian dollar would be the most exposed.
- **If the Bank of Japan sounds more hawkish** with December in mind, the yen could react sharply tonight; a decision with no new message would keep the focus on Tokyo and its currency warnings.
