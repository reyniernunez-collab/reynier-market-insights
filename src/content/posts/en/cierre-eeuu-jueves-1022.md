> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 22, 2026, 3:48 PM CT:** On Wednesday, the New York session celebrated tech earnings. On Thursday it reread them through a different lens: what matters is not how much they sell, but how much they have to spend to keep growing. Add an increasingly hot Gulf, and money ran to Treasuries, not to the dollar or gold.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the Nasdaq Composite fell **2.6%** to **26,008.86** and the S&P 500 **1.2%** to **7,663.43**, its worst session since July. The Dow ended practically flat (+0.02%) at **52,867.98**, far from the beating in tech. The semiconductor index lost approx. **6.7%**, wiping out far more in one day than it gained on Wednesday.

Two forces pushed the same way: doubts about the return on artificial intelligence spending came back hard, and the escalation in Saudi Arabia took Brent to five-month highs. The market's response was textbook: out of tech, into Treasuries.

## Analysis

### Stocks: the problem isn't sales, it's the bill

Meta reported revenue above expectations and solid guidance, yet fell approx. **7%**. The market focused on something else: future spending commitments on AI infrastructure and computing capacity that keep growing, and a plan to raise capital through a new share class. The cost of insuring its debt hit record highs, a sign that the question is no longer only whether AI pays off, but how it gets financed.

That reading dragged the whole chip sector down: all 30 stocks in the semiconductor index closed lower, leaving the index at its lowest level since late July. Tesla also gave ground despite better-than-expected figures. On Wednesday the market rewarded AI demand; on Thursday it punished what it costs to sustain it. That swing is the clearest sign that tech is fragile and highly sensitive to each earnings report.

Next week Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report, three of the largest investors in data centers. Their spending will be under the microscope.

### Oil: event mode confirmed

Brent topped **$113** a barrel, its highest level since early May, and WTI ended near **$99.5**. The attacks in Saudi Arabia, with casualties, and reports of an evacuation at the Saudi state oil company's headquarters outweighed any export data. In the morning Brent had touched $112 without extending; in the New York session it did break higher. As long as the Gulf headlines continue, there's no reason for the risk premium to fade.

### Bonds and currencies: Treasuries were the haven

Treasuries had their best day in more than a year: the 10-year yield fell 15 to 19 bp to approx. **4.95–4.97%** (⚠️ readings differ by time), below 5% for the first time since September. That stands out because the jobs data was firm: jobless claims fell to **194,000** against 200,000 expected, and continuing claims rose to approx. **1.73 million**. The market ignored the data and prioritized protection.

The yen and the franc also acted as havens: USD/JPY ended in the approx. **157–158** area (⚠️ readings from different times). The euro stayed under pressure from the French spread, near **1.12** against the dollar. Gold, by contrast, fell again, to approx. **$4,050–4,080** (⚠️ sources differ): for now it isn't working as a haven, and our reading, which is an interpretation, is that it's being sold to cover losses elsewhere.

### Europe

At the Europe close, stocks also fell on the same mix of AI doubts and rising oil. The French spread, at its highest since 2011, remains the dominant factor for the euro ahead of Friday's rating review.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If the Asia session extends the chip selloff:** Seoul and Taiwan would be the most exposed, and the yen could keep gaining as a haven.
- **If Brent holds above $112–113:** event mode stays on, with pressure on the euro through the energy channel; a clear move back below approx. $109 would signal the rally is fading.
- **If Japan's inflation comes in firm:** the Bank of Japan's December hike gains support and the yen gets an extra boost on top of haven demand.
- **If the French spread tops today's highs before Friday's review:** the euro tends to extend its decline; stabilization would open room for a partial rebound.
