> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 3, 2026, 15:43 CT:** Midterm election day ended with a stock market that looks calm on the surface and divided underneath. Chipmakers rebounded and pulled the S&P 500 out of its losing streak, but most stocks fell. The data point of the day, job openings, came in much stronger than expected and pushed yields and the dollar higher just before the vote count begins. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 rose **0.36%** to **7,568.92**, the Nasdaq Composite **0.83%** to **25,372.01** and the Nasdaq 100 approx. **0.7%** to **28,180.26**, still in correction territory (approx. 10% below its recent high). The Dow Jones slipped **0.38%** to **51,786.72**, weighed down by Apple.

The shape of the day is the mirror image of Monday. Yesterday most companies rose while tech fell; today tech bounced and **most S&P 500 companies finished lower**. Six of the eleven sectors closed in the red, led by materials and consumer staples. It is a narrow rebound driven by chips, not a broad change in mood.

In the Europe session, the story was a dollar recovering on yields rather than on the Fed. **New York gave it a concrete reason**: a labor market that is tightening again.

## Analysis

### Chipmakers: a breather, not a turn

Chips halted the slide that had built up heavy losses over three sessions. The semiconductor index gained approx. **2.5%**; AMD rose approx. **3–4%** ahead of its results, Broadcom approx. **3%**, and Nvidia and Micron more than **2%**. Technology was the best S&P 500 sector, followed by industrials.

On the other side, **Apple lost approx. 2%** after a disappointing margin outlook and iPad sales below expectations. Palantir also slipped despite beating estimates. Meta, meanwhile, met solid demand for its first bond deal in six months: the market is still willing to fund AI spending, even if each new issue is a reminder that the bill is no longer paid with cash alone.

After the close, **AMD reported revenue and fourth-quarter guidance above expectations**, with its data center business nearly doubling, and the shares were up approx. **6–7%** after hours. It is the first read on AI demand after days of doubt, and the reaction will play out in the Asia session.

### JOLTS: the labor market tightens again

September job openings rose to **7.52 million**, about **333,000** more than in August (revised up to 7.19 million) and well above expectations (approx. 7.10–7.16 million depending on the survey). **Quits** jumped to **3.37 million**, the highest in well over a year, and layoffs fell to **1.64 million**. There are approx. 1.05–1.06 openings per unemployed person.

That is exactly the combination we flagged this morning as the most demanding for the Fed: **more openings and more quits**, meaning workers confident enough to switch jobs and potential wage pressure. Before the data, the probability of a December hike stood at approx. **72–75%**; the JOLTS points to reinforcing it, although the underlying decision still hinges on Friday's payrolls.

### Bonds: the 10-year breaks its streak

The 10-year yield rose approx. **6 basis points** to approx. **4.95%**, its biggest daily gain since late September, ending three sessions of declines. The 2-year finished around **4.62%**. What stands out is that oil fell again and bonds still did not lean on it: today jobs and caution ahead of the election outweighed energy relief.

### Dollar: firm ahead of the count

The dollar index finished at approx. **101.8–101.9**, a modest gain on a choppy day: it slipped in the morning and turned higher with the JOLTS.

The **yen was again the weakest**: USD/JPY climbed to approx. **158.7**, a multi-day high, with US yields rising. The **euro failed to reclaim 1.12**: EUR/USD traded in a tight range and ends at approx. **1.118–1.119**. The **franc** kept giving back what it gained after its inflation data (USD/CHF at approx. **0.821**). Sterling holds at approx. **1.341**.

The **Australian dollar** stayed on the defensive after the Australian central bank's "hike with a pause" (AUD/USD at approx. **0.696–0.698**), and the **Canadian dollar** weakened with oil (USD/CAD at approx. **1.40**), even though the Bank of Canada governor told Parliament the bank can no longer look through the energy shock and that further rate increases could be needed. The yuan also weakened after the Chinese central bank's measures.

### Oil: mediation continues, the premium fades

January Brent settled at **$103.60** (−$1.17, approx. −1.1%) and December WTI at **$96.09** (−$0.37), their lowest closes in nearly four weeks. WTI briefly dipped below $95 during the session. It is the second straight decline, after Monday's plunge of more than 7%, and it comes after Brent touched approx. **$107** in the Asia session on the strike on Qeshm Island.

Qatar says Washington and Iran remain committed to mediation and are reviewing proposals, while acknowledging there is no breakthrough to announce, and Tehran insists it will not accept diplomacy "under coercion." The read is unchanged: what is deflating is the **geopolitical premium**, not physical scarcity, and the attacks on ships in the Gulf have not gone away.

### Gold: slips as yields rise

Gold finished approx. **0.7%** lower, around **$3,930–3,940** depending on the contract. With yields and the dollar rising, it found no safe-haven demand despite the election uncertainty.

### Scenarios for the next few hours

- **If the result confirms what prediction markets price** (a Democratic House with high probability and a more contested Senate; ⚠️ Senate estimates disagree): the initial impact could be limited, since much of it is already priced. The question is whether Democratic control of both chambers weighs on tech and AI.
- **If Republicans keep the Senate or both chambers:** that would be the surprise for markets; it could give stocks a short-term lift and push long yields and the dollar higher on expectations of more fiscal spending.
- **If the count drags on or results are contested:** the franc, the yen and gold would be the natural candidates for safe-haven demand.
- **If the AMD reaction holds in the Asia session:** the chip rebound would gain credibility; if it fades, today's move would look like a breather within the correction.
