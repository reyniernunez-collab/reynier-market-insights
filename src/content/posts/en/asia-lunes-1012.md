> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 12, 2026, 02:05 CT:** The Asia session opens the week with oil unwinding the jump triggered by Houthi attacks on Riyadh's main airport. US and European futures are higher, Asian stocks slip on a sharp drop in Korea, gold stays firm, and the market is already looking ahead to Wednesday's CPI.

## Executive Summary

The weekend brought serious geopolitical risk — Houthi strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid airport and a tanker hit off Oman — and Sunday's reopen threatened a spike in crude. It didn't stick: after an early high near **$105.6**, Brent turned around and during the Asia session trades around Friday's New York settle (**$104.72**) or below, depending on the reference. The market is weighing steady physical flows and the limited Russia–Ukraine energy ceasefire announced by Washington (still not fully confirmed) more heavily than an open escalation.

With crude contained, US futures start the week higher (S&P ~~+0.3%) and European contracts a bit more (~~+0.6%). Asia didn't follow: the regional index is down ~0.2%, dragged by a ~3% slide in Korea on its return from a long weekend. Gold posts a third straight gain near $4,200. It's a thin Monday: US cash Treasuries are closed for Columbus Day, Canada is out for Thanksgiving and Japan is on holiday.

## Analysis

### Oil: the fear never turned into price

The key point overnight is what **didn't** happen. The escalation scenario we laid out in the Week Ahead — Brent holding above ~$105–107 — showed up in the first minutes (~$105.6 Brent, ~$92.6 WTI) and unwound in under two hours. WTI trades near **$91.5**, below Friday's New York settle of **$91.85**.

For Brent the readings **don't match**: one puts it ~~1.2% lower near **$103.4**, another around **$104.7** near 1:30 AM CT. The sensible read is a range (~~$103.4–104.7): either way, no extra war premium over Friday.

The risk hasn't gone away. Sunday's Riyadh toll of **1 dead and 11 injured** is single-source (a projectile hitting the passenger terminal is confirmed), and the tanker incident near Sohar, Oman, reclassified as a drone attack, also comes from a single source. Trump left open the option of joining Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, and the Russia–Ukraine energy truce still lacks full verification. In short: price is discounting containment today, but any confirmed damage to infrastructure or shipping could reopen the oil → yields → dollar chain.

### Equities: green in the West, Korea weighs on Asia

S&P futures are up ~**0.3%** (ES ~**7,878** vs 7,859.75 at Friday's New York close), and Dow futures hold their gap up. The nuance is the Nasdaq: it opened Sunday near ~31,257 and in Asia slipped back to ~**31,171**, giving back about 85 points, though still above Friday's New York close (~31,109). That level is the gauge: lose it, and Sunday's push looks more like a liquidity gap than real demand.

In Asia, the regional index is down ~**0.2%**. Korea, back from a long weekend, drops ~**3%** and accounts for most of the drag. Japan was closed.

### China and Europe: a 16-point deal with fine print

China–EU trade talks ended with a **16-point** consensus covering trade, investment, export controls and intellectual property. Brussels says Beijing agreed to substantially cut hybrid car exports, although, according to people familiar with the talks, the EU is also preparing safeguards to restrict imports. The tone is less hostile than in recent weeks, but the trade front isn't closed: European autos and the yuan are the channels to watch.

### Dollar, bonds and gold

With no cash Treasury market, the 10-year reference stays at Friday's New York close of **5.244%**; bond futures edge higher alongside Australian and New Zealand debt. The dollar barely softens: DXY ~**102.17** (102.23 on Friday).

Indicative levels around **1:30 AM CT**: EUR/USD ~**1.1210**, GBP/USD ~**1.3255**, USD/JPY ~**158.36** (yen soft on its holiday), AUD/USD ~**0.6986**, USD/CAD ~**1.4241** (thin market with Canada on Thanksgiving).

Gold is up for a **third session**: futures around ~**$4,208** and spot near $4,200 (futures and spot trade about $25 apart, so don't mix them). In emerging markets, the Indian rupee firms ~**1%** after the central bank rolled out its strongest support measures since the 2013 taper tantrum.

### Rates: CPI week, no alert

No central bank's next meeting has moved 20 pp or more, so **there's no rate alert**. What the market prices this morning:

- **Fed Oct 28:** ~**22%** hike odds in the futures reading (78% hold, from 80/20 on Sunday); another reading shows ~**16.5%**, so think of a ~17–22% range. December: ~**84%** odds of at least +25 bp cumulative.
- **ECB Oct 29:** ~**92–93%** hold.
- **BoE Nov 5:** ~**89–90%** hike.
- **BoC Oct 28:** ~**15%** hike in one reading vs ~32% in another (conflicting figures; low confidence).
- **RBA Nov 3:** ~**27–28%** hike in futures; another reading is lower (~16%).
- **RBNZ Oct 28:** ~**59%** odds of some hike.
- **BoJ Oct:** ~**95%** hold.

### Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent stays at or below ~$104.5–105** and ES keeps its gap up, the containment scenario gains ground into the Europe open and the dollar has little fuel.
- **If Brent reclaims and holds ~$105.5** (on confirmed damage in the Gulf or Riyadh), the oil → yields → dollar chain returns, and the euro and equity indexes suffer.
- **If the Nasdaq loses Friday's New York close** while Asia stays weak, Sunday's risk-on push is called into question.
- With US cash bonds closed and Canada on holiday, any move today in rates or CAD comes on thin liquidity and can be exaggerated.
