> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 7, 2026, 1:15 AM CT:** Wall Street closed Tuesday at record highs, but Asia didn't buy the story. With Brent back above $101 on tension in Hormuz, bonds are slipping again and the dollar is regaining ground. The market heads into the FOMC minutes with the same dilemma it has had all week: stocks at records living alongside expensive oil and multi-year-high yields.

## Executive Summary

Wall Street's momentum ran out in Asia. The Asia-Pacific index falls ~**0.7%**, dragged down by tech in South Korea and Hong Kong, and US futures give back part of their gains. Brent rises ~1% to ~**$101.60** after an escalation of Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, and that shows up in fixed income: the US 10-year gains ~3 bps to **5.31%**. The dollar firms against most of its peers, bitcoin slips ~1.7% to around **$84,100** and French debt again underperforms German debt. In India, the central bank raises rates for the first time in nearly four years.

## Analysis

### Stocks: Asia doesn't follow the record

The contrast is clear: the S&P 500 closed Tuesday at a record and Asia opens Wednesday in the red. The drop is concentrated in Korean and Hong Kong tech, exactly the segment that had run the most. Underneath there's a divergence worth tracking: Taiwan's Taiex beat Korea's Kospi by about 23 percentage points last quarter. Samsung's preliminary results land on Thursday, and the market expects its best quarterly profit in years. If it delivers, Korea has a case to close the gap; if it disappoints, the gap with Taiwan could widen. US futures paring gains suggest the market doesn't want to stretch the rally before the minutes.

### Oil and bonds: Hormuz is in charge again

Brent rises ~1% to ~**$101.60** and moves away from the $100 line again. The trigger is Iran's escalation against tankers in Hormuz. With crude at these levels the inflation picture worsens and bonds pay for it: the 10-year is back at **5.31%**, nearly undoing Tuesday's decline, when it had closed near 5.27%. In recent weeks crude and the 10-year have moved closely together (30-day correlation of ~0.92 in our Tuesday data), so every Hormuz headline passes quickly into the curve. For reference, Brent's 30-day expected move is around **13.8%**: it's the most nervous asset on the board.

### Dollar and the Fed: the minutes, with the market leaning hawkish

The dollar gains against nearly every major currency, supported by yields and crude. In our latest probability reading (early Tuesday), the market priced ~**76%** odds of a Fed pause in October and ~**24%** of another hike, and ~**83%** odds of a hike in December. The minutes of the September meeting (when the Fed hiked unanimously) will show whether that move was a one-off adjustment or the start of more tightening:

- **If they show broad support for more hikes**, the dollar and the front end of the curve would likely stay under upward pressure, and rate-sensitive stocks would suffer.
- **If they reveal a divided committee** or one worried about overtightening, yields have room to fall and the dollar to give back part of today's gains.

### Europe and emerging markets: France and India

French bond futures underperform Germany's again: the market still doesn't trust France's public finances, and that keeps the euro on the defensive. In India, the central bank raised rates for the first time in nearly four years and shifted its stance to calibrated tightening, leaving the door open to further hikes. It's one more central bank joining the push to tighten against energy-driven inflation.

## What I'm watching today (CT)

- **European session:** how the euro digests German industrial production (released at 1:00 AM) and how the spread between French and German debt reacts.
- **Hormuz:** any tanker headline moves Brent, the 10-year and the Canadian dollar; Brent holding above $100 keeps inflation pressure alive.
- **9:30 AM:** EIA crude inventories. **10:00 AM:** New York Fed consumer inflation expectations.
- **12:00 PM:** 10-year note auction. With the 10-year at 5.31%, demand will show whether there's appetite at these levels.
- **1:00 PM:** FOMC minutes, the event most likely to move pricing for October and December.
