> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 30, 2026, 3:48 PM CT:** The week ends with the question the market has been asking for days turning into open selling: whether Big Tech's AI spending has gotten out of hand. After Alphabet on Thursday, Amazon and Intel wrapped up on Friday a week of earnings that leaves the sector at its weakest point since June. This time, unlike Thursday, the selling did not stay confined to the AI names. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 fell **1.82%** to **7,580.07** and lost its 100-day average; the Nasdaq Composite dropped **2.86%** to **25,437.00**, the Nasdaq 100 **3.46%** and the Dow Jones **1.09%** to **51,770.31**. The small-cap Russell 2000 gave up **1.66%**, so this time there was no shelter in the rest of the market. The VIX rose to **19.4**, from the **17.2** area.

The day's macro release, labor costs, came in exactly in line and did not move the picture. What dominated was tech, a record theft at a major crypto exchange and oil rising again on new incidents in the Strait of Hormuz. Treasuries ended nearly flat on the day but close October with a clear gain on safe-haven demand.

## Analysis

### Tech: from rotation to open selling

Intel fell approx. **29%**, its worst session in more than four decades, after guidance that missed expectations. Amazon fell **1.4%** on plans to raise investment even further. The Nasdaq 100 briefly entered technical-correction territory (a 10% drop from its highs) and the semiconductor index slumped approx. **7%** (⚠️ single-source figure).

The key change from Thursday is that the contagion did reach the rest of the market. On Thursday, most stocks rose while Alphabet fell; on Friday, small caps and the Dow fell too. The core problem is that the businesses funding Big Tech's AI spending (advertising, consumer and retail) are starting to show signs of cooling just as that spending hits record levels. On top of that, the US securities regulator said it is reviewing how AI companies disclose their risks.

### Labor costs: in line, no surprise

The Q3 employment cost index rose **0.8%**, as expected and a bit below the prior **0.9%**; over twelve months, **3.4%**. Wages rose **0.7%** and benefits **0.9%**. It was the only release of the day able to shift Fed expectations, and it didn't.

The Fed's messages were hawkish. Musalem said the central bank will raise rates again soon and that inflation won't come down on its own. Logan went further, saying at least two more quarter-point hikes are needed, while calling a pause to take stock reasonable. Even so, the odds of a December hike settled around approx. **72%** in prediction markets, slightly below the morning's approx. **73–78%** range (⚠️ single-source reading after the close).

### Bonds: flat on the day, a haven for the month

The 10-year ended around **4.93%** and the 2-year around **4.59%**, barely a basis point below Thursday's New York close. Bonds rallied further during the Europe session (the 2-year touched **4.56%**) but gave some back on the Fed comments. For October as a whole, safe-haven demand leaves Treasuries with a solid gain.

### Dollar: wins back what it lost in Europe

The dollar index ended practically flat, around **101.9**, after falling as low as **101.57** in the Europe session. EUR/USD, which reached **1.1205** in the morning, slipped back to approx. **1.116**, below its Thursday New York close. USD/JPY returned to **157.6** from a low of **156.94** and USD/CHF to approx. **0.825**. Sterling held up best: GBP/USD ends around **1.339**, slightly above Thursday. USD/CAD sits at **1.397** and AUD/USD at **0.703**.

In Europe, the release that weighed most on the euro was German inflation: **2.8%** y/y in October, below the **3.0%** expected and September's **3.3%**, with core at **2.1%**. It partly offsets the upside surprises in France and Italy and takes some pressure off the ECB ahead of December. In Canada, August GDP was flat, below the **0.2%** expected, and the September flash points to a **0.1%** decline.

### Gold and bitcoin: the havens that didn't work

Gold fell approx. **3.6%**, to the **$3,886** area, on a day of fear in equities. It is a sign of forced selling: when losses pile up, investors sell what is in profit to cover. Bitcoin fell approx. **5%**, to the **$76,800** area, after one of the world's largest exchanges reported a record theft and suspended withdrawals; it expects to resume them in approx. **34 hours**.

### Oil and Hormuz: Brent expires higher

December Brent, which expired on Friday, settled at approx. **$117.6**, up about **$2.7**, and the January contract, now the benchmark, trades around **$112.3**. For December WTI, closing figures range from approx. **$103** to **$104** (⚠️ sources disagree). In the Strait of Hormuz, a tanker was reported hit by a projectile, and Iranian media speak of a fourth tanker that allegedly exploded after hitting a mine (⚠️ no independent confirmation). Trump said there are talks with Iran and that there is time, and a Qatari delegation arrived in Tehran. The Houthis denied attacking Medina.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If Asian tech opens the week with fresh selling:** the Nasdaq 100 correction could take hold and bonds and the yen would again be the havens; if Asia stabilizes, Friday would look more like the end of a bad earnings week than the start of something bigger.
- **If the Hormuz incidents continue over the weekend:** January Brent would regain ground and the Canadian dollar would stay supported; if the Iran talks make progress, the crude premium could ease.
- **If the exchange resumes withdrawals smoothly:** pressure on bitcoin could ease; a delay or new losses would revive distrust.
- **If the RBA hikes on Tuesday as expected:** the focus would shift to the tone; a cautious message would weigh on the Australian dollar despite the hike.
