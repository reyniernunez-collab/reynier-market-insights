> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 4, 2026, 01:58 CT:** Wednesday's Asia session was, in practice, US election night. As it became clear that Democrats were taking back both chambers of Congress, the dollar was sold across the board, Treasuries rallied and gold climbed back above $4,000. What didn't change is what the market expects from the Federal Reserve.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves the **dollar at a one-month low**, **Treasury yields lower**, **Wall Street futures in the red** and **gold back above $4,000**. Sterling is the night's strongest currency, followed by the Swiss franc, the yen and the euro.

My read: the dollar is paying the political bill, but the Fed hasn't changed the script. The slide rests on three ideas: less federal spending, the risk of tougher regulation for big tech and somewhat slower growth. None of them has cut the odds of a December Fed hike, which are steady or even slightly higher. That is why this dollar move could reverse if US data stay firm, and Friday's jobs report is still the number that matters most.

## Analysis

### Elections: divided government

Democrats secure **the Senate and the House of Representatives**, with wins in traditionally Republican states such as Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, both Carolinas and Maine. The Senate seat count is still in progress, so I'd rather not give a final number.

This opens a period of **divided government**: more legislative gridlock, more congressional investigations and less room for new tax cuts or spending increases. According to remarks reported by the US press (single source), President Trump said he accepts the result and attributed part of the defeat to the war with Iran.

### Dollar: broad selling and a partial rebound

The night came in three stages. With the first Democratic wins, the dollar was sold hard; the low came when Democratic control of the Senate was projected, and in the final hours it recovered part of the move.

- **Dollar index:** approx. **101.46**, about four tenths below the New York session close, after a low of approx. 101.25.
- **GBP/USD:** approx. **1.3475**, about 64 pips higher and at the night's highs. Sterling keeps climbing even when the dollar bounces, one day before the Bank of England.
- **EUR/USD:** approx. **1.1238**, about 47–48 pips higher, after touching approx. 1.1262.
- **USD/JPY:** approx. **158.06**, about 60 pips lower. It reached approx. 157.39 and bounced from there. The Japanese government said it is watching markets "with a high sense of urgency" (single source), a low-grade verbal warning the pair barely noticed.
- **USD/CHF:** approx. **0.8164**, about 40 pips lower, after a low of approx. 0.8124.
- **USD/CAD:** approx. **1.3958**, about 50 pips lower.
- **AUD/USD and NZD/USD:** up about 25–30 pips, to approx. 0.699 and 0.5617, carried by dollar weakness more than by reasons of their own.

### Treasuries and stocks

**Treasury yields are lower across the curve**, led by the 5-year, which fell as much as 9 basis points to approx. 4.68% (single-source figure). The **2-year** is around approx. **4.60%** and the **10-year** approx. **4.93–4.94%**, about 2–4 basis points lower depending on the reference. The underlying idea is that a Congress that restrains fiscal expansion eases worries about federal borrowing.

In equities, the blow landed on tech. **Nasdaq 100 futures** fell nearly **3%** in the middle of the night on fears of tougher artificial-intelligence regulation, and are now down approx. **0.4–0.6%**, with dip buying. **S&P 500** futures are down approx. **0.3%**. In Asia, **Korea** had the most erratic session: the Kospi opened sharply higher, fell as much as approx. 4% and then erased its losses (single-source figures). The broad regional index is down approx. 0.2%.

### Gold: back above $4,000

**Gold** rises approx. **2.5%** and is back above **$4,000**: December futures are around approx. $4,038 and spot approx. $4,016–4,026. It is driven by the weak dollar, falling real yields and demand for protection against a more confrontational political backdrop.

### Oil: Hormuz, no reaction

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claims that four tankers were destroyed by mines on a "prohibited" route south of the Strait of Hormuz and that it seized two other vessels. This is an **Iranian claim with no independent verification**, and Washington has previously denied mines in the main shipping lanes. Crude barely reacted: **January Brent** trades around **$104**, approx. $0.65 above its settlement, and **December WTI** around approx. $96.6.

### China

**China's services PMI** disappointed at **50.8**, against approx. 51.5 expected, and the yuan remains weak after Beijing tightened foreign-currency reserve requirements earlier this week.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If eurozone inflation (4:00 AM CT) comes in at or above the expected 4.0%**, with core at 2.5%, it would reinforce a December ECB hike and give the euro support of its own on top of dollar weakness. A softer print would remove part of that support.
- **If sterling keeps its relative strength** after the UK PMI, the market would be heading into Thursday's Bank of England meeting with the hike firmly priced in.
- **If US ADP and ISM services come in firm**, the view that the dollar's slide is political and temporary would gain weight, because a December Fed hike is already almost fully priced. Weak data, on the other hand, would add an economic reason to the political one.
- **If artificial-intelligence regulation returns to the headlines**, tech would remain Wall Street's weak spot.
- **If the Hormuz incidents are independently verified**, crude could react more strongly than it did overnight.
