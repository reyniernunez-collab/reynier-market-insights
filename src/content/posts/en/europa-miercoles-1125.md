**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

Dallas, Texas — Nov 25, 2026, 08:14 CT

**Executive summary:** Wednesday's Europe session deepened the tech selloff: Nasdaq 100 futures were down as much as approx. 3% and S&P 500 futures approx. 2%, with Nvidia more than 5% lower premarket and all of the Magnificent Seven in the red, as doubts grow over the payoff from AI spending and the cost of funding it. Asia had already dropped hard (Kospi −6.2%, Nikkei −2.8%). Money ran to Treasuries, with yields falling across the curve, and the dollar held up because the Aussie and sterling fell harder than the euro. The yen, by contrast, barely joined in. Gold slipped below $3,500 for the first time since September 2025 and bitcoin kept falling. With New York already in premarket, October core PCE came in at 3.3% year on year (a tenth above forecast; 0.3% month on month, in line) and Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%.

**Key levels (Europe close, approx. 06:37 CT):**

- **Dollar index:** 103.94 · firm as a haven despite lower yields
- **EUR/USD:** 1.0951 · the euro holds up better than the rest of the G10
- **GBP/USD:** 1.3072 · gives up about 44 pips on risk aversion
- **USD/JPY:** 159.30 · the yen ignores the equity slide
- **AUD/USD:** 0.6570 · the hardest-hit currency, down about 71 pips
- **Brent:** $92.16 ⚠️ · quotes vary widely across sources (88.99–92.16)

**Central banks:**

- **Fed (Dec.):** hike approx. 100% priced; slightly firmer core PCE and strong GDP reinforce that view.
- **ECB (Dec.):** hike approx. 94% ⚠️ (other gauges put it somewhat higher); Kocher expects higher inflation projections.
- **BoE (Dec.):** hike approx. 88%, up eight points after Greene backed further increases.
- **BoJ (Dec.):** hike approx. 70% ⚠️ (down nine points; other markets see it higher), which partly explains the yen's muted response.

**Risks:**

1. **Reading PCE and GDP:** 3.3% core and 4.3% growth support the case for a hawkish Fed; if bonds give back their gains, the dollar could also gain against the euro and the yen.
2. **AI selloff:** a second day of steep chip losses, plus new US rules limiting Chinese companies' use of advanced US chips in Southeast Asia, keeps pressure on risk; a sharp turn could trigger a fast Aussie rebound, with shorts heavily crowded.
3. **Tariffs and calendar:** a possible announcement of new tariffs to replace those struck down by the Supreme Court; plus new home sales (09:00 CT), crude inventories (09:30 CT), the 7-year auction (12:00 CT) and the Beige Book (13:00 CT), ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday and thin liquidity.
4. **Energy:** attacks on Russian refineries, lower flows through the CPC pipeline and tension in Hormuz; the Brent level varies widely by source ⚠️.
