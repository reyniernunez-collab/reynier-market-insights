> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 27, 2026, 01:55 CT:** Tuesday's Asia session belonged to stocks and the dollar. Korean and Japanese chipmakers lifted the region after Monday's Wall Street rebound, while the dollar kept rising without yields following along and the yen shrugged off Tokyo's latest warning.

## Executive Summary

Appetite for tech is still in charge. Asia picked up where Monday's New York session left off, and stocks in Japan and Korea rallied hard, although the momentum faded late in the night: Nasdaq 100 futures are down approx. 0.2% and Europe is pointing to a slightly lower open.

In currencies, the dominant note is a **firm dollar that doesn't need bonds**. My read: the market is positioning ahead of four days with five central banks, and the yen is the currency coping worst. Nothing that happened tonight changes the week's script; it just sharpens it.

## Analysis

### Stocks: chips lead, but the momentum fades

At the Asia close, **Japan is up approx. 1.7%** and **Korea** between approx. **2.9% and 3.4%** (the gap depends on when the reading is taken). Korea's two big memory makers rose more than 4% ahead of reporting earnings this week.

The mood also rests on a flood of tech-sector fundraising, with large share and debt deals to fund AI investment. But the real test starts Wednesday: **Alphabet and Microsoft** report on Wednesday, and **Amazon and Intel** on Thursday. The market will focus mainly on spending plans and whether that investment is starting to pay off; not everyone is ready to call this rebound a sustained recovery yet.

### Dollar: rising without help from yields

The **dollar index** hit the night's highs at approx. **102.1**, even though 2- and 10-year Treasury yields fell 1 to 2 bp. It's a third straight session of a firm dollar, and doing it without support from rates points to positioning ahead of Wednesday's Fed rather than a shift in expectations.

- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1165**, a small decline.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3330**, at Monday's lows a day before the UK Budget. A hotter-than-expected shop price reading (approx. 2.1% year on year versus approx. 1.6% expected, a single-source figure) doesn't help sterling on the eve of the Budget.
- The **New Zealand dollar** was the night's firmest currency, hours before its central bank decides.

### Yen: Tokyo warns, the market doesn't listen

**Japan's finance minister** told parliament the government is ready to take decisive action in the currency market and is watching moves with a high sense of urgency. Even so, **USD/JPY rose about 30 pips, to approx. 157.8**, near one-month highs.

My read: the market took it as the usual verbal warning. But the risk of actual intervention grows as the pair approaches the 158–158.5 area, and action from Tokyo would move the yen violently at any time of day. The yen isn't working as a haven whether stocks are up or down.

### Oil: steady with diplomacy in the background

**Brent** holds around **$115**, slightly lower (approx. −0.7%), as senior officials from several countries travel to Iran to revive talks and prevent a wider escalation in the Middle East. There were reports of new missile and drone launches from Yemen toward Saudi Arabia that were intercepted, a single-source item the market didn't price in. Today's elections in Israel are another risk focus for crude.

### Central banks: five meetings in four days

Market probabilities barely moved overnight (I give ranges because the measures differ):

- **New Zealand (tonight, 8:00 PM CT):** a 25 bp hike to 3.00% is all but priced (approx. 94–98%). What matters is the guidance on December, where the measures disagree quite a bit.
- **Fed (Wednesday):** a near-certain pause and a December hike at around **76–80%**. The chair's message is what will move prices.
- **Bank of Canada (Wednesday):** a hike at around 81–87%.
- **ECB (Thursday):** on hold; the December probability is high, but sources disagree on how high.
- **Bank of Japan (Friday):** on hold; the December probability shows a wide spread between measures.
- **Australia (November 3):** approx. 60–68%, with tonight's CPI as the data point that could tip the balance.

In overnight data, Japan's services producer prices rose 3.7% year on year, in line with expectations, and China's industrial profits are up approx. 15.5% year to date.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If the dollar keeps climbing without support from yields**, the pre-Fed positioning reading would gain weight, and the risk would be a sharp reversal if the Fed chair doesn't confirm a December hike.
- **If USD/JPY keeps moving toward 158–158.5**, the risk that Tokyo moves from words to action would grow; an intervention would trigger a broad yen move within minutes.
- **If durable goods and consumer confidence come in strong**, they would reinforce the idea of a Fed that isn't done; weak data would give the euro and the yen some room.
- **If sterling stays at its lows on the eve of the Budget**, the market would be hedging against a poorly received fiscal plan; a low-key Budget on Wednesday could bring relief.
