**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

# Week of Nov 16–20: the December Fed hike is priced in, and this week decides January

Dallas, Texas — Nov 15, 2026, 15:45 CT

**Subtitle:** The dollar ended the week up approx. 1%, and a December Fed hike is now practically priced in. The question is **January**, which the market puts between approx. 59% and 77% ⚠️. The **FOMC minutes** (Wednesday), Warsh's first speech and retail sales can push that number; Wednesday's UK CPI and Nvidia's earnings round out a week with no G10 central bank meetings.

**Pills:**

- **FOMC minutes (Wed 1:00 PM CT):** the week's main event
- **UK CPI (Wed 1:00 AM CT):** consensus approx. 3.5–3.7% ⚠️ (prior 3.5%)
- **Fed:** December approx. 96–100%; January approx. 59–77% ⚠️
- **DXY (Friday New York close):** 103.41, +1.1% on the week
- **USD/JPY:** 159.66–159.69, with 160 as a de facto ceiling
- **Brent:** $95.84, −2.5% on the week despite Hormuz

**Cards:**

**1. Fed: December is priced, January is in play.** Nothing this week changes December (approx. 96–100%); what moves is January. On Monday Warsh speaks for the first time as Fed chair (single-source timing ⚠️), on Tuesday October retail sales arrive (7:30 AM CT; expected 0.2% after 0.8%, ex-autos 0.4% after 0.9%) and on Wednesday the minutes. More than the retail headline, the control group matters (approx. 0.4% ⚠️, single source): firm spending plus minutes that discuss several hikes would push January toward the top of the range. Holding it back: October PPI (0.3% m/m versus 0.4% expected, core in line) and the Treasury Secretary's public call for no further tightening. The threshold I'm watching: if January drops below approx. 55%, the dollar loses its main support.

**2. United Kingdom: the busiest week.** Wages on Tuesday (1:00 AM CT; expected 3.5% after 3.7%, unemployment 4.7%) and CPI on Wednesday (consensus approx. 3.5–3.7% ⚠️, core 2.8%). The market sees a December BoE hike at approx. 70–78%, and one committee member has already talked about more hikes in 2027. A CPI of 3.7% or higher would reinforce December; 3.5% or lower would cast doubt on it. Retail sales close the week on Friday (expected 0.0% after −0.4%).

**3. Japan, Canada and Australia: inflation and jobs with split meetings.** In Japan, Q3 GDP (Sunday 5:50 PM CT, expected 0.1% q/q) opens the week, and Thursday's core CPI (5:30 PM CT, expected 2.3% after 2.1%) is the test for a December BoJ hike, priced anywhere from approx. 84–93% to 55% depending on the measure ⚠️. USD/JPY remains capped near 160 on suspected, unconfirmed intervention; its implied volatility, the highest in FX, points to a weekly range of approx. 158.00–161.20. In Canada, Monday's CPI (7:30 AM CT; expected 3.2% y/y) arrives with a split December BoC (approx. 47–62% ⚠️), making it the data point with the most power to move a meeting. In Australia, Wednesday's jobs report (6:30 PM CT, approx. +25–28K ⚠️) comes with the RBA almost certainly on hold.

**4. Nvidia and the US consumer.** Nvidia reports on Tuesday after the close, following its CEO's exit; its volatility could spill over into risk appetite. Home Depot (Tuesday), Target, Lowe's and TJX (Wednesday) and Walmart (Thursday, 6:00 AM CT), all before the open, complete the read on spending alongside retail sales.

**5. Hormuz and geopolitics: loud headlines, smaller reaction.** Iranian sources report tankers damaged by mines over the weekend, while the US says the main routes are clear. Brent still fell 2.5% on the week: only a breakthrough or a breakdown in negotiations would move it sharply. Implied volatility gives a weekly range of approx. $89–103; a shock scenario (Hormuz or Nvidia) would push it toward the top and favor the yen as a haven. On Thursday, the Putin–Xi–Trump summit on the sidelines of APEC arrives with no deal expected.
