**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

Dallas, Texas — Nov 26, 2026, 15:45 CT

**Executive summary:** Thursday's New York session was a holiday session: US stocks and bonds were closed for Thanksgiving and futures ended early, at 12:00 CT. S&P 500 futures settled approx. 0.5% higher, though they gave back part of the gains they carried from Europe. The dollar barely moved and currencies spent the afternoon in very tight ranges. The day's main story came from the Middle East: a projectile hit Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery, killing one worker and halting part of its operations, and Kuwait reported an attack it blames on Iran, which Tehran denies ⚠️. Oil and gold rose. In Brussels, euro-area leaders chose Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next ECB president. US trade data scheduled for today could not be confirmed because of the holiday ⚠️.

**Key levels (approx. 15:45 CT; futures at the 12:00 CT early close):**

- **S&P 500 futures:** 7,234.5 · mildly bullish (+0.5%, below the morning high)
- **Nasdaq 100 futures:** 26,221 · mildly bullish (+0.5%)
- **Dollar index:** 103.78 · neutral (+0.1%, holiday liquidity)
- **USD/JPY:** 159.27 · neutral (flat ahead of Tokyo CPI)
- **WTI:** $84.91 · bullish (+1.5%, attack in Saudi Arabia)
- **Gold:** approx. $3,566–3,593 ⚠️ · bullish (readings differ across providers)

**Central banks:**

- **Fed (Dec.):** hike approx. 98% priced (morning reading); with no data or speakers because of the holiday, markets still expect another increase.
- **ECB (Dec.):** hike approx. 93% ⚠️ (another reading puts it at 94%). Hernández de Cos will succeed Lagarde in February; he is seen as a continuity pick, with formal appointment expected in December.
- **BoE (Dec.):** hike approx. 83% ⚠️ (other gauges put it lower).
- **BoJ (Dec.):** hike approx. 71% ⚠️ (other markets put it higher); tonight's Tokyo CPI could shift that bet.

**Risks:**

1. **Gulf and Red Sea:** the Jazan refinery attack, Kuwait's accusation against Iran and the tanker hit near Hormuz raise oil's risk premium; further escalation would support the dollar and the Swiss franc. Brent prices differ widely across providers ⚠️.
2. **Tokyo CPI (17:30 CT):** 3.1% expected; a reading of 3.4% or more would support the yen and the bet on a December BoJ hike, while 2.8% or less would cool it.
3. **Friday's half session:** Wall Street trades shortened hours and closes at 12:00 CT on thin liquidity; before that, French and Spanish CPI and German unemployment could move the euro in the Europe session.
4. **OPEC+ on Sunday:** the group is reportedly leaning toward keeping output targets through at least the first quarter of 2027; any surprise would move oil at Monday's Asia open.
