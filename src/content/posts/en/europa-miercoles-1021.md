> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 21, 2026, 08:09 CT:** The Europe session left an uncomfortable milestone: France's 10-year yield touched 5% for the first time in more than two decades. Even so, the euro didn't sink against the dollar; it took the hit against sterling. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens: what moved, what's priced and the conditional scenarios I'm working with for the rest of the day.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was Australia (a more hawkish central bank) and oil climbing back to $110. The Europe session added two pieces: **France crossed a symbolic line** and **UK inflation came in above expectations**, though the market barely rewarded it.

With the Bank of England's November hike, the Bank of Canada's October hike and much of the Fed path already priced, and no big US data today, what's moving currencies are **two risk premiums: France's and oil's**. My read: the euro isn't falling against the dollar despite French stress; it's paying for it against sterling.

## Analysis

### France: the 10-year hits 5%

France's 10-year yield reached **5.00%** around 4:05 AM CT, something not seen since 2002, and the spread over Germany widened to approx. **157–159 bp** (several measures agree; around 158 bp now), close to 2011 levels. Behind it: the 2027 budget in Parliament, weekend unrest, next year's election and Friday's (Oct 23) rating review, where many expect a downgrade (⚠️ unconfirmed expectation).

What stands out is that **the stress didn't spread**: eurozone stocks rose approx. 0.6–0.8%.

### The euro holds vs the dollar, not vs sterling

- **EUR/USD:** dipped only about 20 pips (low of approx. 1.1211 at 4:11 AM CT) and returned to approx. **1.123**, practically where Tuesday's New York session closed. An ECB member sent a two-sided message: more tightening if inflation persists, but caution because high yields are already slowing the economy (⚠️ unconfirmed).
- **EUR/GBP:** fell to approx. **0.8366** (4:04 AM CT) and hovers near 0.838. It's the fifth straight session of euro losses against sterling, which is up approx. 2% on the month. GBP/USD touched **1.3400** (3:56 AM CT), near one-month highs.

### UK: hotter inflation, no extra reward

September CPI rose to **3.5%** year on year (3.4% expected; August 3.1%). Services, the part the Bank of England watches most, came in at **3.7%**, above forecasts (⚠️ sources disagree on the consensus), and core at **2.8%**, in line or a tenth above depending on the source. Fuel explains most of the jump; monthly core actually eased. House prices rose only 0.9% year on year in August, below expectations.

The key is the reaction: the UK 2-year yield **fell** approx. 3 bp (⚠️ unconfirmed). With the Nov 5 hike practically priced (approx. 95–97%) and about four hikes priced through mid-2027, a hot print adds little. Sterling is rising more on euro weakness and flows than on the market adding hikes. And that's the risk: November looks locked in, the following hikes less so.

### Australia: the shift holds

AUD/USD printed approx. **0.7036** (5:56 AM CT), a more-than-three-week high. The market didn't unwind the jump in RBA hike odds after its deputy governor's hawkish speech: inflation is running worse than the bank projected in August (pricier oil, data centers, services with little spare capacity). The Nov 3 hike sits at approx. 40–53% (⚠️ sources disagree; read it as "about half"). The filter is Australia's quarterly CPI on Oct 28.

### Oil: a bounce inside the range

Brent rose to approx. **$111.3** (5:16 AM CT) on attacks on ships and no diplomatic progress with Iran, then slipped back to approx. $109.4–109.7 without a clear headline. It remains inside the sideways range of recent days, below Tuesday's high (approx. $112). The US envoy's meeting with Putin on a Ukraine peace plan had a conciliatory tone.

### Dollar, yen, gold and stocks

- **Dollar:** the index is flat, approx. **101.7**.
- **Yen:** USD/JPY eases to approx. **157.9**, with short-term US yields a bit lower. For the BoJ, October remains nearly ruled out and the focus is on December.
- **Gold:** up approx. 0.2% (near **$4,150**).
- **Stocks:** the eurozone is in the green. Underneath, risk gauges (volatility and credit spreads) have tightened somewhat, but earnings keep supporting equities. Meta, Tesla, IBM, SAP and Texas Instruments report today.
- **Emerging markets:** a surprise from Indonesia, which held rates when a hike was expected.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Bank of England governor (8:00 AM CT):** if he stresses services and inflation expectations, he validates the hiking path and sterling can extend. If he insists the spike is energy-driven, that the economy is running below capacity or that the Oct 28 budget will tighten fiscal policy, the market could trim 2027 hikes (not November's) and sterling would give ground, especially against the euro.
2. **France:** if the spread tops October's highs (approx. 159–160 bp) with the yield above 5%, the euro would start to suffer against the dollar too. If it eases below approx. 150 bp, EUR/GBP would benefit most.
3. **Oil:** only a clean break above Tuesday's high would bring back event mode; a sharp drop would strip out premium and some support for the Canadian dollar.
4. **New Zealand (Q3 CPI, after the New York close):** domestic inflation is what matters. A firm print locks in the October hike; a soft one would put it in doubt and widen the Australian dollar's edge over the kiwi.
