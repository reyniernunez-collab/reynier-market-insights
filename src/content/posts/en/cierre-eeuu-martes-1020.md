> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 20, 2026, 3:46 PM CT:** Tuesday started with fear of oil and of the artificial intelligence bill, and ended with the New York session buying almost everything except chips. Brent gave back its overnight spike, the dollar weakened and stocks outside tech did the heavy lifting.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the Dow gained **1.0%** to **52,495.88**, the S&P 500 rose **0.3%** to **7,712.57** and the Russell 2000 approx. **1.7%**. The Nasdaq Composite finished practically flat (**26,424.82**) and the semiconductor index slipped approx. **0.3%**. More than 400 S&P 500 members ended in the green: the mirror image of Monday, when the rebound was concentrated in chips and the rest of the market didn't follow.

The change in tone has two drivers. First, oil: Brent touched approx. **$112** in the Europe session and settled at approx. **$108.8**, below Monday's close. Second, earnings season, which keeps supporting companies outside tech while the market debates how long the AI spending cycle can last. The VIX fell approx. **6%** to **17.3**.

## Analysis

### Oil: the Hormuz spike didn't hold

Overnight, the escalation scenario was confirmed by price: Brent strung together candles above $110.7 in the Europe session and hit approx. **$112**, its highest since May, with Iran's Revolutionary Guard claiming tankers had struck mines south of Hormuz. That claim still lacks independent verification (⚠️ neither the date nor the damage is clear), and it was repeated in the afternoon without new evidence.

What weighed more in the New York session was the other side: contacts between the US and Iran continue, with conflicting reports about a Qatari delegation in Tehran and Pakistan's army chief leaving the Iranian capital after his meetings. Neither side is talking about a deal, and Tehran insists it doesn't trust Washington, but the market took out part of the risk premium. US officials also said flows through Hormuz are close to pre-war levels.

The result: Brent settled at approx. **$108.8** (−0.7%). That is the false-breakout scenario we saw as the least priced this morning. I'm not using November WTI as a reference: it expired today and its expiry price isn't comparable with other contracts.

### Stocks: breadth yes, chips no

The pattern of the day was rotation. The Dow and small caps rose strongly, while the Nasdaq 100 slipped approx. **0.1%** and semiconductors failed to extend Monday's rebound. Doubts about AI came back two ways: the Asia session had already punished chips in Seoul, and in New York a report said a major AI developer has compute commitments worth hundreds of billions of dollars over the coming years (⚠️ the figures differ depending on the reading). The market's question isn't whether there is demand, but who finances that spending and at what return.

That question is already visible in credit: a heavily indebted AI infrastructure company bounced in the stock market, but its bonds remain at distressed levels. After the close, more data arrived for the debate: SpaceX reported better-than-expected revenue and Super Micro pre-announced preliminary sales well above estimates (⚠️ preliminary single-source figures).

### Dollar and bonds: the long end eases, sterling rules

The dollar closed weak: the DXY ended near **101.7** (−0.3%), EUR/USD rose to approx. **1.124** from a low of **1.1204** in the Europe session, and USD/JPY stood at approx. **158.2**. In US bonds, the 30-year yield fell approx. **5 bp** to **5.42%** and the 10-year to approx. **5.11%**, while the 2-year rose to approx. **4.78%**. The curve is flattening: cheaper oil eases the long end, but it doesn't yet change the Fed reading. The Fed stays in its quiet period with an October hike at approx. **14–20%** (⚠️ the measures diverge).

Sterling was the strongest in the G10: GBP/USD approx. **1.338** (+0.6%) and EUR/GBP approx. **0.839**, its lowest since May 2025. Solid overnight jobs data was joined by a hawkish message from a Bank of England policymaker: wage disinflation has stalled and the risk of second-round effects is greater than thought. With November almost fully priced, that supports the 2027 part of the curve more than the next meeting.

The Canadian dollar kept almost all of its gains despite the drop in crude: USD/CAD approx. **1.4056**, about 55 pips below Monday's New York close. Gold, on the other hand, still didn't act as a safe haven and closed near **$4,141** (−0.5%).

### Europe: a better close than the morning

At the Europe close, stocks pared almost all of the morning's losses: Stoxx 600 approx. **−0.1%**, DAX approx. **−0.1%**, CAC 40 approx. **−0.4%** and FTSE 100 approx. **−0.3%**. The French 10-year premium over Germany held around approx. **153 bp**, near multi-week highs. The French budget vote that had been expected today moved to October 27, so the political risk is still there, but without an immediate catalyst.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If crude stays below approx. $109:** long-dated US bonds still have room to ease and the rotation into companies outside tech can continue; a new verified incident in Hormuz would bring the risk premium back immediately.
- **If the Asia session punishes chips again:** the gap between the Dow and the Nasdaq widens and the AI financing doubt gains weight ahead of big tech earnings.
- **If UK CPI comes in firm on the core measure:** sterling gets another boost via the 2027 curve; a soft print would take back part of today's gains, not the November hike.
- **If the French premium clearly breaks above 155 bp:** the euro becomes the weak side of the map again despite the soft dollar.
