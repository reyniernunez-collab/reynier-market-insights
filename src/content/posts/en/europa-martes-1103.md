> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 3, 2026, 08:19 CT:** Tuesday's Europe session reversed the overnight move: the dollar, which had slipped in the Asia session, recovered across the board. The interesting part is why: Fed expectations didn't change; yields rose and crude fell at the same time. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was an Australian central bank that hiked but hinted at a pause, chipmakers sinking Korea and crude bouncing after the strike on the Iranian island of Qeshm. **The Europe session changed the tone**: the dollar recovered, the franc gave back what it gained on its inflation data, and crude fell again when Qatar spoke of progress in mediation.

My read: **the dollar's recovery is about yields and caution, not the Fed**. The odds of a December hike are still approx. 72–75%, same as overnight. What rose were US yields (and European and UK yields too), on a day when the market is cutting risk ahead of the elections. With a more Democratic Congress already largely priced in, the move looks more like position-squaring before the result than a fresh bet on the dollar. That leaves the rest of the day to a second-tier data point, job openings, and to the Iran headlines.

## Analysis

### The dollar rises without the Fed moving

- **Dollar index:** goes from approx. **101.7** at the Asia session close to **101.85–101.9**, with a high near 101.88 mid-morning in Europe. Versus Monday's New York session close the gain is small (approx. +0.1).
- **US yields:** the 2-year rises to approx. **4.60%** and the 10-year to approx. **4.93%**, about 2–4 basis points above Monday's New York session close. UK and French 10-year yields also rose about 5 bp.
- **The Fed, unchanged:** a December hike is still priced at approx. 72–75%. No central bank moved its odds by 20 points or more since overnight.
- **Stocks:** the pan-European index is down approx. 0.4–0.5%; Germany opened near −1% and Italy and Spain approx. −1.1%, with tech lower on doubts about AI spending. US futures arrive mixed: S&P 500 approx. −0.1/−0.2% and Nasdaq 100 flat to slightly higher after entering a correction yesterday. Premarket, Palantir drops more than 8% on disappointing guidance and Apple approx. 3.9% on margin pressure (⚠️ unconfirmed premarket figures).

### The franc gives back its CPI move, the euro loses 1.12 and the yen is the weakest

- **USD/CHF:** Swiss inflation came in above expectations (1.5% year on year, the fastest pace in almost three years; ⚠️ the exact figure appears in only one source) and the franc jumped overnight, but in the Europe session it gave it all back: the pair went from approx. **0.815 to 0.820**. Core came in as expected, and the market barely raised the odds of a December Swiss central bank hike (approx. 13–17%, ⚠️ sources disagree).
- **EUR/USD:** loses **1.12** and falls to approx. **1.118**, with a low near 1.1175. A December ECB hike is still priced at approx. 83–85%; the euro's real test comes tomorrow with eurozone inflation.
- **USD/JPY:** rises to approx. **158.2**, with a high near 158.3, the highest in four days, with Tokyo closed for a holiday and US yields higher.
- **GBP/USD:** nearly unchanged (approx. 1.341) two days before the Bank of England, whose Thursday hike is all but priced (approx. 95–99%).
- **AUD/USD:** stays on the defensive (approx. 0.698) after the Australian central bank's cautious message; a second hike has nearly left pricing (December approx. 8–9%, ⚠️ thin market).
- **USD/CAD:** slightly lower (approx. 1.398). The odds of a December Bank of Canada hike moved the most today (approx. 43–51%, ⚠️ sources disagree), with no clear headline behind it.

### Oil and gold

**January Brent** touched approx. **$107** early in the Europe session on the US strike on Qeshm, then eased toward the **$105–106.5** area (⚠️ price references differ) when Qatar said both sides remain committed to mediation and are studying a proposal (a reply is expected in about a day and a half, ⚠️ unconfirmed). Pulling the other way, Iran spoke of tankers on fire in Hormuz (⚠️ unconfirmed). The striking thing today is that **yields and crude parted ways**: they usually rise together, and today the dollar rose without help from oil.

**Gold** falls again, approx. **−1%**, near **$3,910–3,925**: neither the Qeshm strike nor the drop in stocks sparked safe-haven demand.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Openings in line (approx. 7.1 million) and no Iran news (most likely):** the dollar keeps what it gained in Europe but without fresh momentum; the market waits for the elections.
2. **Strong openings (well above 7.1 million, with more quits):** yields and December Fed odds would rise; the dollar would extend its recovery, especially against the euro and yen.
3. **Weak openings:** yields would fall and the dollar would give back what it recovered in the Europe session.
4. **Iran:** a confirmed positive reply to Qatar's proposal would push crude lower, weigh on the Canadian dollar and help the euro and yen; a confirmed escalation would send crude back up and favor the franc and gold.
5. **Elections:** a more Democratic Congress is largely priced; the dollar surprise would be a Republican win in both chambers, and close or contested results would favor the franc, yen and gold.
