> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 7, 2026, 10:30 CT:** Europe closes Wednesday by undoing Tuesday's relief. Attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz push Brent back above $100, French debt comes under pressure again, and markets sell bonds and stocks at the same time. The dollar comes out ahead, and the New York afternoon is packed: a 10-year auction and the Fed minutes.

## Executive Summary

On Tuesday it looked like France was catching a break and oil was calming down. On Wednesday both reversed. Iran targeted ships in Hormuz again and made clear that passage won't normalize until its conditions are met, sending Brent to around $101.75. Pricier crude brings back worries about inflation and higher-for-longer rates: the US 10-year moves back above 5.30%, French bonds give up their rebound and the spread over Germany returns to ~137–139 bp. The Stoxx 600 falls ~1%, S&P 500 futures slip ~0.7% after Tuesday's record and Nasdaq 100 futures ~0.8%, led by chipmakers. The dollar is the big winner and the euro is again approaching its 17-month low.

## Analysis

### France: the market doesn't buy the fiscal fix yet

The OAT-Bund spread is once again the session's gauge. By our numbers, the French 10-year yield rises to the **4.82–4.86%** area and the premium over Bunds is back at **~137–139 bp**, after dropping to ~127–130 bp on Tuesday. Markets doubt a minority government can pass the cuts, and the option of forcing the budget through by decree (Article 49.3) opens the door to a no-confidence vote. On top of that came a warning from the IMF chief: with yields this high, heavily indebted countries face mounting budget pressure, and France is the clearest example. A few things keep this from turning into panic. A senior eurozone official said they see no contagion so far, and press reports say Paris is considering issuing more short-term debt to ease pressure at the long end. As long as the spread stays above ~130 bp, the euro carries its own drag.

### Oil: Hormuz in charge, reserves as the brake

Brent rises ~**1.2%** to ~$101.75, with WTI around $89.6. Iran insists free navigation depends on sanctions being lifted and what it calls aggression against the country being condemned, and it denies any nuclear talks with the US. Russia offered to mediate, with nothing concrete yet. The EIA data added pressure: crude inventories fell **3.19 million barrels**, against an expected build of 1.9 million. Gasoline, by contrast, rose 0.38 million when a draw was expected, and US production hit another record. The counterweight comes from the International Energy Agency: its members back speeding up the reserve releases pledged in March, roughly 100 million additional barrels, with priority for diesel. It's a brake, but it doesn't change the underlying risk while Hormuz stays blocked.

### Bonds and the dollar: everything pushes the same way

What stands out today is that bonds and stocks are falling together, a sign that the driver is inflation rather than growth fears. The US 10-year climbs to ~**5.30%**, near Monday's 5.35% (highest since 2002), right ahead of a $39 billion auction. In our data, the 30-day correlation between crude and the 10-year sits at **0.97**: when oil rises, yields follow, and the dollar with them. The DXY is around **102.3** (+0.5%), the highest since April 2025. EUR/USD trades near **1.1206** (−0.5%) after touching ~1.1186 this morning, close to its 17-month low. Sterling holds up somewhat better (GBP/USD ~1.322), because a Bank of England hike in November is almost fully priced (between ~87% and ~94% depending on the source; the figures don't fully match). USD/JPY sits around 158.1, just below the 158.5–160 zone where the risk of Japanese intervention rises. Gold drops more than 1% toward $4,120–4,130, weighed down by the dollar and yields.

### European data: Germany surprises, nobody notices

German industrial production for September rose **2.0%** on the month, well above the 0.5% expected. On another day it would have helped the euro, but today it went unnoticed next to France and oil. The 7-year Bund auction cleared at 3.36%, far above the previous 2.68%, another sign of how far European yields have climbed. From the ECB, Lane warned that rising long-term rates are slowing growth more than projected, a less hawkish message than the Fed's. Even so, markets still price ~**80%** odds of an ECB hike in December.

### US: inflation expectations heat up the run-up to the minutes

The New York Fed survey delivered the morning's most uncomfortable number. One-year inflation expectations rose to **3.9%** (3.58% the previous month and ~3.64% expected), the highest since May 2023. Three-year expectations rose to 3.3% and five-year stayed at 3.0%, so the long run remains anchored. Early this morning markets priced ~**22%** odds of a Fed hike in October and ~85% for December. A reading like this, with crude above $100, strengthens the case for the Fed's hawks. That's why the 1:00 PM CT minutes matter so much: if they confirm most members wanted to keep hiking soon, October stops being a side scenario.

## What I'm watching today

- **12:00 PM CT, 10-year note auction:** it will clear at the highest yield in decades. Weak demand would push the 10-year above 5.35% and give the dollar more strength; a solid auction would cap the rise before the minutes.
- **1:00 PM CT, FOMC minutes:** if they talk about more hikes "at upcoming meetings," October odds (~22% early this morning) could jump. If they sound stale next to the weak jobs data, the dollar could pull back from overbought levels.
- **OAT-Bund spread:** above ~140 bp, the euro would retest its 17-month low; below ~130 bp, it would get some breathing room. Any headline about Article 49.3 or a no-confidence vote will move the euro before the Fed does.
- **Hormuz and Brent:** if Iran follows through on blocking routes, crude could climb further and drag yields, CAD and JPY along with it.
- **USD/JPY at 158.5–160:** any warning from Tokyo about "excessive moves" could trigger a quick drop.
- **Tomorrow (Thursday):** Waller at 3:30 AM CT, ECB accounts at 6:30 AM, Bailey (Bank of England) at 7:15 AM and jobless claims at 7:30 AM CT.
