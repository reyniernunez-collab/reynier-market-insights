> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 23, 2026, 01:59 CT:** Friday's Asia session picked up where New York left off: selling in everything tied to AI and buying in bonds. But the scare didn't last. The region's stocks clawed back nearly all their losses before the close, US Treasuries gave back a small part of their rally, and the only move that didn't stall was oil.

## Executive Summary

The night came in two halves. In the first, the hangover from the New York session close (the S&P 500's worst day since July, the Nasdaq down more than 2% and the 10-year yield's biggest drop in more than three years) dragged Korea and Japan to deep intraday lows. In the second, Nasdaq futures turned around and Asian stocks closed well off those lows.

The exception was **oil**: **Brent** topped **$114** and hit its highest level in five months, with fresh attacks on Saudi Arabia in the background. It is the figure most likely to challenge the "bonds up, less hawkish Fed" reading that Thursday left behind.

## Analysis

### Stocks: the scare stays intraday

At the Asia session close:

- **Nikkei:** approx. **−0.3%**, after falling as much as approx. 2.3% at the worst point.
- **Kospi:** practically flat. It fell as much as approx. 4% to three-month lows, and closing readings range from a small loss (approx. −0.6%) to a small gain (approx. +0.2%) depending on the time and the source.
- **Hong Kong:** approx. **+1.1%**. **Shanghai:** approx. +0.6%, after trading in the red for much of the session.
- **Australia:** flat. **Taiwan:** closed for a typhoon.

**Nasdaq 100** futures are up approx. **0.3–0.45%** and **S&P 500** futures are flat; Dow futures are down approx. 0.3%. The takeaway: the market is still cutting exposure to tech and chips, but the night brought no second wave of selling. A rebound with no new news suggests part of Thursday's drop was about positioning.

### Oil: the one thing that isn't slowing

Brent went from approx. **$113.1** at the New York session close to approx. **$114.4** (approx. +1.3%), with a high of approx. **$114.7** around 9 PM CT. The night's hourly closes ranged between approx. $113.7 and $114.5, always above Thursday's close. US crude is around **$100.8** (approx. +1.7%).

Behind it is the Gulf: overnight there were reports of a ballistic missile fired toward Riyadh being intercepted and of drones in southern Saudi Arabia, with early warnings in several cities. That adds to what Thursday already brought: reports of plans for new strikes on Iran after the US elections, and Washington's decision to use an emergency production law to expand oil capacity, whose short-term effect looks limited.

We read this as a market that is **already pricing an event**, not just a risk premium. The risk for other assets is inflation: crude holding above $114 makes it harder to argue the Fed can afford to be less hawkish.

### Bonds and the Fed: the rally holds, with a small breather

The US 10-year yield ended Thursday at approx. **4.96%**, a drop of about 17–19 bp depending on the closing basis, the biggest since March 2023. In the Asia session it bounced about **3 bp, to approx. 4.98%**, and the 2-year rose to approx. **4.67%**. In other words, the market is keeping almost all of the move.

What that means for pricing: a Fed hike in December is **no longer fully priced**; the different measures put it at approx. **74–81%**, down from more than 80% early Thursday. October sits at approx. 6–10%. Japanese bonds also rallied: the 10-year yield is down between 6 and 9 bp depending on the time, to approx. 2.80%.

### Japan: firmer inflation, a yen that couldn't follow through

September inflation came in as expected on the headline (2.1%) and the core (2.1%), but the measure excluding fresh food and energy rose to **2.4%** from 1.9%: all three measures above 2% at once. On top of that, the government is considering removing gasoline subsidies from December, which would push prices higher. The flash manufacturing PMI fell to 53.3, below expectations.

**USD/JPY** touched approx. **156.1** after the data, then recovered to approx. **157.1** (approx. +0.2% versus the New York session close) as US yields bounced and stocks steadied. The inflation data strengthen the case for a Bank of Japan hike in December (October remains at approx. 3%), but tonight the dollar's rebound against the yen weighed more. The central bank also released its financial stability report: it sees banks able to withstand an oil and AI shock, but flags the Middle East and AI investment as things to watch.

### Currencies: a steady dollar, havens lose steam

Indicative readings versus Thursday's New York session close:

- **DXY** approx. **101.7** (practically flat).
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.121**, in a narrow range of about 20 pips: with French markets still closed, the euro waited.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.339** (slightly lower): consumer confidence plunged to −24 (approx. −15 expected) and September retail sales fell **0.4%** month on month (approx. −0.1% expected), **0.7%** excluding fuel. Two weak readings in a row against a Bank of England with a November hike practically priced in (approx. 95–97%).
- **USD/CHF** approx. **0.822** (approx. +0.25%): the franc gives back part of its gains.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.702**, flat despite a services PMI that dropped into contraction (**47.8** from 51.9). The odds of a November hike by Australia's central bank slip to approx. **56–65%** depending on the source.
- **NZD/USD** approx. **0.569** (approx. +0.2%). **USD/CAD** approx. **1.401**, steady with the CAD supported by crude.

In China, the central bank set the yuan at its strongest level since February 2023. **Gold** still isn't working as a haven: it slips further, to approx. **$4,010–4,030** depending on whether you look at spot or futures, after losing almost 3% on Thursday.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent holds above $114** with hourly closes above that level, inflation pressure could slow the bond rally and give the dollar back some support; if it slips back toward $113, the "less hawkish Fed" reading stays in charge.
- **If the European PMIs come in weak**, the euro would have a second front on top of France; the French rating review, after the Europe session close, adds risk heading into the weekend.
- **If sterling starts pricing the consumer data**, the UK PMI at 3:30 AM CT would be the confirmation; so far, the weak data have barely moved the price.
- **If the Nasdaq futures rebound holds** into the New York session open, the yen and Treasuries could keep giving back part of their gains; if it fades, Thursday's risk-off would be back on the table.
