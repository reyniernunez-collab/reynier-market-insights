**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

# Week of Nov 9–13: Tuesday's CPI decides whether the December Fed hike is confirmed

Dallas, Texas — Nov 8, 2026, 15:50 CT

**Subtitle:** October payrolls surprised to the upside (165K versus approx. 60K expected) and left the dollar in charge. The market already prices a December Fed hike at around 75%, so **Tuesday's CPI** isn't asking whether a hike is coming, but whether the data confirms it or casts doubt. Around it, tech remains fragile after the Democratic sweep and the Gulf adds oil risk.

**Pills:**

- **US CPI (Tue 7:30 AM CT):** expected +0.3% m/m and +0.2% core
- **Fed, December:** hike approx. 74–77%
- **ECB, December:** hike approx. 85%
- **DXY (Fri close):** 102.26–102.32, after +0.77% on Friday
- **USD/JPY:** 158.45, near the zone of verbal intervention
- **Brent:** $98.32, with upside risk from the Gulf

**Cards:**

**1. US CPI (Tuesday 7:30 AM CT): the composition matters.** Consensus is 0.3% and 0.2% core; there is no consensus for the annual rate. An in-line 0.3% / 0.2% (approx. 45% probability in my read) keeps December unchanged and USD/JPY in 157.8–159.0. A hot print (approx. 30%), with the US 2-year above 4.60%, pushes USD/JPY above 159.2. A soft print, or an Iran deal (approx. 25% combined), takes support from the dollar. Core and services matter more than the headline.

**2. Central banks: no probability moved 20 points on the week.** December Fed at approx. 74–77% (three measures agree). December ECB at approx. 85%. For the UK, the December hike runs from approx. 40% to approx. 62% depending on the measure ⚠️, and for Japan one measure gives approx. 76% for a hike while another puts "no change" at 61% ⚠️: treat them as ranges. Q3 UK GDP (Thursday 1:00 AM CT) and US PPI (Friday 7:30 AM CT, expected 0.4% and 0.3% core) can move those prices.

**3. Dollar, euro and yen: same wind, different ammunition.** The DXY and USD/JPY bias both turn bullish, with the dollar backed by jobs (revisions +84K, unemployment 4.1%, wages +0.4%) and yields (2-year approx. 4.51–4.52%). Against that, EUR/USD at 1.1143 and sterling at 1.3355 depend on CPI not contradicting them. Against the trend: speculative positioning as of Nov 3 shows long yen (80th percentile or higher) and a very short euro (below the 20th percentile) ⚠️, a single-source visual read.

**4. Gulf and crude: no deal, but damage.** A mandate to negotiate exists, but there is no agreement. Over the weekend the Houthis damaged Aramco facilities in Riyadh (confirmed), while mines in Hormuz are only cited by Iran ⚠️. A deal would lower Brent; a Hormuz escalation would take it above $100. Brent already fell approx. 16% last week, so the room for a rebound exists.

**5. Tech and politics: regulatory fear is still alive.** The tech selloff after the change of control in Congress left the Nasdaq down close to 3.4% on the week and gold up approx. 5%. If tech rebounds, the yen loses its haven bid; if it falls again, the dollar and the yen compete for safe-haven demand.
