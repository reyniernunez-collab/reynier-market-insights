> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 2, 2026, 15:41 CT:** Monday had two stories pulling in opposite directions. On one side, oil plunged as the diplomatic track with Iran gains momentum. On the other, the chip selloff didn't stop and pushed the Nasdaq 100 into correction territory. In between, a stronger-than-expected ISM manufacturing report gave the dollar some air back right before tomorrow's elections. Here is how the landscape looks after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 fell **0.51%** to **7,541.40**, the Nasdaq Composite **1.07%** to **25,164.32** and the Nasdaq 100 **1.39%** to **27,989.82**. The Dow Jones, by contrast, rose **0.41%** to **51,983.98**, and the Russell 2000 slipped just **0.21%**. The VIX climbed to the **21–21.5** area, from **19.4** at Friday's New York close.

What matters is the shape of the day: **most stocks rose, but the tech-heavy indexes fell**. That is the opposite of Friday, when the selling spread across the whole market. Cheaper oil helped the rest of the market and long-dated bonds; chips and AI spending remained the problem.

## Analysis

### Chipmakers: the correction is now official

The semiconductor index fell **3.37%** to **10,328.33** and is down approx. **12%** over three sessions. That leaves the Nasdaq 100 10% below its October peak, the definition of a technical correction.

Intel dropped approx. **8.8%** after Friday's collapse. Marvell lost approx. **12.7%** after announcing the purchase of Lumentum, a deal valued at about **$115 billion**. Oracle fell approx. **8.9%** after it emerged it is seeking about **$20 billion** in debt and equity to fund its AI investment. Meta is also testing the bond market for at least **$20 billion**. The message for the market is uncomfortable: AI spending is no longer paid for with cash alone, and every new issue is a reminder that the bill keeps growing.

Apple slipped approx. **1.8%** before publishing its results after the close. It is the first report under its new CEO and one of the few big tech names that has avoided heavy data-center spending. The reaction to those numbers will play out in the Asia session.

### Oil: diplomacy takes the premium out of the barrel

December WTI settled at **$96.46**, about **$7.66** lower (approx. **−7.4%**). January Brent finished in the **$104–105** area (⚠️ closing figures vary by source), some **$8.7–9.3** below Friday's settlement. In the Europe session Brent was around **$109**: almost all of the additional drop came during New York.

The driver was political. Iran's president said the supreme leader has given him more room to negotiate peace, and Trump said a deal "could happen," linking the oil drop to that progress. He also signaled he does not plan to resume strikes after the elections. For the market, what is deflating is the **geopolitical premium**, not physical scarcity: supply remains tight, which is why any headline in the opposite direction could reverse part of the move just as quickly.

### ISM: industry accelerates and prices cool

October's ISM manufacturing index rose to **55.5**, above the **54.5** expected and the prior month. Production jumped to **59.0**, its highest since November 2021, and new orders rose to **55.5**. Employment dipped slightly to **52.4**. The most important figure for the Fed, **prices paid**, eased to **75.2** from **77.9**: still high, but rising at a slower pace.

It is a mixed combination for the Fed: stronger activity, but less cost pressure. That is why the probability of a December hike barely moved and stays at approx. **72–74%** in prediction markets. The underlying decision still hinges on Friday's jobs report.

### Bonds: the long end wins, the short end holds

The 2-year yield ended around **4.60%**, slightly above Friday's New York close. The 10-year fell to the **4.89–4.92%** area (⚠️ sources disagree on the exact level) and the 30-year to approx. **5.29%**. The curve flattened: cheaper oil eases long-term inflation expectations, while the short end stays anchored to a Fed expected to hike in December.

### Dollar: gives back part of what Europe gained

The dollar index ended around **101.77**, practically flat versus Friday's New York close, after falling as low as **101.43** in the Europe session. The strong ISM and a 2-year yield that didn't fall gave it support back.

EUR/USD closed at approx. **1.119**, well off the Europe-session high of **1.1237**, though still above Friday. The **Swiss franc** was the day's strongest: USD/CHF at approx. **0.818** (approx. −0.8%). The **yen still didn't participate**: USD/JPY at approx. **157.9**, still near the 158 ceiling. Sterling ends at approx. **1.341**.

The **Australian dollar** was again the weakest: AUD/USD lost 0.70 and closed at approx. **0.6995–0.6999**, hours before the RBA decision. The **Canadian dollar** barely suffered despite the oil plunge: USD/CAD at approx. **1.3985**.

### Gold: up with the dollar calm

December gold rose approx. **1.5%** to the **$3,957** area, recovering part of Friday's slump. With oil falling and long-dated yields easing, it worked as a hedge again.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If the reaction to Apple's results is positive in the Asia session:** tech could stabilize and the Nasdaq 100 correction would stay confined to chips; if it disappoints, the last big tech name acting as a haven would join the selling.
- **If the RBA hikes tonight as expected:** the focus will be on the tone. Guidance open to more hikes could support the Australian dollar after days of selling; a "one and done" message would extend the weakness.
- **If the Iran talks keep progressing:** the oil premium could deflate further, a relief for energy importers such as the euro area and Japan; a setback would quickly reverse part of the day's drop.
- **If tomorrow's elections confirm what prediction markets price** (a divided Congress or Democratic control): the impact could be limited because it is already priced; a surprise in either direction would move the dollar and long-dated bonds first.
