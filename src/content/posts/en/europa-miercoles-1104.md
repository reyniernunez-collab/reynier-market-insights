> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 4, 2026, 08:16 CT:** Wednesday's Europe session confirmed what began in Asia: the dollar kept falling to a one-month low on the midterm election result. But the market isn't reading it as a softer Fed, and that's the key to the day. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was a Democratic Congress sinking the dollar, gold reclaiming $4,000 and a Fed sticking to its script. **The Europe session deepened the dollar's slide**, brought back the risk appetite lost overnight and left the euro as the big winner, with euro area inflation at a three-year high.

My read: **the dollar is falling on a political and risk premium, not on a change at the Fed**. With Democrats running the Senate and the House, the market expects tighter limits on the president's fiscal agenda, doubts over AI regulation and possible government shutdowns in 2027. But the odds of a December Fed hike didn't fall: they rose. That divergence suggests the dollar's drop may run short if US data stay strong, which is why today's ISM services report matters more than usual.

## Analysis

### Dollar at lows, Fed pricing higher

- **Dollar index:** kept slipping in two legs through the Europe session, with a brief mid-morning bounce (approx. 101.5), and hit a **one-month low near 101.24** shortly before the New York open. Versus Tuesday's New York session close, the drop is about 0.6 points, on track for its worst day since September.
- **The Fed, priced higher:** a December hike rises to approx. **79–82%**, some 3–4 points above overnight. Not a big jump, but it runs against the dollar.
- **US yields:** the 2-year roughly flat at approx. **4.60%**; the 10-year eases to approx. **4.92–4.93%**, some 3–5 basis points below Tuesday's New York session close. The read: a divided Congress would rein in further spending and borrowing.
- **Stocks:** the overnight tech panic reversed. European stocks rise approx. 1% and S&P 500 futures gain approx. 0.9–1.1%, with the Nasdaq 100 also green as buyers return to AI names. Tesla-related shares fall after Democratic wins in the Senate and Texas.

### The euro leads, sterling stalls and the yen goes the other way

- **EUR/USD:** euro area inflation rose to **4.0%** (from 3.8%), with energy surging (approx. +20.8% year on year) and core at **2.5%**, no surprise. Still, the euro hit its session high at the end, approx. **1.127**, near four-week highs. A December ECB hike sits around **87–88%** (⚠️ one press report puts it near 72%).
- **GBP/USD:** stalls just under **1.35** after a final UK services PMI of 52.8, slightly below the flash reading. With tomorrow's Bank of England hike all but priced (approx. 96–99%), sterling hands the lead to the euro (EUR/GBP from approx. 0.8334 to 0.8347).
- **USD/JPY:** rises to approx. **158.3**, with a high near 158.6, even as the dollar falls against almost everything else. One reading is that structural Japanese capital outflows outweigh lower US yields (⚠️ single-source analysis).
- **Rest of the G10:** the New Zealand dollar leads gains (approx. +0.7%), the Swiss franc rises approx. 0.4% (USD/CHF approx. 0.815) and the Aussie reclaims 0.70.
- **Sweden:** its central bank raised rates to 2.00%, as expected.

### Oil and gold

**January Brent** went from approx. **$104.3** early in the Europe session to the **$101.3–102** area, near three-to-four-week lows, on the view that Washington may deprioritize the Iran conflict after the electoral defeat. Iranian claims about tankers hit by mines remain **unverified independently** (⚠️).

**Gold** holds above **$4,000** and touches approx. $4,060 late in the session, supported by the weak dollar.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **ISM in line (approx. 54–56) (most likely):** December Fed pricing stays near 80% and the dollar remains driven by the political premium, with no clear new direction.
2. **Strong ISM, with high prices paid and employment:** would reinforce a December Fed hike, push the 2-year higher and could halt the dollar's slide; the already weak yen would be most exposed.
3. **Weak ISM or softer prices:** would take weight off a December hike and open the door to fresh dollar lows, with the euro, the franc and gold benefiting.
4. **Iran:** verified progress in mediation would sink crude further and help the euro and the yen; a verified escalation would send crude back up and favor the franc and gold.
