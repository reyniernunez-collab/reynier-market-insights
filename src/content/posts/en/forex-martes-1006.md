> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 5, 2026, 4:45 PM CT:** The dollar ends Monday firmer, but not because the Fed turned more hawkish. It's being pushed by Europe, with sovereign risk in France and Spain, and by long-end yields at multi-decade highs. With "pause in October, hike in December" already priced, euro weakness remains the path of least resistance and the other majors stay in ranges.

## What's priced and why it matters

Tuesday's question isn't whether the Fed hikes in October. It's whether anything changes the script the market has already written.

- **Fed (3.75–4.00%):** October **81.7%** pause / 18.3% hike; December **79.1%** hike, with **24.3 bp** cumulative; **82.8 bp** through October 2027 (my early-morning data, before the ISM). According to FedWatch, the odds of an October hike are 18%, down from 64% a week ago. On Sunday the October hold stood at 78.4%: the NFP keeps draining urgency.
- **ECB:** December **83.3%** hike (+20.8 bp). With the stress in France, that hike is less of a sure thing than it was a week ago.
- **Rest of the G10:** BoE November **73.8%** hike; BoJ December **76.8%** hike (+19.2 bp); BoC October **65.4%** pause; RBNZ October **56.1%** hike.

The banks' views line up with the curve. For CACIB, the 29k NFP takes the urgency out of October, and its base case is a pause now and a hike in December. Deutsche Bank sees a stable labor market (household survey +406k, participation 61.8%, U-6 at 7.6%) and expects two more hikes over the next couple of quarters. Through December, the market sides with CACIB; beyond that, it already prices more than DB expects (82.8 bp through October 2027).

**Bottom line:** the dollar has little room left to run on the Fed. Its support is relative (Europe) and comes from real rates. That's why Tuesday's US data matter less than Ueda and European risk.

### Which data matter most

- **Ueda** is the event with the most surprise potential. With a 76.8% December hike priced, a clear signal toward that meeting would hit a USD/JPY that lives off the rate differential. A neutral stance leaves the pair stuck between the differential and the intervention zone (158.5–160).
- **US trade balance:** with no single consensus, direction matters more than the number. A deficit worse than −$95B plus dovish Fed speakers is the scenario that would give the euro some relief.
- **Euro-area retail sales:** coming off −0.6%. A rebound toward the expected 0.3% isn't enough to change the sovereign story. Another drop would reinforce the case for a less hawkish ECB.
- **Mann (BoE):** with a 73.8% November hike priced, a hawkish tone is the only clear upside risk for sterling.
- Wednesday's **FOMC minutes** remain the week's biggest expectations mover. On Tuesday, Ueda and Mann matter more than the trade balance.

## Scenarios for Tuesday

1. **European stress returns.** If the OAT-Bund spread pushes back above 150 bp and ECB speakers sound dovish, EUR/USD returns to 1.1160 or lower.
2. **The dollar takes a breather.** If the US trade deficit comes in worse than expected and Williams, Bowman and Logan sound dovish, the DXY corrects toward 102.0, without changing the euro's trend.
3. **Ueda opens December.** If he points to a December hike, USD/JPY drops sharply from 158. If he stays neutral, the pair stays supported by the differential, capped by the intervention zone.
