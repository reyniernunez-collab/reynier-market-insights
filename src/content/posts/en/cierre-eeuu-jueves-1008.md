> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 8, 2026, 15:30 CT:** Wall Street posts its second decline from the record, this time led by tech. Reports that OpenAI's revenue is lower than thought revive doubts about artificial-intelligence spending, and chipmakers pay the price. Bonds, by contrast, have a better afternoon: crude backs off its highs after Trump's comments on Iran and the 30-year auction goes off without a hitch.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 ends at **7,765.36** (−0.47%), its second straight losing session since Tuesday's all-time high. The Nasdaq 100 falls **1.39%** to 30,725.81, and the semiconductor index **3.4%**. The Dow Jones, with less tech weight, rises 0.10% to 51,231.64, and the Russell 2000 is practically flat (+0.03%). The VIX rises to ~**15.4**. Oil closes with another strong gain, though well off the day's highs: Brent settles at **$104.28** and WTI at **$91.49**. Bonds recover after a rough morning and the 10-year drops to ~**5.23%**. The dollar gives up some ground.

## Analysis

### Stocks: the AI bill reaches the chipmakers

The day's blow came from artificial intelligence. According to press reports, OpenAI is on track for roughly **$50 billion** in annualized revenue, below the ~$70 billion that had been discussed earlier. For a market that has justified high valuations with the promise of AI, that matters: if revenue grows more slowly, the massive spending on data centers and chips takes longer to pay off. And that spending is increasingly financed with debt, just as yields sit at multi-decade highs.

Chipmakers took the worst of it, with the sector index down 3.4%. In the morning, Samsung had already disappointed: its profit rose almost ninefold but fell short of expectations. The options market had already flagged this as the most fragile area: early this morning, the highest 30-day expected moves among large S&P 500 names were **Intel (~18.7%)**, **AMD (~15.5%)** and **Micron (~13.2%)**. For the indexes, the 30-day expected move was around **3.8%** for S&P 500 futures and **5.7%** for Nasdaq 100 futures. The rotation also shows in the Dow and the Russell 2000 ending positive or flat while the Nasdaq fell almost 1.4%.

### Oil: Trump puts a lid on it, for now

Crude kept most of the day's gain but backed off its highs. Brent came close to $106 in the morning and settled at **$104.28 (+$4.08)**, while WTI ended at **$91.49 (+$3.21)**. The brake came from the White House: Trump said the US will not strike Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, which removed part of the risk premium crude had built up on reports of strike plans. From Tehran, President Pezeshkian said Iran is not walking away from negotiations. The other two drivers are still active: attacks on tankers in Hormuz and Hurricane Isaias, which keeps forcing production shut-ins in the Gulf of Mexico ahead of landfall.

### Bonds: the 30-year auction passes the test

Bonds had two faces. In the morning, with crude surging, the 10-year Treasury reached the **5.33–5.35%** area (figures vary by source), very close to its highest since 2002. In the afternoon, with oil easing and a smooth auction, it fell to ~**5.23%** at the close. The 30-year bond auction cleared at **5.618%**, the highest yield since August 2000, just 0.1 bp above the pre-auction level. Demand was 2.54 times the amount offered and dealers took only 6.8%, a sign that end buyers showed up. It wasn't as strong as Wednesday's 10-year sale, but it did the job. The 30-year closed around 5.61%.

The St. Louis Fed's Musalem said financial conditions have tightened "modestly and in an orderly way." He added that strong demand for capital, from AI and from government deficits, may keep rates higher than in the past. He said nothing explicit about October, in line with Waller in the morning: more hikes, but no rush to deliver them at back-to-back meetings. Jobless claims (197,000 initial, 1.716 million continuing) didn't change the reading either. Markets price ~**18–19%** odds of an October hike and, for December, between ~78% and ~84% depending on the source; in some readings the December hike is already fully priced.

### Dollar and gold: afternoon relief

With yields falling, the dollar eased. The DXY ended around **102.1**, after touching ~102.47 in the morning, and EUR/USD returned to ~**1.122**, well above the ~1.1176 low of the European session. Sterling regained the 1.323 area and USD/JPY slipped to ~157.9 after touching ~157.5 around midday. Gold (futures) recovered and closed around **$4,157** (+0.4%), after a low near $4,130. In Europe, pressure on French debt, with the spread over Bunds at ~142–143 bp during the session, remains the euro's own drag.

## What I'm watching

- **Tonight, 6:30 PM CT, Japan household spending (consensus −3.6% y/y):** a weak reading would undercut the case for a Bank of Japan hike in October.
- **Tomorrow, 7:30 AM CT, Canadian jobs:** consensus is around +6,000 to +7,000 jobs depending on the source, after −41,700 the previous month. Full-time hiring matters more than the headline.
- **Tomorrow, 9:00 AM CT, preliminary Michigan:** mostly the 1-year (previous 4.6%) and 5-year (previous 3.4%) inflation expectations. Another rise would support yields and the dollar.
- **Schnabel (ECB) at 8:30 AM CT and Collins (Fed) at 3:00 PM CT.**
- **Isaias:** landfall is expected between Friday night and Saturday. Shut-in figures differ widely across sources (from ~25% to ~60% of offshore Gulf output), so any official number could move crude.
- **Iran:** after Trump's message, the immediate strike risk is lower, but attacks in Hormuz continue. A change in tone would bring the premium back to oil and the pressure back to bonds.
- **Chips and earnings season:** after Thursday's hit, the sector needs signs that AI spending is starting to generate revenue.
