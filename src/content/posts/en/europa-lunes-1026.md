> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 26, 2026, 08:17 CT:** Monday's Europe session gave the euro everything it needed: a better-than-expected German business survey and a narrower French spread. And yet EUR/USD ended at its lows. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the focus was oil easing on diplomacy and a euro breathing after the decision on France's rating. The Europe session confirmed that relief in French bonds and added a solid German print. But the move in charge is not European: it is the **dollar**, strengthening across the board with the US short end two days before the Fed.

My read: **the euro's fundamentals are improving, but the market prefers to hold dollars into Wednesday's meeting**. With almost every decision this week already priced, EUR/USD depends more on US yields than on Europe today.

## Analysis

### Germany: business mood improves

October's **Ifo** put the business climate at **90.2** (prior 89.9) and, above all, **expectations at 91.6** (prior 90.4; approx. 90.0 expected), the highest since February 2022. The current assessment worsened (88.8 vs 89.5), so this is a shift in mood rather than in activity. It is a survey, not hard data, but it takes some steam out of the stagflation story for Europe in the first month with Brent above $110. The climate consensus ranges from 89.8 to 90.0 depending on the source (⚠️), but the result is the same everywhere.

### France: the spread narrows

After the country's rating (Aa3) was kept on Friday, the French 10-year yield fell about 5 bp to approx. **4.98%**, while the German one rose about 2 bp (approx. 3.37%). The **spread between them dropped to approx. 161 bp**, with a low around 157-158 bp during the Europe session, versus approx. 166-176 bp on Friday depending on the measure. The next review of France's rating comes in late November. Stocks cheered: Paris is up approx. 0.6-0.9% and Frankfurt approx. 0.8%.

### The dollar takes over

- **EUR/USD:** approx. **1.1194**, the Europe-session low (-6/-8 pips vs the Friday New York session close), after a high near 1.122 early on.
- **Dollar:** the index rises to approx. **101.9**, the session high, with the **US 2-year at approx. 4.70%** (+1/+2 bp). The 10-year, by contrast, holds at approx. 4.99%.
- **Yen:** the weakest currency alongside the kiwi. **USD/JPY rises to approx. 157.3** (about +45/+50 pips) with no support from yields: risk appetite and softer crude are not helping it.
- **Franc:** loses its haven role with stocks higher (USD/CHF approx. 0.826).
- **Sterling:** GBP/USD gives up about 25 pips to approx. **1.335**, even though the UK retail survey jumped from -55 to **-10** (approx. -47 expected). The market is watching Wednesday's Budget, not the data.
- **Euro crosses:** EUR/GBP tagged **0.840**, which acted as a ceiling, and EUR/JPY rises to approx. 176.1. That confirms the euro is firm and the driver of EUR/USD is the dollar.
- **Antipodeans:** NZD/USD slips to approx. 0.568 ahead of the RBNZ; AUD/USD is little changed (approx. 0.703).

### Oil and energy

**December Brent** sits at approx. **$114.9** (-1.5% vs Friday's settlement) and US crude at approx. $101.1. Diplomacy is weighing: Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan meet Iran in Tehran today, after a Qatari mediation visit on Sunday. The claimed attacks on Saudi infrastructure remain officially unconfirmed. A technical note: the December contract expires at month-end and January trades about $5 cheaper, so some "below $110" figures going around refer to a different contract. Spain's producer prices (18.3% year on year) are a reminder that the energy shock is still feeding into European costs. Gold edges up (approx. $4,026) but still isn't acting as a clear haven.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Positioning Monday:** with no top-tier US data, if the 2-year holds around 4.70% or rises, the dollar would stay firm and EUR/USD would struggle to recover despite the good European news.
2. **2-year auction (12:00 PM CT):** weak demand, with the yield clearly above approx. 4.75%, would reinforce a December Fed hike and the dollar; strong demand, with the yield falling toward approx. 4.65%, would open the door to a dollar pullback.
3. **Tehran:** signs of de-escalation would take more premium out of crude (stocks up, Canadian dollar somewhat weaker); confirmed damage in Saudi Arabia or around Hormuz would do the opposite, with the euro among the most exposed.
4. **France:** a spread back above approx. 170 bp would reopen the debate over a December ECB move; further narrowing would let the euro keep its strength on the crosses.
