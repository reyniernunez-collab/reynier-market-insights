> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 29, 2026, 08:16 CT:** Thursday's Europe session had two halves. The first kept Asia's tone: a firm dollar and the euro at its daily low. In the second, the news that Lagarde will leave the ECB in February turned the euro around, and the dollar ended flat against Wednesday's New York session close. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was Alphabet cooling the AI trade and a dollar clawing back some ground. The Europe session started the same way, but **the news of the day came from the ECB itself**: Lagarde said she will leave on February 7, 2027, instead of serving out her term in October.

My read: **the market prefers knowing**. The exit removes a question mark over the bank's future and comes on meeting day, with today's decision (no change) fully priced. That's why the euro rose and so did European bonds. What stays open is December, at both the Fed and the ECB, and that hinges on PCE and on Lagarde's tone.

## Analysis

### The euro turns on the Lagarde news

- **EUR/USD:** fell to approx. **1.1124** early in Europe, the day's low, and after the announcement recovered to approx. **1.1151–1.1155**. It ends the Europe session at approx. **1.1146–1.1150**, about 6–7 pips below Wednesday's New York session close.
- **Dollar:** the index climbed to approx. **102.33** and then gave it all back to approx. **102.14**, the same level as the New York session close. The Asia session gains are gone.
- **US rates:** the 10-year ended at approx. **5.02%** and the 2-year at approx. **4.69%**, practically unchanged. The dollar fell without yields pushing it: this was a euro move, not a Fed move.
- **European bonds:** the 10-year Bund sits at approx. 3.37% and **the French spread fell to approx. 154 bp**, well below last week's nearly 178 bp. Less stress in France means less pressure on the ECB to talk about backstop tools.

### European data: soft growth, firm prices

- **Euro-area economic sentiment:** **96.9**, below expectations (97.5), with weaker services (⚠️ single-source data).
- **Selling-price expectations:** up to **24.8** from 20.8 (⚠️ same source). This is the number that backs those who want another ECB hike: companies plan to raise prices.
- **Italy and Spain:** Italian unemployment rose to 6.3% and Spanish retail sales fell 1.4% year on year.
- **UK:** mortgage approvals (approx. 53,400) and consumer credit came in below expectations. Soft UK credit, though sterling barely noticed: **GBP/USD ends the Europe session at approx. 1.3327**, almost level with the New York session close.

### The Canadian dollar leads; the antipodeans lag

**USD/CAD fell to approx. 1.3970–1.3976**, a two-day low, some 17–21 pips below the New York session close, even as oil fell. Support came from bets on another Bank of Canada hike in December, which recovered in prediction markets after collapsing overnight (⚠️ sources range from approx. 27% to 52%).

At the other end, **AUD/USD lost about 17–18 pips** to approx. 0.7035, with the tech hit still weighing and next week's RBA hike already priced. **USD/JPY** brushed approx. 158.05 with nothing new from Tokyo and hovers around approx. 157.96 ahead of the Bank of Japan.

### Hormuz and oil: two stories pulling opposite ways

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said **three tankers hit mines and caught fire** while using an "unauthorized" route through the Strait of Hormuz. Even so, oil **fell**: a US official spoke of talks on a **temporary peace deal** with Iran, though with many obstacles and the possibility of new strikes after the midterm elections if they fail. December Brent ended the Europe session at approx. **$114**, about $1.35 below Wednesday's settlement, after touching approx. $113.25.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Core PCE in line (0.3% m/m) and a non-committal Lagarde (most likely):** December bets at the Fed and the ECB would stay put and the euro would keep the morning's relief. The Canadian dollar would stay tied to oil and to the Bank of Canada speeches.
2. **Hot PCE (0.4% or more):** the December Fed odds would rise toward approx. 85–90% and the 2-year above approx. 4.75%; the dollar would regain ground and the euro would retest the morning lows, unless Lagarde sounded clearly hawkish.
3. **Soft PCE (0.2% or less):** December would slip toward approx. 65–70% and the 2-year below approx. 4.62%; the dollar would lose support, especially if Lagarde keeps a December hike on the table.
4. **Lagarde focused on growth or France:** ECB December odds could fall below 50% and the euro would give back its rebound even with an in-line PCE.
5. **Escalation in Hormuz:** more damaged ships or the end of the diplomatic track would push Brent back above approx. $116, with the Canadian dollar benefiting and the euro and yen among the most exposed.
