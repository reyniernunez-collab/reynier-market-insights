> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 15, 2026, 08:19 CT:** The Europe session did not ease the chip selloff or return the dollar to the CPI impulse. Treasuries marked a three-week low, Brent stayed lateral-bearish and AUD pierced Asia's floor. This post closes the Europe-session landscape as New York opens: what was priced, how the AI risk-off transmitted, and the conditional scenarios around PPI.

## Executive Summary

In our Asia session post, Seoul and Tokyo sank with chips (Kospi approx. −7.5/−7.6%, Nikkei approx. −4.9%), Treasuries rallied, DXY touched a low near **101.88** and Brent lost the escalation branch. The Europe session **consolidates that dollar weakness without much extension** and adds its own damage in European equities and in the AUD.

The Stoxx 600 closes approx. **−0.26%**, with tech **−2.7%** (worst day since July); ASML and Infineon fall hard. DAX approx. −0.48%; FTSE **+0.55%**; CAC **+0.15%**. US futures arrive into the New York open soft: Nasdaq 100 approx. **−1.1/−1.2%** and S&P approx. **−0.2/−0.3%**.

In FX, DXY sits around **102.03**; EUR/USD trades a tight range approx. **1.1203–1.1229**, with hourly closes approx. **1.1210–1.1217** and none above 1.1240. GBP/USD marked a day high near **1.3275**. AUD/USD **broke** Asia's low to approx. **0.6911–0.6917**. The Swiss franc remains the strongest major.

The core thesis does not change: the dollar gave back Wednesday's hot CPI **through the bond channel** (haven demand into the AI/chip selloff and softer oil), **not** because the market cut October Fed odds. That meeting stays around **27–30%** hike (convergent readings). December, with at least one hike, looks even firmer on one cumulative metric (approx. **93%**) versus per-meeting readings near **81–82%** — they are not 1:1.

## Analysis

### Chips and equities: Europe pays the AI bill

What began in New York on Wednesday and widened in Asia did not stop in Europe. The Stoxx 600 closes approx. **−0.26%**, but European tech falls approx. **2.7%** — the worst day since July. ASML and Infineon rank among the hardest-hit names. The DAX slips approx. **0.48%**; the FTSE, by contrast, advances approx. **0.55%** and the CAC approx. **0.15%**.

Into the New York open, Nasdaq 100 futures trade approx. **−1.1/−1.2%** and S&P futures approx. **−0.2/−0.3%**. Semiconductor pressure continues in the US premarket. The session's doubt is not whether chip demand exists, but **how much of that investment translates into returns** when real rates and valuations tighten — the same read that already marked Asia.

### Treasuries and the dollar: consolidation, not a reclaim

From Wednesday's New York close (10-year at **5.19%**), the US 10-year moves toward approx. **5.08–5.085%** (intraday low near **5.075%**; three-week low) and the 2-year toward approx. **4.74%**. DXY, after Asia's low near **101.88**, stabilizes in Europe around **102.03** — soft versus the New York close (approx. 102.54), but **without extending** Asia's floor.

Orientative levels toward the end of the Europe session (approx. 6:30 AM CT):

- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1217–1.1220** (Europe range approx. 1.1203–1.1229; 1h closes approx. 1.1210–1.1217; none >1.1240).
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3255–1.3256** (day high near 1.3275).
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.6918–0.6921** (Europe low approx. 0.6911).
- **USD/JPY** approx. **157.83–157.86**.
- **USD/CHF** approx. **0.8299–0.8300** (low near 0.8279): the franc remains the strongest major.
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.4195** (low near 1.4189–1.4190).

Important: **October Fed hike odds did not unwind**. Main readings converge around **27–30%**. The yield move is **term premium / haven**, not an aggressive repricing of the Oct 28 meeting. That is why the dollar turn is **fragile** into the 7:30 CT data: because October did not fall, a firm PPI does not need to “undo” a dovish repricing; yields turning higher is enough for the USD to reclaim the giveback.

### Brent: lateral-bearish; the Gulf does not move price

The escalation branch was already invalidated in the Asia session (1h closes below $105.5 and then below $104.72). In Europe the regime is confirmed:

- Session low near **$103.37**.
- Rebound toward approx. **$104.5–104.6** (Europe high near $104.76).
- **No 1h close ≥$105.5**.

Price keeps **ignoring** Gulf tension headlines; the supply narrative and the report that the US is weighing easing sanctions on ghost-fleet vessels dominate. The official EIA print arrives at **11:00 AM CT**.

### AUD, European production and other central banks

The **AUD was already the downside exception** in Asia after September jobs (−39.3k; unemployment 4.7%). In Europe it **extends**: it breaks Asia's low to approx. **0.6911–0.6917**. RBA November 3 hike odds, anchored in Australian futures, sit at approx. **16%** ⚠️: other readings stay around 23–28% and should not be mixed with the futures anchor.

**Euro-area industrial production** for August printed very weak: **−0.5%** month on month / **−1.1%** year on year versus a flat consensus. UK August GDP (+0.1% vs −0.1%) still supports a BoE November hike (approx. **90–91%**).

Other central banks (indicative readings; flag conflicts):

- **Fed Oct 28:** approx. **27–30%** hike ✅. December: at least one hike even firmer on one cumulative metric (approx. **93%**) vs per-meeting readings near **81–82%** — not 1:1.
- **BoE Nov 5:** approx. **90–91%** ✅.
- **BoC Oct 28:** prefer approx. **19–20%** ⚠️ (another series sits near 35%).
- **ECB Oct 29:** hold dominates; hike around approx. **6–7%** on the lower readings ⚠️ (another sits near 12%).
- **RBNZ Oct 28:** approx. **54%** ⚠️ (another series higher).
- **BoJ October:** clear hold (approx. **4–5%** hike).

December gold futures rise with bonds (zone approx. **$4,178–4,180** versus New York settle near $4,108). Do not mix that print with spot readings from other windows.

### Conditional scenarios into New York (without inventing the print)

The day's filter is **PPI and retail sales at 7:30 AM CT**. Orientative consensus: headline PPI approx. **0.5–0.6%** m/m ⚠️ (calendars differ); core **0.3%**. Retail sales approx. **0.3%**. What matters for the Fed is **core PPI** and, in sales, the control group. Conditionals on the table into the New York open:

1. **Firm core PPI (≥0.4% with retail control ≥0.5%, or a very hot headline with firm core):** October toward approx. **35–45%**; yields can give back the overnight drop and the dollar reclaim what Asia/Europe gave back (DXY toward 102.3–102.5; EUR/USD more exposed to 1.116–1.118).
2. **In line (core 0.3%, control 0.2–0.4%):** October stays near **27–30%**; the driver returns to risk (chips/AI) and bonds; FX more range-bound.
3. **Soft (core ≤0.2% with weak control):** October toward approx. **20%** (near the pre-CPI level); the dollar can extend softness and EUR/USD can point higher only via the USD side.

Parallel oil branch: the regime stays **lateral-bearish** until a 1h close ≥$105.5. A 1h close below Europe's low (approx. $103.37) would open a downside extension.
