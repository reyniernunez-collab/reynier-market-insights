> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 19, 2026, 01:54 CT:** The week opens with a scare in Asia that gradually faded. Doubts about how AI spending is being financed sank the open, but by the Asia session close much of the region was in the green, US futures were recovering and oil was shedding premium on a supply headline.

## Executive Summary

On Friday, the New York close left a deeper tech selloff, with the semiconductor index extending its longest losing streak since 2008. Monday's Asia session started along the same lines and then turned: the Kospi went from a drop of more than 3% at the open to a positive close, while Nasdaq 100 futures are up approx. **1.4%** and S&P 500 futures approx. **0.8%**.

The most relevant shift is in **oil**. Brent loses approx. **2.4%** and breaks Sunday's opening low, not because Hormuz has calmed down, but because the White House on Friday suspended Russia-linked sanctions and tariffs, which the market reads as more Russian fuel exports.

**China** helped the mood with better-than-expected activity data, and the commodity currency bloc (AUD, NZD and, surprisingly, CAD) strengthened against the dollar. The euro, by contrast, remains weighed down by France.

## Analysis

### Oil: the premium gives way on the Russia side

Brent moves from Friday's **$109.86** settle in the New York session to approx. **$107.2–107.5**, below Sunday's opening low (approx. $108.6). WTI falls below **$100** (approx. $99.3; quotes differ by contract).

The cause is the order signed late on Friday that suspends several Russia-related sanctions and tariffs before they took effect. There is an important caveat: it was later reported that the final decision has not been made and that there is a window of up to 60 days to assess its impact (single-origin information). If the measure is reversed, part of this drop could unwind.

In Hormuz, Iran reported another tanker damaged by a mine (not independently verified) and price ignored it, just as at Sunday's open. Today the market is reacting more to a supply headline than to each Gulf incident. The bias moves from event-driven bullish to **sideways-to-lower, with a tail premium**: confirmed damage in the Gulf or a reversal of the Russia measure would push it back up.

The negative correlation between crude and US indices worked overnight: oil down, Nasdaq up.

### China: better activity, property still deep in the hole

- **Q3 GDP:** 4.5% year on year (4.4% expected).
- **Retail sales:** 1.1% (approx. 0.5% expected).
- **Industrial production:** 5.5%; **unemployment:** down to 5.2%.
- **Property:** investment is still falling sharply, around 20% year on year so far this year (single-source figure).

The yuan was fixed at its strongest level since February 2023. AUD/USD rose to approx. **0.6975** (approx. +28 pips versus Friday's New York close), but stayed well short of 0.7000: property caps the enthusiasm.

### Stocks and bonds: from scare to recovery

At the Asia session close:

- **Nikkei:** −0.6%, after falling as much as approx. 2.3%.
- **Kospi:** +1.6%, after an open near −3% (the opening print may not be representative).
- **Shanghai:** +0.6%; **Australia:** +0.4%; **Taiwan:** −0.6%.
- **Hong Kong** did not trade, apparently for a holiday.

The US 10-year yield is down approx. **3 bp** at approx. **5.07%**. Firmer bonds and rising futures tell the same story: the market is buying risk again, although doubts about AI financing remain ahead of a week with major tech earnings.

### FX: the CAD gets ahead of its CPI

Indicative readings versus Friday's New York close:

- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.4100** (from 1.4137). The Canadian dollar rises even with crude falling; there is no local headline explaining it, so it looks like a commodity-bloc move after China and perhaps some positioning ahead of the data.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.6975** and **NZD/USD** approx. **0.5659**, both higher.
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1222–1.1226**, about 17–18 pips below Friday and inside the overnight range (1.1184–1.1237).
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3327**, flat, ahead of Tuesday's UK jobs data.
- **USD/JPY** approx. **158.0**, no net change.
- **DXY** approx. **101.84–101.87**, flat.

Gold futures trade near approx. **$4,196** (don't mix that with spot readings, which sit lower).

### Japan: Ueda insists, no timetable

The BoJ governor repeated that underlying inflation is close to 2% and warned that if the short-term rate isn't adjusted in time, long-term yields could rise. He gave no timing hints, and October hike odds stay low at approx. **3–5%**.

### Central banks: what is priced

- **Fed (Oct 28):** hike odds approx. **12–13%**, about 2 points lower (the main readings converge). December remains the central meeting, at approx. 77–78% on the most stable reading (another series shows higher figures).
- **BoC (Oct 28):** the most uncertain meeting in the G10; market readings disagree (approx. 16–32%) and today's CPI is the key piece.
- **BoE (Nov 5):** a hike is very likely, approx. **86–94%** depending on the reading.
- **RBNZ (Oct 28):** hike odds approx. **71–81%** (range across sources).
- **RBA (Nov 3):** approx. **17–22%**.
- **ECB (Oct 29)** and **BoJ (Oct 30):** a hold dominates.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Canada's core CPI measures** (median and trim, consensus 2.0% and 1.9%) come in above forecast, the BoC meeting would move closer to a hike and the CAD would get extra support; if they come in line or below, part of tonight's CAD gain could unwind. The headline will be inflated by gasoline, so the core measures matter more.
- **If the French risk premium widens** in the Europe session, the euro would stay the weak leg of the map; if it calms, EUR/USD would have room to close the gap to Friday.
- **If Brent holds below approx. $108.6**, the "deflating premium" reading gains strength; a move back above approx. $108.6–109.6 would signal the market is paying for Gulf risk again or doubting the Russia measure.
- **If the Nasdaq futures rebound holds** into the New York open, the Asia scare will look like an episode; if it fades, doubts about AI financing return to center stage ahead of earnings.
