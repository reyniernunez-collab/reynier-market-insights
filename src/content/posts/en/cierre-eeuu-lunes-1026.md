> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 26, 2026, 3:39 PM CT:** Last week the market doubted the bill for artificial intelligence. This Monday, Anthropic's IPO filing brought the appetite back: chips pulled Wall Street higher, oil eased on diplomacy in Tehran, and the dollar stayed firm two days before the Fed. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 rose **0.7%** to **7,676.77**, the Nasdaq Composite **0.8%** to **26,169.38** and the Dow Jones **0.3%** to **52,361.58**. The S&P broke a two-session losing streak, with tech in front and a calmer volatility reading (the VIX eased to the **18.4** area).

The catalyst was Anthropic's public IPO filing; the company plans to list on the Nasdaq. For a market that had spent two weeks questioning the cost of sustaining the AI bet, the deal works as proof that investor appetite is still there. At the same time, the AI bill hasn't gone away: Meta unveiled a large equity financing plan and Microsoft opened a voluntary exit program to contain costs.

Outside equities, oil eased on regional talks in Tehran and the dollar strengthened across the board, supported by the front end of the curve ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision.

## Analysis

### Stocks: AI leads again, but with its bill in plain sight

The semiconductor index rose approx. **2.8%** and the Nasdaq 100 approx. **1.1%**. The big chip names advanced strongly, a sharp contrast with last week's selloff. The market's message is clear: demand for AI investment is alive, and an IPO of this size will be the gauge of sentiment toward the sector in the coming weeks.

But the same day brought two reminders of what that race costs. Meta announced a plan to raise up to approx. **$95 billion** (part through immediate offerings of common and preferred stock, the rest through an at-the-market program planned for later) to fund its AI infrastructure, one of the largest equity raises on record. Microsoft, for its part, offered voluntary buyouts to a pool of approx. **60,000** employees to trim expenses. Dilution and cuts: two different ways to pay for the same investment.

That tension is what this week's earnings will test: Alphabet and Microsoft on Wednesday, and Amazon on Thursday, after the New York close. Their data-center spending and what they say about funding it will decide whether today's rebound has legs.

### Oil: diplomacy takes off some premium

December Brent fell **$0.91** to settle at **$115.81**, while WTI dropped **$1.51** to **$101.76**. It was a choppy session: crude rose at times before giving way, and what tipped the balance was the meeting in Tehran between representatives of Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan and Iran's leaders, framed as an effort to ease regional tensions.

The risk remains open: the attacks claimed against Saudi infrastructure over the weekend have no official confirmation, and the message from Washington is still that decision time is approaching between a deal with Iran or more pressure. ⚠️ Mind the contract: December Brent expires this week and January trades approx. $5 lower, so price references can differ a lot depending on the contract quoted.

### Bonds and the dollar: the front end props up the dollar

The Treasury sold **$69 billion** of 2-year notes at **4.708%**, slightly above the pre-auction level (a **0.3 bp** tail) and with somewhat softer demand than usual (bid-to-cover **2.59** versus **2.63** last time). Not a bad auction, but it gave the front end no reason to rally: the 2-year ended at approx. **4.71–4.72%** (⚠️ readings differ by timing), about 3 bp above Friday's New York close. The 10-year was practically flat at approx. **4.99%**.

That let the dollar prevail: the dollar index rose approx. **0.2%** to the **102** area, EUR/USD slipped to approx. **1.117–1.118**, GBP/USD to approx. **1.334** and USD/JPY climbed to approx. **157.5**. The yen stays weak without yields explaining it. Gold rose approx. **0.5%** to the **$4,027** area (December futures).

### Europe

The Europe session brought the euro good news of its own (the German business survey with expectations at their highest since 2022, and a narrower French risk spread after France kept its rating), but it was not enough against the dollar. The New York session confirmed that read: the euro is improving underneath, but the dollar is in charge ahead of the Fed.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If Australian inflation (Tuesday night) comes in strong on its underlying measure:** the RBA's November meeting, currently the least settled, would move close to a done deal and the Australian dollar would gain support; a soft print would put that meeting back in doubt.
- **If the US 2-year settles above approx. 4.75% before the Fed:** the market would lean harder into a December hike and the dollar would stay firm; a move back below approx. 4.62% would signal the December bet is cooling.
- **If the Tehran talks make progress:** less premium in crude, supported stocks and some relief for the euro on the crosses. If damage to Saudi infrastructure or around Hormuz is confirmed, oil would become the driver again and pressure on inflation expectations would return.
- **If big-tech earnings disappoint on spending or funding:** today's enthusiasm could reverse quickly, as it did last week, with chips back at the center of the selling.
