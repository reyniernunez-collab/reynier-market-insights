> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 8, 2026, 10:30 CT:** Europe closes Thursday with oil back in the lead. Brent rises close to 5% on a mix of war risk with Iran, attacks on tankers and a hurricane in the Gulf of Mexico. With crude moving like that, yields rise, France crosses the 140 bp threshold again and European stocks close in the red. The euro came close to its 17-month low but recovered before the close.

## Executive Summary

The Europe session was an oil day. Brent went from Wednesday's $100.20 settlement to the **$105–106** area, and WTI moved toward ~$92–93. There were three drivers: reports that Washington is weighing strikes on Iran before the midterm elections, a record week of tanker attacks in Hormuz, and Hurricane Isaias, which has shut a quarter of offshore output in the Gulf of Mexico. Pricier crude dragged bonds down: the US 10-year touched the 5.33–5.35% area and French debt came under pressure again, with the spread over Bunds at ~142–143 bp. The Stoxx 600 closed down **0.75%**, the DAX 1.2% and the CAC 40 0.5%, with banks the hardest-hit sector. In currencies, the dollar rose in the morning and then gave some of it back: the euro touched ~1.1176 and ended the European session around 1.120.

## Analysis

### Oil: three drivers at once

The jump in crude doesn't have a single cause. The first is geopolitical. According to press reports, the US is weighing a return to large-scale operations against Iran, or a strike, before the November elections. That contradicts the idea that the White House would wait until after the vote. The second is shipping: attacks on tankers in Hormuz hit their highest weekly count since the war began (between 9 and 12 in the week to October 5, depending on who is counting). The third is physical: Isaias is now a hurricane and producers have shut in about **511,000 barrels a day**, roughly **25%** of offshore Gulf output, with landfall expected between Friday night and Saturday. One industry model estimates up to 11.2 million barrels could be lost. On top of that, the UK sanctioned Russian oil companies and tankers.

A useful reference is the 30-day expected move priced by Brent options, around **13%**. Translated to a single day, that's roughly ±$3. On Thursday Brent traveled more than $5 from the prior settlement, almost twice a normal day. Options came up short, which leaves the price exposed to quick pullbacks if any headline cools the Iran risk. The underlying brake is still the IEA's accelerated reserve release, although it's unclear how much of what was pledged in March has actually reached the market.

### France: the spread tops 140 bp

The French 10-year yield traded in the **4.91–4.94%** area, close to the 24-year high it touched last Friday, while the Bund hovered around 3.50–3.52%. The spread sits at **~142–143 bp**, above the 140 bp we had been flagging as the threshold. The damage showed up in banks: the Stoxx 600 banks index fell as much as 2.2%, on track for its worst two-day run since March. One fact sums up the distrust: more than a third of French corporate bonds trade as if they were safer than the government's own debt. Part of the euro's problem isn't dollar strength but French politics.

### ECB and BoE: two different tones

The ECB's September meeting accounts came out neutral. The council preferred not to signal a rate path, judged that 2.50% is still within staff's estimated neutral range, and saw risks in both directions. Lane described a "medium-sized" shock that calls for a moderate response, while warning that core inflation will rise mostly next year. Other members, such as Greece's central bank governor, insisted that inflation is coming from energy and see no second-round effects. The result: the odds of an ECB hike in December fall to **~73–76%**, from ~80% on Wednesday.

At the Bank of England the tone is tougher. Greene said it's dangerous to rely on markets to do the central bank's job, and Pill called for keeping the focus on inflation. Both already voted to hike, so the question was Bailey. His speech focused on financial resilience and on the need for governments to maintain fiscal credibility, without a clear signal on November. Sterling lost 1.3200 in the morning, touched ~1.3185 (just above its three-month low) and then returned to ~1.3215. Be careful with November pricing: it ranges from **81% to 96%** depending on the source, too wide a spread to read as an exact figure.

### Fed and bonds: Waller doesn't change the script

Waller was the first event of the day. He backed more hikes if the data come in as expected, but made clear they don't have to come at consecutive meetings. Markets read it as confirmation of what was already priced: October stays at ~**18–19%** and December between ~78% and ~84% depending on the source (the gap is methodological). Jobless claims didn't move pricing either: **197,000** initial claims versus 200,000 expected, and 1.716 million continuing claims, slightly above forecast. The labor market remains in "low firing, low hiring" mode.

The US 10-year touched the **5.33–5.35%** area early on (figures vary by source and time), very close to Wednesday's 5.36%, the highest since 2002. By the European close it had eased to ~5.30%. In our data, the 30-day correlation between crude and the 10-year is **0.99**: today oil sets the pace for bonds.

### Currencies and stocks: the dollar rises, then eases

The DXY reached ~102.47 in the morning, near its 18-month high, and at the Europe close it was around 102.26. EUR/USD touched **~1.1176** around 6:30 AM CT, very close to the 17-month low of 1.1160, and came back to ~1.1203. USD/JPY held around 158.3, below the 158.5 zone where intervention risk rises. Gold stayed around $4,140 (futures), getting no safe-haven bid with yields this high. German data went largely unnoticed: August exports fell 0.8% when a rise was expected.

In equities, Samsung's profit rose almost ninefold but fell short of what the market expected, and the stock dropped 2.4%. TSMC's 51% sales jump didn't lift chipmakers either. With yields at multi-decade highs and earnings season about to start, markets are asking whether profits justify valuations, especially at companies borrowing to invest in artificial intelligence.

## What I'm watching today

- **12:00 PM CT, 30-year bond auction (previous ~5.31%):** the 30-year trades near 24-year highs. Weak demand could push the 10-year to a new high since 2002; a solid auction, like Wednesday's 10-year, would bring relief to bonds and the euro.
- **12:40 PM CT, Musalem (Fed):** he would only move October pricing (~18–19%) by talking about that meeting explicitly, against Waller's message.
- **Iran and Hormuz:** a White House denial or a shift in Isaias's track could trigger profit-taking in crude, which has already blown well past its normal daily range.
- **OAT-Bund spread:** above ~150 bp, the euro would look at its 17-month low again; below ~135 bp, it would have room to recover.
- **Tomorrow (Friday):** Canadian jobs at 7:30 AM CT (consensus varies across sources, around +6,000 to +7,000 jobs), Schnabel (ECB) at 8:30 AM, Michigan sentiment and its inflation expectations at 9:00 AM, and Collins (Fed) at 3:00 PM CT.
