> **Dallas, Texas — Sept 24, 2026:** The global bond selloff kept going: strong US economic data and soft demand at the five-year Treasury auction pushed yields higher. The US 10-year held near **5.11%** in Asian trading after Wednesday's **15-basis-point jump**, the largest since the turmoil that followed Trump's April 2025 tariff announcement.

## Executive Summary

The bond selloff deepened. The US 10-year at **5.11%** after +15 bp — the biggest jump since Trump's tariff turmoil in April 2025. The pressure spread across Asia Pacific: Japan, Australia and New Zealand, while EM also weakened. The average global government bond yield is near **4%** as traders add bets on more Fed tightening after last week's first hike since 2023.

Oil eased after its recent rally: Brent **~102** (−0.7%), offering some relief on energy costs. Asian equities under pressure **−0.6%**, European futures pointing to more losses.

Gold lower after falling **1.7%** to **~4,290** as higher rates reduce the appeal of non-yielding assets. Mainland China **−1%** even after Bessent said the US and China agreed to extend the trade truce by 2 months.

China's Xi arrived in Washington for his first state visit to the US in 11 years, while diesel futures rose as the Trump administration works with refiners on a voluntary export curb.

## Detailed Analysis

### Yields and rates

- **US 10Y 5.11%** — held in Asian trading after Wednesday's surge
- **US 5Y auction** — weak demand, low bid-to-cover
- **Fed** — effective 3.75–4.00%, but the market prices more hikes after the first hike since 2023
- **Global avg yield ~4%** — near the highs
- **JGB, ACGB, NZGB** — all sliding

### Oil and energy

Brent down 0.7% to ~102 after its recent rally. Recent path: 99.35 (broke 100) → 98.34 (US-Iran talks) → 102 (rebound on diesel curbs). It offers partial relief on energy costs but doesn't reverse the hawkish Fed narrative.

Diesel futures higher on talks between the Trump administration and refiners for a voluntary export curb — concern about overseas shipments.

### Equities and sentiment

Asia −0.6%, European futures lower, mainland China −1% despite the 2-month truce extension. Gold 4,290 (−1.7%). The risk-off in bonds hasn't fully passed through to stocks yet — a divergence that may close.
