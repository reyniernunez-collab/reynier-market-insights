> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 13, 2026, 3:37 PM CT:** Wall Street spent the morning under the weight of the tech selloff that hit Asia and ended the afternoon unscathed thanks to the Fed. Two of its most closely followed voices made it clear that if rates have to rise again, it won't be the start of a long cycle. Bonds rallied, the dollar gave ground and oil, oddly enough, climbed back to the $106 area without spooking the market.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the index doesn't tell the whole story. The S&P 500 finished at **7,805.45** (−0.02%), but most of its constituents rose; the Nasdaq closed at **27,204.38** (−0.15%) and the Dow at **51,670.10** (−0.35%). The afternoon recovery came from semiconductors: the SOX was down about **3%** at one point in the session ⚠️ (intraday low from a single source) and ended just **−0.23%**. Microsoft hit a record high. The VIX barely moved (~15.3).

The Fed provided the turn. John Williams, president of the New York Fed, left the door open to one more hike this year but said he doesn't see further increases being needed in 2027. Governor Christopher Waller underlined that long-term yields are already making credit more expensive and weighing on sectors such as housing and autos, which could reduce the need for more tightening. For a market that had spent days fearing an open-ended hiking cycle, it was a relief.

## Analysis

### Stocks: from the Asia session selloff to a close without damage

The day started with the same problem as Monday. In the Asia session the AI-linked selloff hit Korean memory makers hard, and Nasdaq futures reached New York below Monday's close. In the Europe session, by contrast, stocks rose about 0.6% on cheaper oil, while European chipmakers also suffered.

What matters for the week is the afternoon reaction: semiconductors found buyers after five losing sessions and the index recovered from its lows. The big banks opened earnings season with a mixed message (strong in equities trading, softer in fixed income, according to the first headline figures ⚠️ not yet checked against the official reports). With the S&P 500 a step away from records and tech still fragile, TSMC on Thursday remains the sector's real test.

### Rates and the dollar: the Fed caps the fear

The 10-year, which didn't trade in cash on Monday because of the holiday, closed at **5.217%** (−2.7 bp versus Friday's 5.244%; day low ~5.19%). Lower yields fed through to the dollar: the DXY fell to ~**102.2** (−0.37%) and EUR/USD rose into the **1.1197–1.1201** area, about 40 pips above Monday's New York close. USD/JPY stayed around **158.3**, well away from the 160 zone that makes Tokyo uneasy. USD/CAD fell to ~1.422 ⚠️ (single source), with CAD supported by pricier oil. Sterling rose to ~1.3255 ⚠️ (single source). Gold finished around **$4,181** (−0.2%): no haven role.

On probabilities, the latest available reading (from this morning) put the Fed's October 28 meeting at roughly **80% hold / 20% hike**, with December at ~**88%** for at least one cumulative hike ⚠️ (methodologies differ across sources). Williams' message fits that split: one more hike is possible, a series isn't. The risk therefore still sits in December, not October.

### Oil: back at the threshold, but without dragging the dollar

The oil story flipped during the day. Through the Asia and Europe sessions Brent lost ground to a low of ~**$104.4**, below Friday's settlement. From mid-morning in New York it reclaimed **$105.5** and held above it all afternoon, finishing near **$106.5** (late futures price, not the official settlement). WTI rose to ~**$94.9**, more than $1 above Monday's settlement.

The striking part is the disconnect: on Monday high oil pushed the dollar up; today the Fed weighed more. If Brent holds in this zone, the question for CPI isn't September, which is already expected to run hot on gasoline, but how much of this oil price passes through to October and November.

### Scenarios for tomorrow's CPI

- **Core in line (+0.2% m/m) with an energy-driven hot headline:** validates today's Fed message; October stays around 20% and the dollar depends more on day-to-day oil.
- **Core at +0.4% or more (or +0.3% with hot services):** would reopen October; today's relief in bonds and the dollar's weakness would reverse quickly.
- **Core at +0.1% or less:** October would move toward ~10%; the euro would only have room to run if oil also doesn't reaccelerate.
- **Oil:** if Brent holds above $105.5 into the release, energy inflation risk returns to center stage; if it drops back below ~$104.7, the Europe session pattern would repeat.

Options markets price a daily range of about **±47 pips** in EUR/USD and **±$3** in Brent (derived from this morning's 30-day implied volatility).
