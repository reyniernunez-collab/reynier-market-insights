> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 6, 2026, 12:45 AM CT:** The Nasdaq 100's record keeps the mood upbeat, but Asia barely moves. What does move is the curve: Treasuries slip again and the 10-year is now trading in 2002 territory. Stocks are shrugging off oil near $100 and yields this high, and that coexistence is the story of the day.

## Executive Summary

The global tone stays positive. After Wall Street's tech rally (Nasdaq 100 at a record, the S&P 500 within **0.5%** of its high on gains in Nvidia and Microsoft), the Asia-Pacific index adds just ~**0.1%** and remains about 2% below its record; the global index is less than **1.5%** from its August peak. Bonds go the other way: the US 10-year rises to **5.32%** and the 2-year to **4.83%**, with Japanese, Australian and New Zealand debt following them lower. Gold eases to ~**$4,120**, Brent bounces to ~**$100.90** and the euro stays near 17-month lows.

## Analysis

### Stocks: near the high, with Asia lagging

Leadership is still US tech. Asia follows half-heartedly (+0.1%), leaving the region roughly 2% below its record. European and US futures point to further gains. The trader's read: equities are ignoring, for now, the double headwind of high yields and expensive crude. While that holds, dips get bought; if yields keep climbing unchecked, that tolerance is the first thing to be tested.

### Bonds: the 10-year in 2002 territory

Treasuries extend their slide. The 10-year adds ~1 bp to **5.32%** and the 2-year ~2 bps to **4.83%**. The move is broad: Japanese, Australian and New Zealand bonds also weaken. With the short end rising more than the long end, the market still seems to be repricing Fed expectations, not just term premium. That's the thing to track heading into Wednesday's FOMC minutes.

### Commodities: gold eases, Brent bounces

Gold falls ~0.4% to around **$4,120** an ounce, with rising yields as a drag. Brent gains ~0.6% to ~**$100.90**, recovering part of Monday's drop and sitting right on the $100 line again.

### Euro and France: fiscal pressure unresolved

The euro remains near its lowest level in 17 months, weighed by rising borrowing costs and doubts over France's public finances. The political focus is on Marine Le Pen and her party, who are set to present alternative budget proposals along with deficit-reduction plans. Any sign of looser fiscal discipline tends to hit French debt and, with it, the euro.

## What I'm watching today

- **Ueda (BoJ), 1:35 AM CT:** with Japanese bonds falling alongside Treasuries, any hint about the next hike moves USD/JPY.
- **Treasuries:** whether the 10-year holds above 5.30% or turns; a higher close would test the optimism in stocks.
- **Euro and France:** how EUR/USD and the French spread react to Le Pen's budget proposals.
- **Data (CT):** euro-area retail sales at 4:00 AM and US and Canadian trade balances at 7:30 AM. A light calendar: markets are looking for a catalyst.
