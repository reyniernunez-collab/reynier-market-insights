> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 18, 2026, 15:50 CT:** This is a week with no G10 central-bank decisions and the Fed in its blackout period (Saturday October 17 through Thursday October 29). With no Fed voices to validate or push back on what is priced, the dollar loses its own engine and the market moves to the crosses. What gets tested is whether inflation outside the US — Canada on Monday, the UK on Tuesday and Wednesday, New Zealand on Wednesday night and Japan on Thursday night — confirms or unwinds what is priced one week before the October 28–30 super-block (Fed, BoC, RBNZ, ECB, BoJ and the UK Budget). On top of that, France adds a new risk for the euro, and oil remains the thread running through everything.

## How we got here: what is priced

At the New York close on Friday October 16, the DXY finished at **101.78–101.84** (approx. −0.4% on the week) and EUR/USD at **1.1239–1.1244**, about 40 pips above the prior Friday. USD/CAD was the big mover: **1.4137**, approx. 120 pips lower on the week, with CAD supported by oil and a soft dollar. The US 10-year closed at **5.08–5.10%**, its biggest weekly rally since February (approx. −15 bp). Gold ended at **$4,181.1** (−0.8%) and the VIX near **20**.

The week had a bit of everything: CPI with hot core (0.3% m/m, 2.6% y/y), a strong PPI (6.3% y/y) and Friday import prices at **+1.1% m/m / 8.1% y/y**. Even so, after Warsh avoided committing to what he will do for the rest of 2026, Fed October hike odds deflated from approx. 30% to approx. 15%, and that is where the week ended. Geopolitics returned on Friday (strikes in Saudi Arabia, Russian refineries) and the tech selloff deepened: the Nasdaq 100 fell **−2.5%** and semiconductors logged eight straight down sessions, their worst streak since 2008, with CoreWeave again at the center of doubts about AI financing.

Brent settled at **$109.86** (+4.9% on the week) and WTI between **$99 and $101.4** depending on the cut ⚠️ (approx. +8–10% on the week). In Europe, the OAT–Bund spread closed Friday’s Europe session at **137.4 bp** (+4.9 bp on the week).

## The weekend: France, Hormuz and the Asia open

1. **France (a new risk for the euro).** Saturday’s cost-of-living protests turned into looting and arson. Arrest counts range from approx. 1,800 to 2,397 depending on the source ⚠️, with nearly 1,000 officers injured according to the Interior Ministry; turnout has no official figure ⚠️. On Sunday (13:00 CT) Macron spoke for about ten minutes: firmness against rioters, powers for prefects to ban gatherings and a crisis cell from Monday. **There were no fiscal announcements**, so the channel to markets is the political risk premium, six months before the presidential election (with polls showing Le Pen ahead, a figure we could not cross-check ⚠️). Sunday’s indicative quotes put EUR/USD at approx. **1.119–1.120**, about 40–50 pips below Friday’s New York close, but on weekend liquidity ⚠️.
   - If the OAT–Bund spread moves above approx. **140–145 bp** in Monday’s Europe session, the euro will most likely validate that gap. If it stays at 137 bp or below and the streets calm down, the gap would tend to close partially.
2. **Iran and Hormuz.** Tehran keeps its offer to reopen the strait in approx. six days if the US meets conditions (an end to the naval blockade and sanctions relief); Washington has not accepted. Separately, the Revolutionary Guard says two supertankers caught fire after hitting mines, which has no independent verification and which US Central Command does not acknowledge ⚠️. A projectile in Najran (Saudi Arabia) injured six on Sunday, and Russia’s foreign minister visits Tehran on Monday. **Net:** conditional de-escalation versus ongoing incidents; the Brent premium should not vanish at the open. A US “yes” would be the biggest oil-downside scenario (CAD and NOK weaker; JPY and EUR helped through terms of trade).
3. **Russia–Ukraine.** Drones over St. Petersburg and air alerts in Lithuania and Latvia over drone debris. Marginal risk of safe-haven demand and a weaker euro if it escalates on NATO’s flank.
4. **AI credit.** The focus has moved from valuations to debt financing of data centers (approx. one third of the sector’s capex is said to be debt-funded, a single-source figure ⚠️). If the stress spreads, it becomes a financial-conditions story: JPY and CHF up, yields down. It is the most likely channel for a weaker dollar in a week without the Fed.
5. **China (tonight, already Monday 19 in Beijing).** Q3 GDP at 21:00 CT Sunday: consensus **4.4% y/y** (prior 4.3%) and 1.0–1.1% q/q ⚠️; industrial output 5.4%, retail sales 0.5%, year-to-date fixed investment −7.3%, unemployment 5.3%. For AUD, retail sales and investment matter more than the headline: GDP at 4.5% or higher with retail sales near 1% would support AUD; retail at 0% or lower with investment worse than −7.5% would weigh on it along with risk appetite in Asia.

## Canada: the CPI that can move expectations the most

Gasoline will explain the headline; the BoC watches the **median, the trim and their three-month annualized pace**, which already rose in August. Macklem said on Friday that the Bank has looked through the energy shock, but the longer it lasts, the harder that gets. With OIS at approx. 32–35% and prediction markets at approx. 15–19% ⚠️, this print should force one of the two readings to converge toward the other.

- **Hawkish scenario:** median at 2.2% or higher, trim at 2.1% or higher, or a clearly rising three-month pace with headline at 3.3% or more → OIS October odds toward **45–50%** or higher, and prediction markets would have to rise from the 15–19% zone. CAD supported.
- **In-line scenario:** cores at 2.0 / 1.9 with headline at 3.2% on gasoline → OIS toward approx. **20–25%**, converging with prediction markets. A hold as the base case.
- **Dovish scenario:** cores at 1.9 / 1.8 or lower, or headline at 3.0% or below → October practically off (below 15% in both markets). CAD would lose support, though oil above $100 cushions it.

The Business Outlook Survey later that Monday can reinforce either side: firms’ rising price expectations would feed the hawkish channel.

## UK: wages and services before the Budget

BoE November is at approx. **91–94%**, so the question is not November but the path after (markets price a bit more than 100 bp of hikes over the next nine meetings). That creates an **asymmetry**: a hot print adds little to odds that are already near 100%, while a soft one can cut them.

- **Jobs (Tuesday):** private-sector regular pay (at its weakest since 2020) and tax-based payrolls matter more than unemployment. **Soft** (unemployment 5.0% or higher, regular pay 3.3% or lower, negative payrolls) → November could slip toward approx. 85% and the 2027 tail gets trimmed. **Firm** (regular pay 3.7% or higher, positive payrolls) → keeps November at approx. 93% and the following meetings intact.
- **CPI (Wednesday):** fuel will lift the headline; the new energy price cap does not kick in until November. **Services is the key piece.** Services at 3.8% or higher with core at 2.9% or higher would cement December and February. Services at 3.4% or lower with core at 2.6% or lower would take November toward approx. **80–85%**, the least-priced scenario.
- **The fiscal nuance:** with the 10-year gilt near 5.4% and the Budget on October 28, a hot print may **not** help sterling if the market reads it as more pressure on gilts. Tuesday’s gilt auction is a useful thermometer.

## Euro area: the risk is for the euro, not the rate

ECB October is an almost-certain hold and December is around 85%. Some Governing Council members (Nagel, Rehn) still talk about an unfinished fight against inflation and second-round effects. France is today more of a risk for the **EUR** than for the rate: an OAT–Bund spread of 145 bp or more could shave something off December (from approx. 85% toward approx. 75–80%). Tuesday’s ZEW closed before the riots, so it will reflect energy and AI more than France. Lagarde (Wednesday) matters if she mentions fragmentation. **Friday’s flash PMIs** are the first hard data after the riots in France (manufacturing consensus 50.6; services 50.8). The least-priced scenario: weaker activity with **sticky output prices** in Europe and the UK, which would give no relief to the ECB or the BoE and weigh on the euro and sterling through growth.

## New Zealand and Japan: the overnight CPIs

- **New Zealand CPI (Wednesday 4:45 PM CT).** **Non-tradables** matter more than the headline. At 1.2% or higher, RBNZ October would move toward approx. 80–90% across measures; since the hike is mostly priced, NZD upside looks limited. At 0.9% or lower, the pause option reopens (prediction markets below 55%) and NZD is more exposed. The reaction is asymmetric, and AUD/NZD is the cross that would reflect the surprise most directly.
- **Japan CPI (Thursday 6:30 PM CT).** Core would reach 2.1%, the first print above 2% this year (Tokyo already printed 2.7%). Only a core-core (ex-fresh food and energy) at 2.6% or higher would move BoJ October from approx. 4% to above 10%; the likely outcome is that it reinforces December. The yen remains more focused on the Ministry of Finance around 160 than on the BoJ.

## The US in silence: PMIs and Michigan as the only tests

With no Fed voices until the 28th, US data have less power to move October. Jobless claims only matter above approx. 215K (a layoffs narrative); today the Fed is watching inflation. Housing is a financial-conditions signal with the 10-year near 5.1%, not a direct catalyst.

- **Threshold to reopen October:** going from approx. 15% to 25–30% or more would take a combination of rising **Michigan inflation expectations** (1-year at 4.9% or higher, or 5-year at 3.7% or higher, versus 4.7% / 3.5% preliminary) **and** surging PMI prices, ideally with Brent above $112. Even then, with no Fed to validate it, the move would be limited.
- **Opposite scenario:** softer Michigan expectations and services PMI below 57 would leave October at approx. 10% and support the soft-dollar read.
- **Earnings:** Netflix and GM (Tuesday), Tesla and Meta (Wednesday) and P&G (Thursday) arrive with tech under pressure from AI financing concerns; they are a gauge of risk appetite more than of monetary policy.

With a dollar that has no Fed to move it, the DXY’s direction will depend more on the euro (France; approx. 58% of the index) and on oil than on US data.
