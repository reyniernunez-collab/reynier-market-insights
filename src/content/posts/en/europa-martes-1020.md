> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 20, 2026, 08:14 CT:** The Europe session turns the overnight oil spike into something more serious: Brent holds the escalation zone and sets highs not seen since May, while France's risk premium widens again and hits the euro. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens: what moved, what's priced and the conditional scenarios I'm working with for the rest of the day.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, Brent had touched $111 without a clear headline, and the question was whether this was real escalation or a thin-liquidity spike. The Europe session **answered in favor of escalation**: crude did not give back the move and set new highs.

With much of the tightening from the most energy-sensitive central banks already priced, what moves currencies today is not US data (housing, low impact) but **two prices: oil and the French risk premium**. My overall read: a neutral dollar, with sterling and the Canadian dollar firmer than the euro.

## Analysis

### Oil: escalation moves from the headline to the price

Brent closed every hourly candle since 3:00 AM CT above approx. $110.7, the threshold separating a "sideways with risk premium" market from one in "event mode". It set a high near **$112** around 6:36 AM CT and trades around **$111.5**, approx. 1.8% above Monday's settlement.

The backdrop is Iran's claim that two tankers hit mines near Hormuz (⚠️ a claim without independent verification or a clear date; the rally also started before that headline). There was a counterweight too: the possibility of further emergency reserve releases if needed.

An important technical detail: the November US crude contract expires today and spiked to approx. $109–110 on the expiry effect. The useful reference is December, near $101.

### France: the premium widens again

The spread between French and German 10-year bonds went from approx. 145–146 bp before the Europe open to **152.5–153 bp** (several sources agree), the widest in about two and a half weeks; the recent early-October peak was near 159 bp. The euro fell from approx. 1.1234 (2:26 AM CT) to 1.1204 (4:14 AM CT) and trades near **1.121**.

One nuance: the vote on the revenue part of the French budget that some expected today has reportedly been pushed to Oct 27 (⚠️ conflicting accounts). If confirmed, there is no scheduled French catalyst today; Saturday's protests and Friday's (Oct 23) rating review remain.

### Euro area: a two-sided ZEW

Expectations worsened more than forecast (Germany **28.1** vs approx. 30 expected; euro area **18.5** vs approx. 23.6), while Germany's current-conditions reading improved sharply (**−35.9** vs approx. −45). Read: the present is holding up, but the outlook is clouding over with expensive energy and France. For the ECB, the Oct 29 meeting still carries a very low hike probability (approx. 3–8%, ⚠️ the series disagree).

### Sterling and CAD: the currencies with a "live" meeting

- **Sterling:** UK unemployment fell to 4.7% (4.9% expected), and the market reads it as "November hike confirmed", not a more aggressive path. EUR/GBP fell to approx. **0.8395** (5:54 AM CT), its lowest since May 2025. With approx. 95–96% of a Nov 5 hike already priced, the room for a hawkish surprise is small; tomorrow's UK CPI is the G10 release with the most power to shift expectations this week.
- **CAD:** USD/CAD fell approx. 60 pips from Monday's New York session close to approx. **1.4045**, driven by oil, the high odds of a BoC hike (approx. 75–83% in swaps) and the Alberta referendum result (a clear majority for staying in Canada).

### Dollar, yen, gold and equities

- **Dollar:** the index touched approx. **101.74** (2:36 AM CT) and bounced toward **101.89**; the US 10-year yield is around **5.13%**. The Fed remains in its blackout period.
- **Yen:** barely moving (USD/JPY approx. **158.2**), supported by record demand at the 20-year Japanese government bond auction. For the BoJ, October is almost ruled out (approx. 3–5%) and attention shifts to December (⚠️ attributed to sources, unconfirmed).
- **Gold:** down approx. 0.4% (near **$4,142**) and not acting as a haven despite oil. Central bank buying remains a background support, not a catalyst for the day.
- **Equities:** Europe in the red (Euro Stoxx 50 approx. −0.9%, CAC approx. −0.8%) and US futures lower (Nasdaq approx. −0.65%), with doubts over AI spending after the Kospi's drop (−3.7%) in the Asia session.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **If oil sustains the escalation**, especially with damage confirmed by independent sources: more pressure on the yen and equities, a firm CAD and more doubts for central banks watching energy.
2. **If oil gives back the rally** (on the November contract expiry, a reserves announcement or diplomatic progress): CAD would be the most exposed and the euro would get some relief. This is the scenario the market prices least.
3. **If the French premium keeps widening** toward the October highs: the euro tends to stay weak against the dollar and, above all, against sterling. A move back below approx. 145 bp would give it room.
4. **Today's US data (housing and pending home sales):** little power to move the Fed; oil and France are in charge.
