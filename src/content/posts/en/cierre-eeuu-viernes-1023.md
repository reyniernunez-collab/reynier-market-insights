> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 23, 2026, 3:37 PM CT:** On Thursday the fear was the bill for artificial intelligence. On Friday tech took a breather, but oil took over as the source of pressure. With Brent near $117 and a business survey showing prices rising sharply, the New York session closed the week focused on inflation, just days before the Fed meeting.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the Dow Jones fell **1.2%** to **52,215.16**, the S&P 500 **0.5%** to **7,623.68** and the Nasdaq Composite **0.2%** to **25,955.34**. Most S&P 500 companies ended in the red, although tech, Thursday's epicenter of the selloff, stabilized.

Oil drove the day: Brent rose approx. **3%** and settled in the **$116–117** area on escalation in the Middle East. That put the question that weighs most on the Fed back in front: whether expensive energy keeps inflation high for longer. Treasuries, Thursday's haven, gave back part of their gains.

For the week, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq post their second straight weekly loss, while the Dow ends the five days slightly higher, helped by defensive sectors.

## Analysis

### Stocks: tech breathes, everything else pays for energy

The semiconductor index rose approx. **0.4–0.6%** (⚠️ readings differ depending on the closing time), a timid bounce after Thursday's plunge. Even so, the sector logs its longest weekly losing streak since November: the debate about the cost of sustaining the AI bet hasn't been settled, only paused.

The pressure moved elsewhere. The energy sector rallied hard and hit record highs, while airlines fell more than 4% on fuel costs. Telecom carriers also suffered on the threat of a new mobile competitor. The Dow, with heavier industrial and consumer weightings, was the day's hardest-hit index.

Next week brings the main earnings course: Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon. Their data-center spending will decide whether the chip bounce has legs.

### Oil: from event mode to inflation pressure

Brent settled in the **$116–117** area and WTI above **$102**, both up close to 3–4%. Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia keep the risk premium on Gulf supply alive. The approx. $112 event-mode threshold was left behind on Thursday, and on Friday the price kept climbing with no sign of exhaustion.

The reading shifts: this is no longer just a geopolitical risk but a price problem that the market is starting to pass through to expected inflation and, therefore, to the Fed.

### Data: strong activity, hotter prices

The US flash PMI confirmed an economy in full expansion: the composite eased to **57.6** (from 58.4), services to **57.9** (58.0 expected) and manufacturing rose to **56.0**, its best reading in more than four years. New orders are at their highest since 2022. The figure that matters most for the Fed, though, is another one: selling prices are rising at the fastest pace since August 2022, pushed by fuel, wages and insurance.

Michigan consumer sentiment came in at **48.0** in its final reading, almost unchanged from September, with one-year inflation expectations at **4.7%** and five-year at **3.5%**, both the highest since May. In Canada, retail sales rose **1.2%** in August, slightly below expectations but with gains across every subsector.

### Bonds and currencies: the haven deflates a little

The US 10-year yield rose about 3–5 bp to approx. **4.99–5.00%** (⚠️ readings differ by time), and the 2-year to approx. **4.69%**. Even so, the 10-year yield is heading for its third straight weekly decline. The dollar regained some ground: the dollar index rose approx. **0.2%**, EUR/USD ended near **1.119** and GBP/USD near **1.336**. USD/JPY finished almost flat, around **156.8**. Gold fell again, approx. **1%**, and still isn't working as a haven.

### Europe

At the Europe close, stocks ended mostly lower, with Paris down approx. **0.4%**. The French risk premium eased slightly ahead of the review of France's credit rating, due after the US market close.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If France's review is a one-notch downgrade:** it is largely priced in; the effect on the euro at Monday's open should be limited. A bigger cut or a negative outlook would widen the French premium and weigh on the euro.
- **If Brent holds above approx. $112 over the weekend:** event mode continues, along with pressure on expected inflation; a clear move back below approx. $109 would signal the premium is deflating.
- **If the US 10-year yield settles above 5%:** the market would be giving more weight to inflation than to fear; below approx. 4.95%, Thursday's haven logic would take charge again.
- **If big-tech earnings revive doubts about AI spending:** chips would again be the focus of selling and the yen the main beneficiary.
