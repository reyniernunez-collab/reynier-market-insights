> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 15, 2026, 3:44 PM CT:** For the second day in a row, inflation came in worse than expected, and this time by more. Even so, Wall Street finished in the green. The market decided the PPI is, above all, an energy problem, and spent the New York session on what has been keeping it up at night this week: whether the massive investment in AI infrastructure is soundly financed. Today's answer was relief, not resolution.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 finished at **7,744.06** (+0.33%), ending three straight sessions of losses. The Dow was the big winner, up **1.08%** to **52,177.69**, while the Nasdaq closed practically flat at **26,713.03** (+0.08%) after futures arrived at the open down approx. 1.1–1.2%. The SOX closed in the red again, **−0.88%** to **11,581.22**, and the VIX eased to **16.46** (−2.4%).

The day opened with a September PPI far above forecasts: **+1.3%** month over month (consensus approx. 0.5–0.6% ⚠️, calendars differed) and **6.3%** year over year. Retail sales also beat expectations at **+0.8%**. Despite that, Treasuries kept rallying, the dollar didn't recover what it lost in the Asia session and the odds of an October Fed hike didn't move.

## Analysis

### PPI: hot, but the market reads it as energy

Final demand PPI rose **1.3%** in September, the biggest monthly gain since March 2022, and **6.3%** over twelve months. Energy explains much of the jump: the energy component rose approx. 10.8%, led by gasoline and diesel. Core excluding food and energy rose approx. **0.7%** m/m ⚠️ (single source), and the measure that also strips out trade services was **+0.5%** (5.0% y/y), up from 0.3% in August.

That last detail is the one not to ignore: there are signs that pricier fuel is starting to filter into other categories. It's the same debate Wednesday's CPI left open.

Retail sales rose **0.8%** (August revised up to +1.3%), but excluding autos and gasoline the gain was just **+0.2%**. My read is that consumers are still spending, but a growing share of that spending is going to the pump, and they're starting to tighten elsewhere.

### Fed: the market isn't buying October

The striking thing about the day is what didn't happen. With two hot inflation prints in a row, the odds of a 25 bp hike on **October 28** remain at approx. **27–30%**, the same zone as in the morning, even though they touched higher levels during the session. The market keeps betting the Fed waits until December, where a hike is seen as almost certain on cumulative metrics (approx. 90% or more ⚠️; per-meeting metrics are lower and not directly comparable).

That balance faces a test tonight: Fed Chair Warsh speaks at **10:30 PM CT**, for the first time after both prints. If he signals October is on the table, the market has plenty of room to reprice.

### Stocks: the AI financing question

The Asia session sank with chipmakers (Seoul and Tokyo fell sharply) and the Europe session deepened the tech selloff. New York started along the same lines, with the PPI adding pressure. The turn came when Nvidia's CEO publicly defended the financial position of CoreWeave, a major AI computing provider that is an Nvidia customer and in which Nvidia also holds a stake. Gauges of credit stress at CoreWeave had reached record levels, fueling doubts about whether the companies behind the data-center buildout can keep up with the pace of investment.

The reaction was uneven, and that says a lot. CoreWeave bounced approx. 6%, but Nvidia closed down approx. 1.8% and the SOX also finished in the red. In other words, the market calmed down about the risk of an immediate credit accident, but not about the underlying question: whether all that infrastructure spending will actually pay off. Meanwhile, Meta released an AI model developers can download and modify while holding back its most advanced technology, another sign the sector is looking for ways to monetize what it has invested.

The money went elsewhere: the Dow (+1.08%) and small caps (Russell 2000 approx. +1.4%) led, helped by the sharp drop in yields. It was a rotation, not a return of appetite for tech.

### Rates and the dollar: the bond rally survives the PPI

The 10-year closed at approx. **5.08%**, about 11 bp below Wednesday's New York close (5.19%), and the 2-year at approx. **4.75%** ⚠️ (single source). Bonds rallying further after a PPI like this reinforces what the Europe session showed: the move is about safe-haven demand and term premium amid the AI selloff, not a cut in Fed expectations.

The dollar stayed soft. The DXY closed at approx. **102.0** (−0.5%), EUR/USD at approx. **1.1218** (+0.56%), sterling at approx. **1.3277** and USD/JPY at approx. **158.05**. AUD/USD remained the bearish exception at approx. **0.692** (−0.7%), still weighed down by the weak Australian jobs report. December gold futures rose approx. **2%** to **$4,191.60**: with yields falling and a weak dollar, this time it did act as a haven.

### Oil: Brent back at the threshold

Brent finished near **$105.9** (−0.2% versus Wednesday's $106.06 settlement), but what matters is the path: from the Europe session low near $103.37, it climbed back to the **$105.5** zone, the level that separates the sideways-to-bearish regime from a new escalation phase. WTI rose more strongly, to approx. **$96–98** ⚠️ (readings don't match, likely due to different contracts). With the PPI showing how much energy weighs on inflation, oil is once again a factor for the Fed.

### Scenarios

- **If Warsh opens the door to October tonight:** the odds could rise clearly from the current approx. 27–30%; yields would give back part of the drop and the dollar would regain ground, with tech exposed again.
- **If he keeps the patience message:** the market would keep treating inflation as an energy problem and December as the key date; the dollar would stay soft and attention would return to AI and credit.
- **If doubts about AI financing resurface:** today's bounce, resting on a single intervention, would be fragile; semiconductors are still in the red and haven't confirmed a floor.
- **Oil:** if Brent holds above $105.5, energy again pressures inflation over the coming months and hands the hawks arguments; if it loses the $104 zone again, the sideways-to-bearish regime is reinforced.
