> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 5, 2026, 1:00 AM CT:** Friday's upbeat tone on Wall Street carries into Asia. Stocks rise, Treasuries stay firm because the soft NFP took pressure off the Fed, Brent eases a little, and the sour note comes from the euro, which drops to its lowest against the dollar since May 2025.

## Executive Summary

The Asian session extends the relief left by the US jobs data. The Asia-Pacific index is up around **1%** and the Nikkei close to **2%**, following the tech-led rally on Wall Street. Treasuries gain: markets now give an October Fed hike **less than a 20%** chance and the 10-year slips 1 bp to **5.26%**. Brent is down ~0.5% to around **$101.75** on the Saudi price cut for Asia. The euro is the exception: it drops to ~**1.1161**, its lowest since May 2025.

## Analysis

### Stocks: Asia follows Wall Street

Friday's US tech rally pulls the region higher: Asia-Pacific ~+1% and the Nikkei ~+2%. US futures, on the other hand, erased their early advance: S&P 500 futures are down ~0.1% and Nasdaq 100 futures are flat. Europe points to a slightly positive open, though the region's sovereign bond jitters remain in the background.

### Bonds and the Fed: the NFP is still in charge

Friday's jobs report keeps supporting bonds. With less than a 20% chance of an October hike, the 10-year is down 1 bp to **5.26%**. It's a breather after a long slide in bond prices fueled by inflation worries, big government spending and the wave of corporate debt raised to pay for AI.

### Oil: Saudi Arabia makes its crude cheaper for Asia

Brent falls ~0.5% to around **$101.75** after Saudi Arabia cut the price of its benchmark crude for Asian buyers, against a backdrop of rising supply. It's still above $100.

### The euro and European debt: Spain adds to fiscal doubts

The euro falls as much as **0.8%** to ~**1.1161**, its lowest against the dollar since May 2025. Spain's political turmoil is feeding worries about the state of Europe's public finances. Bund futures rise and French debt lags again.

## What I'm watching today

- **Euro:** whether the lowest level since May 2025 extends into the European open, and whether French debt keeps lagging German debt.
- **Treasuries:** whether the 10-year settles below last week's highs with markets pricing less than a 20% chance of an October hike.
- **US data (CT):** S&P Global final services PMI at 8:45 AM and ISM services at 9:00 AM.
