> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 21, 2026, 02:05 CT:** Wednesday's Asia session flipped Tuesday's script. Results from the artificial intelligence sector restored confidence in the region's tech stocks, oil climbed back to the $110 area on fresh incidents in the Gulf, and Australia's central bank became the day's standalone currency story.

## Executive Summary

After Tuesday's chip selloff, the Asia session brought a **tech rebound**. Korea led the region, lifted by better-than-expected results from AI infrastructure names, and Nasdaq 100 futures rose overnight, although at this hour they are roughly flat.

**Brent** rose again, to approx. **$110.75**, on attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz and another drop in US crude inventories. Even so, it remains below Tuesday's high, so the market is still in a sideways regime with a risk premium rather than in escalation mode.

In currencies, the **dollar** is bouncing slightly after Tuesday's drop in the New York session. The standalone story is the **Australian dollar**, supported by a central bank that sounds more worried about inflation. **UK CPI** also landed at the Asia session close, a touch hotter than expected, but with no lasting effect on sterling.

## Analysis

### Tech: AI earnings revive Asia

At the Asia session close:

- **Kospi:** up between approx. **2.6% and 3.4%** depending on the reading, after falling approx. 3.7% on Tuesday. Very strong Korean exports so far in October also helped (single-source figure).
- **Nikkei:** approx. **+0.9%**. **Taiwan:** approx. +0.9% (single-source figure).
- **Australia:** approx. +0.2%, held back by a roughly 40% plunge in a data-centre developer that cut its outlook.
- **Hong Kong:** approx. −0.65% and **Shanghai:** approx. −0.5%.

The catalyst was results from SpaceX and Super Micro Computer, both of which jumped in US after-hours trading. Our read: the market is buying again into the idea that AI spending turns into revenue, but the reaction is still very sensitive to each report. The rebound in Seoul of a sector name that had one of the country's worst IPO debuts is a good gauge of how speculative the tone is.

US futures are not fully on board: S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures are roughly flat versus the New York close (between −0.1% and −0.2%). European futures point to a slightly higher open.

### Oil: back at $110, escalation not confirmed

Brent went from a settlement of approx. **$108.8** on Tuesday to a high of approx. **$110.75** around 11:00 PM CT and now trades near approx. **$110.3** (approx. +1.5%). US crude, now on the December contract, is up about 1% to near **$99**.

The drivers: attacks on ships near Hormuz, drones intercepted over Saudi Arabia and the closure of a border crossing between Iraq and Iran (the last two headlines lack independent confirmation), plus a private-survey drop in US crude inventories of approx. 4.9 million barrels.

The key nuance: on Tuesday, a spike to almost $112 unwound within hours and ended as a false breakout. As long as Brent stays below that high, we read it as **sideways with a tail-risk premium**. Crude implied volatility eased after Tuesday, a sign the market is paying less for protection than two days ago.

### Australia: the RBA reprices November

The Australian central bank's deputy governor said inflation looks worse than expected and that the underlying measure for the third quarter will probably exceed forecasts. The market reacted sharply:

- The odds of a hike on **November 3** moved from approx. **27%** to a range of approx. **40–53%**. Sources disagree on the exact level but agree on the direction, and the whole Australian rate curve moved up.
- The Australian 10-year yield is up about 5 bp (single-source reading).
- **AUD/USD** touched approx. **0.703** around 9:00 PM CT and is back to approx. **0.7015–0.702**: the move is real, but it did not hold at the high.

With New Zealand's CPI due this afternoon, the Australian dollar versus the New Zealand dollar now has a clear driver in Australia's favour, as long as the New Zealand data does not surprise to the upside.

### UK: hotter CPI, sterling unchanged

UK September CPI, released at the Asia session close:

- **Headline:** **3.5%** year on year (3.4% expected; previous 3.1%), pushed up by fuel.
- **Core:** **2.8%** (forecasts ranged from 2.7% to 2.8%).
- **Services:** **3.7%** (from 3.4%), the figure the Bank of England watches most.

Sterling rose a few pips against the dollar and gave it back within minutes; the euro against sterling stays near its lowest since May 2025 (approx. 0.839). The UK 2-year yield even dipped slightly. Our read: with a November hike already almost fully priced (approx. 84–96% depending on the source; the lowest figure predates the release), a slightly hotter CPI confirms what the market already had in the price without adding much.

### FX and gold: the dollar claws back a little

Indicative readings versus Tuesday's New York close:

- **DXY** approx. **101.8** (+0.1%), with the US 10-year yield at approx. **5.12%** (+1 bp).
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1221** (about −15 pips), a mild downward drift without losing 1.12. The French risk premium stays high at approx. 153 bp (single-source reading today).
- **USD/JPY** approx. **158.1**, unchanged: neither a worse-than-expected Japanese trade balance nor a more hawkish tone from a senior BoJ official moved the yen.
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.406**, sideways.
- **NZD/USD** approx. **0.5675**, a little weaker ahead of its CPI.

**Gold** eases slightly, in a range of approx. **$4,115–4,135** depending on the reference, still capped by high real yields.

### Central banks: what is priced

- **Fed (Oct 28):** hike at approx. **12–16%** (sources differ). December remains the key meeting, at approx. 80–87% depending on the series.
- **BoC (Oct 28):** approx. **78–81%** in swaps.
- **BoE (Nov 5):** practically fully priced (see above).
- **RBA (Nov 3):** approx. **40–53%**, the meeting that moved most on the day.
- **RBNZ (Oct 28):** approx. **74–80%** (range across sources).
- **ECB (Oct 29)** and **BoJ (Oct 30):** pause dominant (approx. 3–6% and 3–5%).

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent clears Tuesday's high (approx. $112)** with hourly closes above it, the market would go back to pricing a Gulf event: pressure on the yen and equities, and support for the CAD. If it loses Tuesday's New York session low (approx. $106), the premium unwinds.
- **If Bailey validates the CPI** with a hawkish tone, sterling could find the push it lacked on the release; if he stresses caution, the CPI will stand as confirmation and little more.
- **If RBA November odds settle near 50%** over the coming days, the Australian dollar would have a driver of its own; if the figure stays closer to 40%, the overnight move may fade into headlines.
- **If Asia's tech rebound carries into the New York open**, Tuesday's chip selloff would look like an episode; if futures slip again, doubts about AI will keep setting the tone ahead of more earnings.
