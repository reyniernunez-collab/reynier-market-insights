> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 12, 2026, 3:55 PM CT:** The holiday Monday left Wall Street without direction and the dollar in control. Semiconductors extended their longest losing streak since July and held back the S&P 500, which still sits near record highs as earnings season approaches. Oil held the rally it built in the Europe session and the euro finished at the day's low.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the picture is a split market. Beneath the index, most sectors advanced; the drag came from the big chipmakers, now down five sessions in a row. The S&P 500 closed at **7,807.24** (−0.06%), the Nasdaq at **27,245.90** (−0.44%) and the Dow at **51,850.55** (+0.38%); the SOX semiconductor index lost **−2.67%** ⚠️ (closing levels from a single source). The VIX edged up from 14.84 to ~15.2: more caution, not fear.

What the Asia session framed as "contained" oil no longer is. The Europe-session rally, driven by headlines out of Saudi Arabia and doubts over the Russia–Ukraine energy truce, held through the New York session: Brent trades around **$106** and WTI near **$93** late in the day, versus Friday's settlement of $104.72 and $91.85. The market keeps reading it as an inflation shock that favors the dollar, not as a reason to seek shelter.

## Analysis

### Equities: flat index, broken leadership

The S&P 500 remains a step away from record highs, supported by expectations of another solid earnings season, which kicks off this week with the big banks. But tech leadership is faltering: the chip losing streak is the longest since July. Nasdaq futures ended near ~30,877, below Friday's New York close (~31,109), the signal flagged overnight as a warning that Sunday's upbeat tone might have been just a liquidity gap.

The takeaway for the week: with the index near records but carried by defensive and cyclical sectors while tech weakens, bank earnings (Tuesday and Wednesday) and TSMC (Thursday) matter more than usual in deciding whether this rotation turns into a sector correction or stays an adjustment.

### Oil: the Europe rally sticks

Brent touched a high of ~**$107.3** during the day and finished the New York session around **$106**; WTI reached ~$94.4 and sat near **$93.2** (late futures prices, not official settlement). The day's range, from ~$102.4 in the Asia session to above $107, sums up the problem: the market swings between hopes for an energy truce nobody has fully verified and evidence that shipping through Hormuz is still strained. With Brent holding above ~$105.5, the "escalation" scenario we were watching for the New York session stays live heading into the night.

### Dollar and rates: oil pushes December

The DXY closed at ~**102.59** (from 102.23 on Friday) and EUR/USD finished at **1.1157**, the day's low, about 45 pips below Friday's New York session close. The euro is the most exposed: Europe imports energy and pays more for delivered crude than the US does. USD/JPY holds at ~**158.6**, inside the 158.5–159.5 zone where the risk of verbal warnings from Tokyo grows. USD/CAD rose to ~**1.428** with Canada on holiday, and high oil didn't help the CAD. USD/CHF ended near 0.835 and gold near **$4,194** (low ~$4,178): neither the franc nor gold worked as a haven.

In rates, the Fed's October 28 meeting was still pricing ~**16.5–20%** odds of a hike in the morning depending on the source ⚠️, while December rose to ~**88%** for at least one cumulative hike (a single-source futures read ⚠️). Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack argued that smaller, more frequent policy adjustments would help keep inflation expectations anchored, though she did not explicitly back a hike this month. Her message fits what is already priced: more pressure on December than on October. With the cash bond market closed for Columbus Day, the 10-year is still at Friday's 5.24%; Tuesday will bring the first real print since oil jumped.

### Scenarios for the rest of the week

- **If Brent holds above ~$105.5 into CPI:** the dollar keeps the initiative, the euro stays under pressure and December moves closer to fully priced.
- **If oil drops back below Friday's settlement ($104.72):** the Asia-session pattern would repeat and the dollar would give back part of today's gains.
- **Wednesday's CPI:** the headline is already expected hot on energy (+0.6% m/m); what could reopen October is core at +0.4% or higher. Core at +0.1% or lower would send October back toward ~10%.
