> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 9, 2026, 11:00 CT:** Europe closes Friday with a broad rebound: the Stoxx 600 gains close to 1% and erases the week's losses. Crude eases to ~$103 after Trump's message on Iran, US futures recover after two red sessions, and Canadian jobs come in far weaker than expected. Michigan also disappoints on sentiment, though inflation expectations barely move.

## Executive Summary

Friday's Europe session flips Thursday's tone. The **Stoxx 600** rises **0.9%** and wipes out weekly losses that had reached ~**1.3%**; French equities stand out after a week of fiscal and political pressure. Brent trades near **~$103**, well off the prior session's two-week high, after Trump said the US would not attack Iran before the midterms and cited productive talks with Tehran. S&P futures advance ~**0.4%** and Nasdaq ~**0.8%**; a chip ETF gains ~**1.5%** premarket. Treasuries resume losses after Thursday's volatility: the 10-year rises **~2 bp** to ~**5.25%**. The dollar is roughly flat intraday but is on track for a **fourth straight weekly gain**. On the data, Canada loses ~**68,000** jobs and preliminary Michigan falls to **46.3**.

## Analysis

### Equities: European rebound and relief in the AI narrative

Europe closes in the green with an important nuance: France, which had dragged the week on fiscal gridlock and political deadlock, **leads** the rebound. The Stoxx 600 doesn't just rise 0.9%; it erases a weekly drawdown that had reached ~1.3%. This is a recovery Europe close, not euphoria: regional bonds had ridden the late-session Treasury relief yesterday, and equities are using that cushion today.

In the US, the premarket catalyst is the AI valuation narrative. After Thursday's chip selloff, more constructive revenue projections arrive: OpenAI aims to reach or exceed ~**$70 billion** in annualised revenue by year-end. That eases concerns that sales were falling short, but it **doesn't close** the deeper debate: whether companies can monetise massive AI investments with borrowing costs higher. One European asset manager puts it cleanly: the main risk isn't a demand collapse but a **gradual slowdown** in growth or monetisation. Futures (ES ~+0.4%, NQ ~~+0.8%) and the chip ETF (~~+1.5% premarket) reflect that tactical relief after two down sessions.

### Oil: Iran premium narrows; Isaias remains

Brent eases toward **~$103** after settling Thursday at **$104.28** (+4.1%). The brake is political: Trump ruled out an Iran strike before the November 3 midterms and spoke of productive discussions with Tehran. That cuts the near-term upside tail that had pushed prices to a two-week high in the prior session. The physical offset hasn't gone away: Isaias still has platforms shut in the Gulf (~**25%** of offshore in a common reading; some overnight figures ran higher — treat as a range, not a single number), with landfall expected between Friday night and Saturday.

Elevated oil had weighed on equities through rate expectations. With Brent back toward $103, that channel loosens and gives room to the European rebound. A normal Brent day, based on the 30-day expected move from options (~13%), is roughly ±$3; today the price is moving **inside** that range versus Thursday's settlement, unlike yesterday's jump.

### Bonds and the dollar: the 10-year rises again

After Thursday's volatile session — the 30-year auction, relief from ~5.35% toward ~5.23% — Treasuries **resume losses**. The US 10-year rises ~**2 bp** to ~**5.25%** at the Europe close. The dollar is relatively unchanged on the day but remains on course for a **fourth consecutive weekly gain**. Useful read: yesterday's yield relief didn't fully stick; the bond market stays under structural pressure even as crude eases.

### France: a Friday of tactical relief

All week French assets had been under pressure from fiscal stability concerns and political gridlock. The OAT-Bund spread had closed Thursday near **~140 bp** (intraday peak ~142 bp). At Friday's Europe close, the rebound in French stocks and a more constructive tone in regional bonds mark **tactical relief**, not necessarily a regime change. While the spread stays near ~140 bp, the euro still has a limited ceiling; a clear move below ~135 bp would open more room to recover.

### Canada and Michigan: two prints, two reads

**Canadian employment (7:30 AM CT):** the market expected a modest gain (~+7 to +9K depending on the poll). The print was **−68,000** jobs, with unemployment at **6.5%** (from 6.4%). Losses were split between full-time and part-time, concentrated among young workers and in public-leaning sectors (education and health care). This is the last LFS before the BoC's October 28 decision. Before the data, October hike odds sat at ~**32%** in a primary reading versus ~**21–25%** in another (wide dispersion). A print this weak pushes that probability **lower**; we don't invent the exact new price, but the bias is clearly dovish for October.

**Preliminary Michigan (9:00 AM CT):** sentiment falls to **46.3** from 48.1 (consensus ~47.6). Current conditions 44.7 (from 50.9); expectations 47.3 (from 46.3). One-year inflation **4.7%** (from 4.6%) and five-year **3.5%** (from 3.4%). The headline is soft, but inflation expectations **barely rise** — well short of the threshold (~4.8–5.0% at one year) that would have been needed to reopen Fed October. October stays at ~**17–18%**; December ~**80%** by meeting in one reading versus somewhat different cumulative figures by methodology (~5 pp possible gap — flag as a range).
