> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 28, 2026, 3:45 PM CT:** It was central bank day and the script mostly held: Canada hiked, the Fed waited and the UK budget brought no scares. The surprise was in the details. The Fed showed more division in the vote than expected, but its chair refused to give any hint about December, and the market read that silence as a slight reduction in hike risk: the short end of the curve fell and the dollar gave back part of the week's gains. Here is the landscape after the New York session close.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 rose **0.28%** to **7,743.98**, the Nasdaq Composite **0.27%** to **26,549.85** and the Dow Jones **0.19%** to **52,475.10**, after a choppy session around the Fed. The VIX eased to the **16.9** area.

The Federal Reserve kept its benchmark rate at **3.75–4.00%**, as expected, but with two dissents in favor of a hike. Chair Kevin Warsh stressed that the Fed is ready to act if needed, yet made clear he will not pre-announce what happens in December. Earlier, the Bank of Canada raised its rate to **2.50%** and the UK budget passed without trouble for bonds.

After the close, big-tech earnings opened a gap: Microsoft rose in after-hours trading and Alphabet fell hard, a sign that the AI trade is no longer rewarded across the board.

## Analysis

### Fed: more division in the vote, fewer hints on December

The statement upgraded its description of the economy (activity is now expanding at a "strong" pace) and repeated that inflation remains elevated. The key point is that the committee wants to gather more information before deciding its next step. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack voted for a quarter-point hike; two regional presidents who had called for hikes in July joined the majority this time.

At the press conference, Warsh argued that monetary policy is already working: market yields have risen, credit spreads have widened for some assets, and tighter financial conditions, he said, show that policy is having an effect. He rejected giving guidance on upcoming meetings outside of a crisis and declined to speculate about December. He also announced a calendar change: from 2027, one of the eight yearly meetings will be devoted solely to strategy, with no rate vote.

**What's still priced:** a December hike remains the base case, around **80%** in prediction markets after the meeting (⚠️ a single source after the decision; in the morning, the different measures showed approx. 77–82%). In other words, the market did not unwind the bet, but it did not get the confirmation the most hawkish players wanted either.

### Bonds and the dollar: the short end breathes

The 2-year yield fell approx. **5 bp** to the **4.69%** area, while the 10-year finished practically flat, around **5.0%**: a steeper curve, consistent with a Fed that is not pushing short rates and a market that still demands a long-term premium with oil expensive.

The dollar index slipped approx. **0.2%** to **102.1**. EUR/USD rose to approx. **1.1155**, well away from the **1.110–1.111** support that had held three times across the Asia and Europe sessions. GBP/USD climbed to approx. **1.333** and USD/JPY edged down to the **157.8** area, once again failing to clear **158**. The New Zealand dollar was the day's weakest (approx. **−1%** against the US dollar) after its central bank's surprise pause in the Asia session; the Australian dollar, by contrast, gained approx. **0.4%**. Gold ended flat, in the **$4,070** area (December futures).

### Canada: first hike since 2023, with caution

The Bank of Canada raised its rate **25 bp to 2.50%**, its first hike since July 2023, and revised both inflation and growth higher: it now expects headline inflation to reach approx. **3.5%** by year-end because of energy. The message was cautious: further hikes are not ruled out, but decisions will be taken one meeting at a time. The market read it as "one and see": the probability of another hike in December fell to approx. **40%** (⚠️ a single source; it was near 48% in the morning). Even so, the Canadian dollar ended strong, with USD/CAD below **1.40** (approx. **1.399**), helped by broad US dollar weakness and oil.

### United Kingdom: a budget without scares

The budget kept to the fiscal rules and left headroom of approx. **£15 billion**, in line with expectations though smaller than in the spring. Gilts reacted with moderate relief after the speech and the pound barely moved at that point. With fiscal risk cleared for now, the focus returns to the Bank of England, which has a hike almost fully priced for November 5.

### Stocks: AI no longer rises as a bloc

Oracle jumped approx. **11%** after presenting long-term free cash flow projections that eased doubts about the return on its AI spending, and the semiconductor index gained **1.8%**. After the New York close came the test the market was waiting for: Microsoft rose approx. **3–4%** after hours on cloud strength, while Alphabet fell approx. **9–10%** despite faster search growth, weighed down by much lower free cash flow due to AI spending (⚠️ after-hours prices, preliminary). The takeaway: the market is increasingly separating those who turn AI investment into cash from those who have not yet done so.

### Oil and the Gulf: more diplomacy, premium intact

December Brent settled at approx. **$115.3**, about **$1.4** above Tuesday (⚠️ settlement figure from a single source; the contract gave back some of it later), and WTI hovered around **$101.9**. In the afternoon there were signs of indirect US–Iran contacts and of a possible meeting in Turkey before November 4 (⚠️ single source), and the US military said Hormuz shipping routes are clear of mines. None of it removed oil's premium.

⚠️ Contract reminder: December Brent expires this week and January trades well below it, so price references vary a lot depending on the contract.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If tomorrow's core PCE comes in above expectations:** Warsh's silence would stop mattering and December would look almost done again, with the 2-year yield recovering today's drop and the dollar firm again. A moderate print would consolidate today's read.
- **If the ECB holds but clearly opens the door to December:** the euro could consolidate above 1.11; if it sounds cautious on weak growth and France, today's rise would look like a mere adjustment against the dollar.
- **If Alphabet's drop drags other tech names in the Asia session:** the index would lose the support Oracle gave it today; if Microsoft offsets it, the market would keep separating AI winners and losers without a broad sell-off.
- **If US–Iran contacts are confirmed with a concrete meeting:** oil could lose premium and ease pressure on energy-importing currencies; a new incident in the Gulf would put crude back at the center.
