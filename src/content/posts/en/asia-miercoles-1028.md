> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 28, 2026, 01:58 CT:** Wednesday's Asia session belonged to the Pacific central banks. In under an hour, Australia released inflation data that pushes its central bank toward a hike, and New Zealand chose to wait by a single vote. Two neighbors, the same energy shock, two different paths.

## Executive Summary

The night delivered a genuine surprise. New Zealand's central bank **held its official rate at 2.75%** when the market had almost fully priced a hike, and at nearly the same time Australia's inflation data convinced the market that its central bank will hike on November 3. The result was a sharp move between the two currencies, not against the US dollar.

Outside that bloc, the script is unchanged: **a firm dollar, Treasury yields back above 5% and crude higher** on headlines from the Gulf. My read: the market heads into the busiest day of the week (UK Budget, Bank of Canada and the Fed) with the dollar as the dominant position, and New Zealand's surprise is a reminder that a "priced-in" decision is not a guaranteed one.

## Analysis

### New Zealand: a hawkish pause the market doesn't fully buy

New Zealand's central bank left its rate at **2.75%** in a **4–3 vote**. The majority weighed lower-than-expected headline inflation, a smaller pass-through from higher fuel prices, contained wages and a fragile recovery. Even so, all members agree more tightening will be needed.

The **New Zealand dollar** sank approx. **1.8%** within minutes and then recovered part of it; it is still down approx. **1.2%, around 0.561**. At the press conference, the governor stressed that more hikes are likely, possibly into restrictive territory, and warned that a weak currency is a concern for imported inflation. The market gave her some credit (the odds of a December hike rose to approx. **63–71%**), but not enough to erase the drop.

### Australia: inflation is too high

**Third-quarter core inflation** rose **1.0% on the quarter**, in line with forecasts, and the monthly September reading came in around **4.3% year on year**, slightly above expectations. The governor of Australia's central bank said inflation is **too high** and that demand is outpacing supply, without committing on the November 3 meeting.

The odds of a hike at that meeting moved from approx. **60%** to a range of approx. **82–94%**: a jump of more than 20 points, although measures differ by about 12 points, so the exact figure deserves caution.

### The Antipodean cross at a more-than-decade high

With one central bank leaning toward hiking and the other waiting, the Australian dollar–New Zealand dollar cross **rose more than 1.3% to its highest since 2013**, then corrected part of the gain. **AUD/USD**, by contrast, barely changed: it rose on the data and gave almost all of it back. The divergence played out between the two currencies, not against the dollar.

### Dollar and bonds: firm ahead of the Fed

The **dollar index** is at approx. **102.4–102.5**, the week's highs, and this time with support from bonds: the **US 10-year yield is back above 5%** and the 2-year is up approx. 2 bp.

- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.111–1.112**, brushing mid-October lows.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.328**: sterling is the weakest in the group hours before the Budget.
- **USD/JPY** steady below **158**, with no new warnings from Tokyo; the level is still acting as a ceiling.
- The **Swiss franc** is also losing ground against the dollar.

### Oil and gold: Gulf headlines

**Brent** is up approx. **$2, to the $116 area**. The Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, which Saudi authorities **have not confirmed**, and according to US press reports a short campaign against Iran after the midterm elections is being weighed, with no decision taken. Both headlines are single-source. **Gold** is down approx. **0.7%** despite the tension, held back by the dollar and yields.

### Stocks: Korea soars

**Korea** soared approx. **6–7%** after results from a major memory-chip maker, **Japan** is up approx. **1.2%** and **Australia** closed flat. Nasdaq 100 futures are up approx. **0.4–0.5%** ahead of Microsoft and Alphabet earnings.

### Central banks: what the market is pricing

I give ranges because measures differ:

- **Fed (today, 1:00 PM CT):** a hold is near certain (approx. 88–96%); a December hike sits at approx. **77–79%**.
- **Bank of Canada (today, 8:45 AM CT):** a hike is expected, but the odds slipped to approx. **80–87%**. New Zealand shows that a priced-in hike is not guaranteed.
- **ECB (Thursday):** no change; December in a wide range of approx. 70–85%, because sources disagree.
- **Bank of England (November 5):** hike at approx. 96–98%.
- **Bank of Japan (Thursday night, CT):** no change; December at approx. 76–79%.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If the UK Budget is well received**, sterling could recover some of its losses; if the market sees it as expansionary without clear funding, gilts and sterling would come under pressure.
- **If the Bank of Canada hikes as expected**, the focus shifts to its guidance; if it surprises with a pause, as New Zealand did, the Canadian dollar would suffer and the market would reassess other hikes it takes for granted.
- **If the Fed keeps the door open to December**, the dollar would have reasons to stay firm; a more cautious message would test a position that is already crowded.
- **If the attack on the Saudi refinery is confirmed** or signs of action against Iran grow, crude could keep rising and weigh on energy-importing currencies such as the euro and sterling.
