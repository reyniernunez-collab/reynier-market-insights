> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 8, 2026, 2:00 AM CT:** The Asia session closes with oil out in front again. Brent tops $102 on a mix of a hurricane in the Gulf of Mexico and the risk of a strike on Iran, and that is weighing once more on bonds and on stocks that had been brushing record highs. FX was quiet: the dollar holds firm, and what's moving is the pricing of other central banks' hikes.

## Executive Summary

Global stocks slip ~**0.2%**, extending their pause after nearing records earlier in the week; Wall Street had already given ground on Wednesday and Asia followed the same way. Brent rises ~2% and trades in the **$102–103** area depending on the time and benchmark, with WTI around **$90**. The US 10-year is back near **5.31%**. The dollar holds close to its 18-month high, while the euro stays steady despite fiscal strain in France. In rates, markets scale back hike bets for the BoE, ECB and RBA, while Fed pricing is little changed.

## Analysis

### Oil: a hurricane and a war headline at once

Oil has two engines running at the same time. The first is physical: Isaías is now a hurricane and is expected near the northern Gulf coast on Friday night. Producers have shut in roughly **511,000 barrels a day**, about **25%** of Gulf of Mexico offshore output, with several platforms evacuated. The second is geopolitical: according to press reports, the White House asked the Pentagon to prepare strike options against Iran ahead of the midterm elections. On top of that comes the Houthi–Saudi tension, with claimed attacks on Saudi airports that Riyadh hasn't fully confirmed. The cost of chartering supertankers is at record highs, which raises costs across the whole oil chain.

The counterweight is still the IEA's accelerated reserve release. For reference, Brent's 30-day expected move is around **13.2%**, and its typical one-day range is roughly ±$3; after rising almost $3 from Wednesday's settlement ($100.20), Brent is already close to the edge of that daily range. That doesn't say where it goes next, but it does say much of a normal day's move already happened in Asia.

### Bonds and stocks: expensive oil feeds back into the curve

The US 10-year is back near **5.31%**, a few basis points below Wednesday's **5.36%**, the highest since 2002. The week's pattern repeats: when oil rises, markets price in more inflation and bonds pay for it. The Fed already raised rates last month in a unanimous vote, and crude above $100 complicates the path. Stocks, which had leaned on enthusiasm for artificial-intelligence investment despite high borrowing costs, now look to earnings season: the market wants to see that spending start to pay off.

### FX: firm dollar, sterling at its floor

FX was calm in Asia. The dollar index holds around **102.25**, near its 18-month high. Japan posted a much larger current-account surplus than expected (about **¥4.06 trillion** versus ~¥3.19 trillion forecast), and Prime Minister Takaichi talked about keeping bond issuance in check; the yen got a brief lift, but USD/JPY returned to ~158.2 and traded between ~157.6 and 158.3, without approaching 158.5. Sterling is testing **1.3200**, the floor it has held for about ten sessions. The euro stays near **1.12**: France insists it won't change its issuance strategy, but strain over its public finances remains the single currency's main drag. German August trade data, with exports falling instead of rising, barely moved the euro.

### Rates: hike bets deflate outside the US

The most relevant shift overnight is in central-bank pricing:

- **Fed:** ~**18–19%** odds of an October hike depending on the source, and between ~78% and ~84% for December (figures differ by methodology). Wednesday's minutes didn't pull the hike forward to October.
- **BoE:** a November hike drops to ~**82%** in our main reading, from ~94% on Wednesday. Note: sources disagree widely, from 81% to 96%, so treat it as a range rather than an exact figure.
- **ECB:** a December hike eases to ~**73–76%**, depending on the source.
- **RBA:** November drops to ~**25%**, even though Australian inflation expectations rose to 5.3% (a single-source figure). Markets didn't take it as a case for a hike.

The big picture: if other central banks lose hike bets and the Fed doesn't, the gap favors the dollar against sterling and the euro.

### Scenarios for the European session

- **If Waller explicitly talks about October** with a hiking tone, October could come back into play, adding pressure on the 10-year and support for the dollar.
- **If BoE speakers sound hawkish**, November can win back part of the lost pricing and give sterling some air; if not, the 1.3200 floor is more exposed.
- **If Isaías veers off or tension with Iran cools**, oil, having already covered almost a full daily range, has room to pull back.
