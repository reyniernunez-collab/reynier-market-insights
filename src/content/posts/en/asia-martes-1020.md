> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 20, 2026, 01:57 CT:** The chip rebound left by Monday's New York close did not cross the Pacific. During the Asia session doubts about the AI business returned ahead of a week packed with earnings, oil touched a sensitive area without a headline to justify it, and the dollar lost ground against almost the entire G10.

## Executive Summary

Tuesday's Asia session was **risk-off in tech**. The Kospi, the region's gauge of AI investment, closed down approx. **3.7%**, and the rest of the region also finished in the red. US futures are holding up better: Nasdaq 100 futures are off approx. **0.3%** and S&P 500 futures are virtually flat.

At the same time, **Brent** climbed to approx. **$111** and posted an hourly close above approx. $110.7, the level that separates a sideways market with a risk premium from one that is pricing a Gulf event again. But no new headline emerged and it is already giving back part of the move.

The **dollar** weakened across the board as US yields fell. Against that backdrop two stories stand out on their own: **sterling**, on a better-than-expected UK jobs report, and the **Canadian dollar**, after the Alberta referendum result.

## Analysis

### AI chips: New York's rebound doesn't carry over

At the Asia session close:

- **Kospi:** −3.7% (official close), dragged down by the big memory makers (between −3% and −6%).
- **Nikkei:** approx. −1.3% (close not confirmed by a second source).
- **Taiwan:** −1.1%; **Hong Kong:** approx. −1.2% and **Shanghai:** approx. −0.5%, both still trading at the time of writing.
- **Australia:** −0.3%.

The read is that the market doesn't trust Monday's rebound yet. Questions about how AI spending is being financed remain open, and this week's big tech earnings will test whether the sector's gains can hold. European futures point to a weaker open.

### Oil: Brent touches the escalation zone without confirming it

Brent went from Monday's New York session settle of **$109.55** to a high of approx. **$111** near 1:00 AM CT, and one hourly candle closed above approx. $110.7. It now trades around approx. **$110.2**.

Two important caveats. First, we found no new headline to explain the spike: the backdrop is still Monday's Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi facilities, which have **no independent confirmation**. Second, it's the same pattern as Monday, when a touch of that area was sold within the next hour. With thin Asia liquidity, it may just be buying flow.

On top of that, the November WTI contract expires today (approx. $104), which muddies its price; the cleaner reference is December (approx. $98.7). Implied volatility in crude is rising, as it is in US indices; in currencies it isn't. Hedging is being paid for in commodities and equities, not FX.

The negative correlation between crude and US indices worked again: oil up, Nasdaq slightly down.

### FX: broad dollar weakness, with two stories of their own

Indicative readings versus Monday's New York close:

- **DXY** approx. **101.78** (−0.2%), with the US 10-year at approx. **5.12%** (−2 to −3 bp; single-source live reading).
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1228** (+15 to +18 pips), with no European driver: it's the dollar that's falling. The French risk premium remains around 146 bp.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3352** (approx. +50 pips), the group's best performer.
- **USD/JPY** approx. **158.1** (approx. −35 pips).
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.4079**, after touching 1.4071, Monday's low.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.6994** and **NZD/USD** approx. **0.5685**, both higher.

Gold futures sit around approx. **$4,151** (−0.2%): unmoved by either oil or the softer dollar, held back by high real yields.

### UK: strong jobs, measured reaction

- **Unemployment:** 4.7% (4.9% expected).
- **Three-month employment:** approx. +102k (published forecasts were very scattered, so the surprise is hard to measure).
- **Pay including bonuses:** eases to 3.7% (from 3.9%); regular pay rises to 3.7%.

Sterling gained about 10–20 pips after the data, but most of its rise since the New York close came **before** it, on the weak dollar. Gilt yields did not rise (indicative reading before the cash open). With a November BoE hike already almost fully priced (approx. 87–95% depending on the reading), there is little room for hawkish surprises. The next test is tomorrow's UK CPI.

### Germany: red-hot producer prices, no impact

German PPI rose **1.9%** on the month (forecasts ranged from 0.8% to 1.6%) and **6.7%** year on year. Neither the euro nor the Bund moved: the ECB watches consumer inflation, and the October 29 meeting still carries a very low probability of a hike.

### Canada: Alberta stays and the BoC is almost priced

In Alberta's non-binding referendum, the option to remain in Canada stands at around **76–77%** with more than a million votes counted; official results come on October 29. A risk premium comes off and the CAD firms.

On the BoC, in a week the market has gone from doubting to all but taking for granted a 25 bp hike on October 28: swaps price approx. **80–83%**, even though Canada's September CPI came in below expectations. The governor's tone and inflation expectations weighed more. A prediction market shows a lower figure (approx. 69%), but it is shallow and we read it as profit-taking, not a signal of a pause. With the hike nearly priced, there is little extra room for the CAD on that front.

### Japan and China

According to press reports citing sources (single source), the **BoJ** would hold rates in October and leave the door open to a hike as early as December, which the market prices at approx. **78%**. The yen recovered ground, though it was already rising on lower US yields.

In **China**, the loan prime rates were left unchanged, as expected. The central bank announced daily very short-term liquidity injections through early November (single-source figure), which is not the same as a rate cut.

### Central banks: what is priced

- **Fed (Oct 28):** hike at approx. **14–18%** (sources differ). December remains the central meeting, at approx. 78–90% depending on the series.
- **BoC (Oct 28):** approx. **80–83%**, the meeting that has shifted most in a week.
- **BoE (Nov 5):** approx. **87–95%**.
- **RBNZ (Oct 28):** approx. **74–81%** (range across sources).
- **RBA (Nov 3):** approx. **22–27%**.
- **ECB (Oct 29)** and **BoJ (Oct 30):** a hold dominates (approx. 4–8% and 3–5%).

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent holds hourly closes above approx. $110.7** or sets a new high with confirmed damage in the Gulf, the market would be pricing an event again: more pressure on the yen and on indices, and some support for the CAD. If it slips back below approx. $109.6, it would look like a false breakout and the sideways market with a tail premium would return.
- **If Nasdaq futures extend losses** into the New York open, Asia's chip selloff would stop being a regional episode and AI doubts would move back to center stage ahead of earnings. If they stabilize, Monday's rebound keeps its meaning.
- **If gilt yields start to rise** during the Europe session, the jobs data would gain weight for sterling; if they stay flat, its advance remains mostly a weak-dollar story.
- **If the French risk premium widens**, the euro would stay the weak leg of the map even as the dollar falls; if it calms, EUR/USD would have more room.
