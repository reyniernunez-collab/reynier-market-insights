> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 25, 2026, 15:40 CT:** This is the busiest week of the quarter: the RBNZ on Tuesday night, the UK Budget, the BoC and the Fed on Wednesday, the ECB on Thursday, the BoJ on Thursday night, and in between US Q3 GDP and PCE, plus earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon. But nearly every decision is already priced. So what gets tested is not what central banks do this week, but what they say about **December**, which is where markets still have room to move. There are two exceptions where the decision or the data itself can surprise: the **BoC**, with markets and economists on opposite sides, and **Australia CPI**, which defines an RBA that is still open. On top of that sits oil, with Brent at its highest since April after a weekend of strikes on Saudi Arabia.

The 2-year ended at **4.686%** (single-source figure ⚠️). December gold closed at **$4,007.9** (−4.1% on the week despite the Gulf: real yields and a December Fed weigh). The S&P 500 ended at **7,623.68** (−0.5% on the week) and the Dow fell 1.2% on Friday. December Brent settled at **$116.72** (+3.1% on Friday and +6.2% on the week, highest since April) and WTI at **$103.27**.

It was a week of crosses and risk premia, not the Fed (which was in its blackout). Canada CPI came in below expectations (3.1% y/y vs 3.2%; median 1.9% and trim 1.8%) and yet the BoC was repriced toward a near-certain hike, as markets gave more weight to Macklem and to the Bank's own surveys. UK CPI came in hot (3.5% vs 3.4%; services 3.7%) without helping sterling. France took over on Thursday: the 10-year bond touched approx. 5.14%, the highest since 2002, and the OAT–Bund spread reached **174 bp** at the Europe session close (with an intraday high of approx. 178 bp). On Friday the spread closed the Europe session between **approx. 166 and 170 bp** depending on the source ⚠️, and after that close Moody's **kept France at Aa3**, assuming a 2027 budget that cuts the deficit (projected at 5.2% of GDP). That was a relief versus the downgrade much of the market expected.

## BoC: the meeting most able to surprise

Markets (OIS approx. 80–87%, prediction markets approx. 82%) have run ahead of economists: the consensus of one of the major surveys is a hold, and another is split, with slightly more than half expecting a hike ⚠️. September CPI came in below expectations and yet the odds went from approx. 30–35% to approx. 80% in one week, as markets gave more weight to Macklem (who flagged an upward revision to the inflation forecast) and to the Bank's surveys. Washington also helps: the partial rollback of tariffs on Canadian products takes effect on October 30.

- **A 25 bp hike with neutral guidance** ("one and see"): this is what is priced. December (approx. 52–60%) would fall toward approx. 40% and CAD could **weaken despite the hike**.
- **A hike with hawkish guidance** (a Report with higher inflation on energy and a line about "further tightening needed"): December at 65–70% or more and a firm CAD.
- **A hold:** the biggest surprise of the week. Approx. 20 bp of immediate repricing and CAD under pressure.

**Threshold for December:** the statement's line on whether more tightening is needed and the Report's inflation projection, with WTI above $100. Friday's August GDP will validate the tone or not.

## Australia: the CPI that defines the RBA

The RBA on November 3 is the least defined G10 meeting: approx. 56% in futures and approx. 68% in prediction markets ⚠️. The quarterly headline (1.3% expected) is inflated by energy; the Bank looks at the **trimmed mean**.

- **Hawkish:** quarterly trim of 1.0% or more (or monthly m/m of 0.4% or more) → November above approx. 80% and AUD supported.
- **In line:** trim of approx. 0.8–0.9% → November stays "likely" (approx. 60–70%), with no clear direction for AUD.
- **Dovish:** trim of 0.7% or less → November back below approx. 45% and AUD under pressure.

Bullock speaks 15 minutes before, and the RBNZ decides 30 minutes after: **AUD/NZD** packs two events into half an hour.

## RBNZ: the hike is done, the guidance is not

The hike to 3.00% is all but priced (a large majority of economists expect it). Q3 CPI came in slightly soft (0.7% q/q vs 0.8%; non-tradables 1.0%), which gives the Bank grounds not to commit to December. **Asymmetry:** NZD only gains on guidance that lifts December from approx. 56% to 70% or more (some analysts see a final hike to 3.25%). A "this is enough" message or a lower projected OCR path would weaken it.

## UK: a Budget with volatility asleep

There is no numeric consensus; the components matter more than the headline: (1) the **fiscal headroom** against the rule; (2) the gilt issuance volume; (3) whether there are concentrated tax rises (banks, wealth) or credible spending cuts; (4) the fiscal watchdog's growth and inflation scenario.

- **Negative for sterling:** tax rises that hurt growth or more issuance. The warning sign is the 10-year gilt rising from **5.369%** (Friday's New York close) **while sterling falls at the same time**, the 2022 fiscal-premium pattern.
- **Positive:** discipline with no new taxes and more headroom. Gilts relieved and sterling stable or higher.
- Sterling's implied event volatility jumped to approx. 16% when the date was announced and has slipped back to approx. 10% (single-source figure ⚠️). That complacency makes a large reaction more likely if there is a surprise.

The Budget does not change the November BoE (approx. 95–97%) unless a shock unanchors gilts, but it can move December (approx. 35–47% ⚠️).

## Fed: no projections, so everything rests on Warsh

This meeting brings no projections (they come in March, June, September and December), so the statement and the press conference carry everything. A hold is priced at approx. 88–94% ⚠️ (futures show roughly twice the hike probability that prediction markets do) and December at approx. 75–80%.

- **Components:** the language on additional adjustments; any mention of financial conditions (Thursday's Treasury rally, AI-linked credit); and dissents (one in favor of hiking would be hawkish).
- **Validates December** (more work to do on inflation, nothing ruled out): December holds at approx. 80% or more, the 2-year moves back toward approx. 4.75% and the dollar firms, especially against EUR and JPY.
- **Leaves it open** (data dependence with emphasis on market volatility): December toward approx. 65–70% and a broadly weaker dollar.
- **Thursday's PCE:** comes after Warsh. A core of 0.4% m/m or more would lift December to approx. 85% or more; 0.2% or less, toward approx. 70%. In **GDP**, the headline (3.0–3.2% ⚠️) matters less than **final sales to private domestic purchasers** (prior 2.8%) and quarterly core PCE (prior 3.3%): GDP inflated by inventories with final sales at 2% or less is not hawkish; a deflator of 3.5% or more is.
- **Tuesday:** in durables, what matters is **non-defense capital goods ex-aircraft** (business capex). Capex at 0% or less alongside housing at its lows would support the cooling story; 1% or more pushes against it. In the Conference Board, the labor differential and 12-month inflation expectations.

## ECB and the euro area: decision done, Friday decides December

A hold is all but certain. December cooled from approx. 90% in the middle of last week to approx. 69–80% ⚠️ on French stress. The dilemma is stagflationary: high CPI in France, Spain and Germany would cement December, while weak GDP (euro area 0.2% q/q expected) could delay it, though not by much.

- **Lagarde:** if she mentions fragmentation or the protection tools, the French spread would compress (EUR higher). If she sticks to "the data" and October inflation comes in at or above consensus, December would return to 80% or more.
- **Friday's combination:** inflation at or above consensus **with** GDP at or below = stagflation; December odds rise, but the euro does **not** benefit (the French spread widens). Inflation at or below consensus with GDP at or above is the only clean euro-positive scenario.
- Oil near $117 is more stagflationary for Europe than for the US, and that weighs on the euro even if the ECB hikes in December.

## Japan: the signal is in the dissents

A hold at approx. 97% and December at approx. 73–82% ⚠️. The Bank has picked up the pace since the summer but is reluctant to hike at consecutive meetings; several analysts expect at least one dissent in favor of a hike. A "boring" statement with a single dissent is what is priced. **Threshold:** two or more dissents, or an Outlook Report with underlying inflation revised clearly higher, would lift December to approx. 85% or more and support the yen. Tokyo CPI comes hours earlier: a core ex fresh food and energy at 3.0% or more (not falling) would strengthen the dissenters.

## Earnings: the cost of capital for AI

Alphabet and Microsoft (Wednesday) and Amazon (Thursday) report with 2026 capex guidance at record levels (approx. $195–205 billion at Alphabet and approx. $220 billion at Amazon). The issue for markets is not AI demand but **the cost of funding it**: cutting capex, delaying rollouts or funding with more equity would be bad news for stocks. That scenario would bring risk-off in Thursday's or Friday's Asia session, with JPY and CHF higher, AUD and NZD lower and a possible Treasury rally. Reaffirmed capex with visible monetization (cloud, Copilot) would sustain risk appetite. ExxonMobil and Chevron close the week with oil as the backdrop.
