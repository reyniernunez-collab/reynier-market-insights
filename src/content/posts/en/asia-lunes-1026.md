> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 26, 2026, 01:55 CT:** Monday's Asia session confirmed what Sunday's reopening hinted at: nobody wants to make a move ahead of a week with five central banks. The weekend attacks on Saudi Arabia added no premium to oil, which actually fell, and the rest of the market traded in tight ranges while waiting for Europe.

## Executive Summary

A night with no meaningful data (New Zealand is on holiday) and with headlines that, in another week, would have moved prices far more. Oil fell as the diplomatic track gained ground over the military one, US Treasuries recovered a little, and stocks in Tokyo and Seoul ended in the red, without panic.

In currencies, the only move with a story of its own was the **euro**, which is leaning on relief over France. Everything else, the yen included, is hovering around Friday's New York session close. My read: the market is keeping its powder dry for Wednesday and Thursday.

## Analysis

### Oil: diplomacy outweighs the missiles

**December Brent** is trading at approx. **$114.9**, about **$1.8 below** Friday's settlement ($116.72, the highest since late April), and touched approx. **$114.0** overnight. US crude is down about $2, at approx. **$101.3**, from Friday's $103.27.

The striking part is the backdrop. Yemen's rebels again claimed attacks on Saudi infrastructure, this time on pumping stations of a major pipeline and on Riyadh's airport, and the Saudi-led coalition reported interceptions and the destruction of launchers in Yemen. But there is **no official confirmation of damage to oil facilities**, and without it the market won't pay more premium. Diplomacy added to that: senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan meet Iran in Tehran today, after several days of regional mediation. Part of the drop also looks like profit-taking after a week of strong gains.

Two caveats. First: if real damage to Saudi facilities were confirmed, this picture would change within minutes. Second, a technical one: the December contract expires at the end of the month and the next one trades about $5 cheaper, so the contract roll should not be mistaken for a price drop.

### Bonds and central banks: almost everything is still priced

The **US 10-year yield** is down about **3 bp, at approx. 4.97%**, after closing Friday right around 5%. The **2-year** is down less, at approx. **4.67%**. Japan's 10-year yield eases to approx. 2.78% and Australia's is down about 4–5 bp.

Expectations barely moved from Friday:

- **Fed:** no change this week (the odds of a hike range from approx. 5% to 12% depending on the measure) and a December hike at around **77–80%**. Friday's data back that up: US business activity stayed strong in October and companies raised prices at the fastest pace since 2022.
- **Canada and New Zealand:** hikes heavily priced for this week (approx. 81–85% in Canada; approx. 85–92% in New Zealand).
- **Japan:** the prediction market cut the odds of a December hike and now converges with swaps at approx. **74–75%**. There is speculation that the central bank will toughen its assessment of underlying inflation this week, but that is only an unconfirmed possibility.
- **Australia:** a November hike sits between approx. **62% and 69%** depending on the market you look at, a wide spread ahead of Tuesday's inflation data.

### Stocks: red in Tokyo and Seoul, no drama

At the Asia session close, the **Nikkei** is lower, though the size depends on the reading: earlier hours pointed to a small drop (approx. −0.1/−0.2%) and the latest data put it at approx. **−0.7/−0.8%**. The **Kospi** is down approx. **1%** (between −0.9% and −1.3% depending on the closing basis). **Hong Kong** is up approx. **0.5%** and **Shanghai** is roughly flat.

In the US, **S&P 500** futures are flat and **Nasdaq 100** futures are down approx. **0.3%**: the small bounce they opened with on Sunday faded within the first hour and a half and never came back.

### Currencies: the euro breathes, the rest waits

Indicative readings versus Friday's New York session close:

- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1215** (about +13/+15 pips), after a high of approx. 1.1226. **EUR/GBP** approx. **0.8386**, near the night's high: the cleanest reflection of relief over France.
- **USD/JPY** approx. **157.0**, after trading between approx. 156.6 and 157.2. No direction: the yen didn't act as a haven despite red stock screens. **EUR/JPY** rises to approx. 176.1.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.337**, essentially flat, with Wednesday's UK Budget as the week's big risk.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.7025** (about −8/−10 pips): the rates market is gearing up for Tuesday's inflation data, but the currency isn't following. **NZD/USD** approx. 0.570, with its local market closed.
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.401**, almost unchanged despite the drop in crude. **USD/CHF** approx. 0.824.
- **DXY** approx. **101.7**, slightly lower.

**Gold** is up approx. **0.2%**, at approx. **$4,018** (futures), after trading between approx. $3,991 and $4,033. It still isn't working as a major haven with real yields this high.

### China, France and Brazil: the background headlines

- **China** opened the Party's plenary meeting (October 26–29), with the president presenting the work report. The central bank set the yuan at its strongest level since February 2023 and the onshore currency is at its highest since June 2022: a sign of stability the market reads as intentional.
- **France:** the rating agency kept the country's rating (Aa3) on Friday, after the Europe session close, on condition that the 2027 budget cuts the deficit (projected at 5.2% of GDP). French bond futures rose at the start of Asia, according to early readings. The spread over Germany ended last week at its widest since 2011, around 166–170 bp depending on the measure; today we'll see whether it starts to narrow.
- **Brazil:** with the count complete, President Lula was re-elected with approx. 51.8% versus approx. 48.2%. According to some reports, the defeated candidate does not recognize the result; that is a risk for the real more than for the major currencies.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If the French spread starts to narrow at the open**, tonight's relief in the euro would have a foundation; if it stays at its widest despite the agency's decision, the euro could give back its gains against the pound and the dollar.
- **If the Tehran meeting produces signs of de-escalation**, oil could keep shedding premium and ease the pressure on bonds; a headline confirming damage in Saudi Arabia would do the opposite, with the yen and the euro among the most exposed.
- **If the German Ifo surprises**, especially in expectations, it would be the week's first data point able to move the euro on its own.
- **If the US 2-year auction meets weak demand**, the short end would again reflect the idea of a Fed that isn't done, right before Wednesday's meeting.
