> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 9, 2026, 15:30 CT:** Friday resolved the Canada question in a dovish direction and left the rest almost intact. Employment fell hard, the Canadian dollar hit an 18-month low and Wall Street advanced toward records with earnings season in view. Crude settled above $100 and the Treasury cash market is closed Monday for Columbus Day.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, Canada's employment print (−**68.3k** against a consensus of +7k to +9.2k depending on the source ⚠️) was the move of the day: USD/CAD jumped from ~1.4232 to ~1.4293 in a single five-minute candle and touched **1.4298–1.4302**, 18-month highs, before closing around **1.4254–1.4261** ⚠️. Michigan came in line where it matters (inflation expectations), so the Fed barely repriced. Stocks advanced toward record highs on earnings optimism despite energy and yields. Brent settled at **$104.72** and WTI at **$91.85**. The DXY closed at **102.23** and the 10-year at **5.24%**.

## Analysis

### Canada: composition explains the hit

The headline (−68.3k) is harsh, but the composition is harsher: full-time **−35.4k**, part-time **−32.9k**; private sector **+24.1k** and public **−70k**. Unemployment holds at **6.5%** (consensus 6.5%, prior 6.4%) only because participation falls to **64.8%** (prior 65.0%). Permanent wages rise to **2.3% y/y** (prior 2.0%). It is the second straight monthly decline.

With that print, the odds of a Bank of Canada hike on October 28 drop from ~32% to a rough range of **~15–25%** depending on the source ⚠️. December is also cut, but permanent wages and positive private hiring keep a full pivot to a long pause off the table. Macklem speaks Wednesday and Thursday next week: his tone on labor slack versus wages will be the next test.

### Michigan: in line where it matters

October preliminary sentiment lands at **46.3** (consensus ~47.6–47.8 ⚠️; prior 48.1), with current conditions at 44.7 and expectations at 47.3. What the Fed watches — **1-year inflation expectations at 4.7%** and **5-year at 3.5%** — comes in line with consensus. Result: Fed October stays around **~17–20%** hike odds depending on the source ⚠️, and December still carries a high chance of at least one hike (~80–85% ⚠️). Michigan does not change the script; Wednesday's CPI can.

### Stocks: toward records with earnings season ahead

At the New York close, stocks advanced toward record highs. S&P 500 cash ended at **7,811.54** (+1.15%) and S&P futures hovered around ~7,859.75. The index wrap tone: investors positioned for another strong corporate earnings season, overcoming concerns about elevated energy prices and Treasury yields. Most S&P 500 names gained, though semiconductors kept underperforming. The S&P 500 has posted seven consecutive quarters of double-digit earnings growth; markets are looking for evidence that momentum can hold despite higher borrowing costs and persistent inflation.

### Oil, dollar and bonds

December Brent settled at **$104.72** (+0.44) and November WTI at **$91.85** (+0.36). Isaias shut in ~71–72% of Gulf production before landfall; the Russian diesel announcement trimmed some premium intraday, but settlement stayed above $100. The dollar posted its longest weekly winning streak since early 2025: the DXY closed at **102.23**. The 10-year Treasury ended at **5.24%** and the 2-year around 4.79–4.80%.

New York session FX closes: EUR/USD **1.1201**; GBP/USD **1.3231**; USD/JPY **158.26–158.31**; USD/CHF **0.8300–0.8301**; AUD/USD **0.6982–0.6985**; NZD/USD **0.5609–0.5613**; EUR/GBP **0.8461**; EUR/CAD **1.5966**. Spot gold hovered around **~$4,194** (December futures ~$4,216 ⚠️). In Europe, the OAT–Bund spread closed at **132.5 bp**, tactical relief from the early-October peak near ~147 bp, but no fiscal resolution.

Treasury cash trading is closed Monday for Columbus Day; US equities are open. Canada is on Thanksgiving: thin CAD liquidity.
