## Executive Summary

Pre-NFP Friday: the regime is _duration stress + safe havens_. 10-year UST **5.34% → ~5.25%**; Fed **Oct hold 74% / hike 26%**; **Dec hike 73%**. Spots: EUR **1.1251**, GBP **1.321**, USD/JPY **157.71**, AUD **0.6935**, USD/CHF **0.8285**, USD/CAD **1.422**, Brent **102**, WTI **92.4**, gold **4,146**. The BoJ was less hawkish than feared: no support for an October hike or for 50 bp. The NFP (08:30 ET) decides whether October comes back to life or dies.

## Detailed Analysis

### Monetary policy

Effective Fed funds 3.877%. Oct "no change" **74%** / +6.6 bp implied; Dec hike **73.4%** and ~25 bp cumulative by Dec (down from ~30 bp). The soft PCE and Williams ("no rush") trimmed October; December is still alive. ECB: Oct hold **84.9%**, Dec hike falls to **70%** (from 93%): French fiscal stress takes positive beta away from the euro despite a more hawkish ECB. BoE: Nov hike **76.8%** (from 84%); Dec is now a coin flip. BoC: Oct hold **65%**, Dec hike 72%. BoJ: Oct hold **85.7%** (OIS ~14–20% hike odds), Dec hike **78.5%**. RBA anchored at 4.60% (Nov hold 70%, Dec hold 94%). RBNZ Oct hike falls to **56%**. SNB Dec hold **85%**.

### Data, cycle and confidence

September's NFP is the only print that can reopen October. FactSet/MUFG put the median at ~90k (range 60–130k) and unemployment at 4.1%. A "stable" number shouldn't reprice the front end much; a clear miss kills what's left of October; a beat with rising wages revives Dec and the long end of the Treasury curve. PMIs and AI capex keep supporting the nominal cycle, but the term premium (the toughest quarter for Treasuries in 32 years) is now the valuation driver, not growth.

### Geopolitics and energy

Brent 102 / WTI 92.4 with 30d IV ~13.4–13.5% (1SD Brent 88–116). The Gulf still has a military build-up and Hormuz as a premium, not a closure. Crude is no longer just risk-off beta: at 102 it squeezes European and Japanese margins and feeds the inflation swap term.

### Cross-asset correlations

60/90/120d (the 30d link didn't return a readable matrix). On 60d: CL-EUR **0.77**, CL-GBP **0.79**, EUR-DXY **−0.96**, JPY-DXY **0.68**, EUR-GBP **0.83**, AUD-ES **−0.69**. On 90d the oil-beta fades (CL-EUR **0.22**): the crude-G10 coupling is a short-window story. Gold-silver is still >0.95. The 17:00–03:00 session chart shows USD giving ground at the end, CHF spiking (European safe-haven flow) and JPY volatile with no clean trend.

### Balance, flows and positioning

The euro is heading for its worst week in about four months (~−1.2% vs USD) on France + the Fed. CHF absorbs the European carry being unwound (EUR/CHF had its worst session since April 2025 in the previous session). Mainland China is closed until Wednesday: less Asian beta and less yuan flow.

### Sentiment

Asia lower (MSCI APAC ex-Japan ~−0.5%; Nikkei −0.7% intraday but up ~3% on the week). Stoxx +0.8/0.9% in Europe with US futures higher (S&P +0.4%, Nasdaq +0.8%) and the S&P still on track for its worst week since August. "Safer" bonds in demand after the 5.34% peak.
