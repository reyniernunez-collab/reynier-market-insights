> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 3, 2026, 02:04 CT:** Tuesday's Asia session had three protagonists, and none of them was the dollar. Australia's central bank raised rates, but in a tone the market read as the end of the cycle; the semiconductor selloff kept hitting the region's most tech-heavy markets; and the Strait of Hormuz returned to the headlines after a limited US strike on Iran. All of this on the day the US votes.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves a more defensive market than on Monday. **The region's tech-heavy stock markets fall sharply**, **crude recovers part of yesterday's losses** and the **Australian dollar is the hardest-hit currency** after a rate hike that sounded like a farewell. At the other end, the **Swiss franc** is the strongest, lifted by local inflation and its safe-haven role.

My read: Australia's message is "one more hike and, probably, wait", and that shifts the balance between the two Antipodean currencies, because New Zealand still has a very likely hike in December. In the background, Hormuz is a reminder that the diplomatic track with Iran is not closed, but not guaranteed either. With the midterm elections under way and the US job openings data ahead, the dollar has no clear driver of its own.

## Analysis

### Australia: a hike, but no more promises

Australia's central bank raised its cash rate by a quarter point to **4.85%**: the fifth hike of the year and the highest level in 18 years. The decision was priced in; what surprised was how it came:

- **A split vote:** six members in favor and **three in favor of holding**.
- **A statement without promises:** despite sharply raising its inflation forecasts, which it now sees above 3% until late 2027, the text stopped hinting that more hikes will be needed.
- **A cautious press conference:** according to reported remarks from Governor Bullock (single source), her message was that the sensible thing now is to wait and watch the data, with no guidance on next steps.

The market reacted as it often does when a hike is already priced in and the message disappoints: **the currency fell once the decision was out**. **AUD/USD** briefly touched 0.70, fell to a low of approx. **0.6971** and now hovers around **0.6985**, between 11 and 13 pips below the New York session close. Against the **New Zealand dollar** the drop is larger: AUD/NZD loses about **44 pips** to approx. 1.241, after touching approx. 1.2385. The odds of a second Australian hike in December collapse to approx. **5–8%**, from approx. 17–24% before the meeting; measures differ, and one of them, with thin liquidity, shows the sharpest cut.

### China reins in the yuan

Beijing also weighed on China-linked currencies. The Chinese central bank set the yuan reference rate considerably weaker than expected and raised banks' foreign-currency reserve requirements, aiming to slow the currency's appreciation. The yuan fell as much as approx. 0.4% and snapped a 13-session winning streak, the longest since 1996 (single-source figure). At the same time, authorities encouraged financial institutions to make more use of stock-support tools, which did not prevent losses in Chinese equities.

### Stocks: semiconductors still in charge

The US semiconductor selloff stretched to a third session, with cumulative losses of approx. 12% in the sector index, and the Nasdaq 100 is now down approx. 10% from its peak. In Asia, that showed up in the most tech-heavy markets:

- **Korea** led the losses: the Kospi is down between approx. **3.6% and 4%**, depending on the time and the measure, with its two big memory-chip makers falling more than 4%.
- **Taiwan** lost approx. 3.6% and the broad regional index approx. 1.2% (both single-source figures).
- **Australia** held up with a slight gain, and **Japan** was closed for a holiday.

**S&P 500 futures** are down approx. **0.25%** and **Nasdaq 100** futures are barely moving. After the New York close, Apple and Palantir beat expectations, but their shares fell on guidance.

### Oil: Qeshm halts the slide

The US struck a facility on Iran's **Qeshm** island after a US drone was shot down. Washington describes it as a limited, defensive action, not the start of a new campaign. Messages from Tehran said a deal remains far off (single source), although contacts through mediators continue.

**January Brent** rebounds approx. **1.7%** to around **$106.5–106.6**, after a high of approx. $107.2. **December WTI** rises by the same proportion to approx. **$98**. The bounce does not erase Monday's steep drop. This time the **Canadian dollar** strengthens with crude: USD/CAD falls about 22 pips to approx. 1.396.

### Switzerland: inflation above expectations

Just before the Europe open, Swiss October **CPI** came in at **1.5% year on year**, against 1.2% expected, with 0.2% month on month when no change was expected. Core inflation, however, was in line at 0.5%. These are single-source figures at this hour. **USD/CHF** fell to approx. **0.8157**, about 27–28 pips lower, with a low of approx. 0.8148: the franc is the strongest currency of the night. Since this inflation is mostly energy-driven, it remains to be seen whether the Swiss central bank gives it much weight.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Australia's message settles in** and no one reopens December, the Aussie would likely remain the weak currency against the kiwi, ahead of New Zealand's jobs data this afternoon.
- **If Swiss inflation is confirmed** and Swiss rates react at the Europe open, the franc could hold its strength; if the market sees it as a purely energy-driven effect, part of the move could unwind.
- **If new incidents hit Hormuz**, crude could extend its rebound; verifiable diplomatic progress would push it back toward Monday's downtrend.
- **If US job openings (9:00 AM CT) come in firm**, they would reinforce a Fed hike in December and could slow the dollar's softness. With the elections under way, liquidity may be patchy in the afternoon.
