> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 22, 2026, 08:18 CT:** The Europe session confirmed what yesterday only hinted at: France is no longer just a euro-sterling story, it is a euro-against-everything story. With the Gulf flaring again and tech lower, this post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the focus was the Gulf sinking the Kospi, Brent nearing $112 and the yen as the haven. The Europe session added the missing piece: **the French spread surged to its widest since 2011 and the euro, for the first time in days, gave ground against the dollar**.

The rest of the central bank map barely moved: hikes by the Bank of England, the Bank of Canada and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand remain priced, and the Fed has almost no hike priced for October. My read: today FX is driven by two risk premiums, France and oil, on a risk-off day.

## Analysis

### France: the spread goes from awkward to center stage

France's 10-year spread over Germany went from about 159 bp at Wednesday's Europe close to a range of approx. **168–178 bp** this morning (⚠️ readings vary by time). All of them agree it is the widest since 2011. The French yield reached approx. **5.14%** and, for the first time in more than a decade, is above the US Treasury of the same maturity. Contagion to Belgium and Italy shows up in a single report (⚠️), so I treat it with caution.

France's rating review comes Friday. The market expects a downgrade; the surprises would be a deeper cut or no change.

### The euro finally pays

EUR/USD slipped to approx. **1.1195–1.1200** (−0.3%). One nuance: part of the euro's resilience until yesterday came from the gradual unwinding of very crowded positions in US tech and in the dollar. The front end of the US curve still prices about 75 bp of Fed hikes over the next year (a long horizon, separate from what is priced for December). If the tech selloff turns into a downward revision of those expectations, the dollar could correct more broadly and slow the euro's slide.

### Gulf and oil: event mode

New attacks on ships and mines near Hormuz, threats against US bases in the region, a drone strike on a Saudi oil field (⚠️ the casualty figure comes from a single report) and the ordered departure of some US diplomatic staff in Saudi Arabia. Brent rose approx. 2.8% to the **$112** area, with a high of approx. $112.7 in the Europe session, and has since hovered just below without extending.

### Risk-off and havens

- **Equities:** tech earnings that disappointed against very high expectations dragged Asia (Korea −3.5%, Japan −2.7%, Hong Kong −3%) and Europe (Euro Stoxx 50 −1.6%). Nasdaq futures are down approx. 1.8%.
- **Bonds:** the US 10-year yield is down approx. 7–8 bp, to approx. 5.05–5.08% (⚠️ depending on the time of the reading).
- **Yen and franc:** USD/JPY falls approx. 0.75% to approx. **156.9** and USD/CHF approx. 0.3%.
- **Dollar:** the index is flat (approx. **101.8**): euro weakness offsets yen strength.
- **Gold:** down approx. 2% (near **$4,075**) despite the Gulf and lower yields. My read, not a fact: selling to cover losses elsewhere.

### UK and Switzerland

The UK industrial orders survey came in well below expectations (−32 vs −20) and sterling slips with the rest of risk (approx. 1.3365). In Switzerland, the summary of the central bank's last meeting sees policy as appropriate and no longer sees a need for greater readiness to intervene in FX (⚠️ single report).

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **France:** if the spread keeps widening past today's high, the euro tends to extend its slide against the dollar and the yen. If it stabilizes, a partial euro rebound after the Europe close is common. A move back below approx. 165 bp could trigger short-covering in the euro.
2. **US jobless claims (7:30 AM CT):** 200k expected (prior 199k), continuing claims approx. 1.72 million. Continuing claims are what matter: a weak print could trim the odds of a December Fed hike; a strong one would undo part of the drop in yields. It doesn't change the October meeting.
3. **Oil:** a fresh high on Gulf headlines would support the yen and the franc and weigh on the euro through the energy channel. A clear drop below approx. $109.4 would suggest the move was a false breakout.
4. **Tech and the dollar:** if the Nasdaq futures slide deepens and drags Fed expectations lower, the dollar could weaken across the board and cushion the euro's fall.
