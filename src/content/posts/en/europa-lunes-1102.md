> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 2, 2026, 08:15 CT:** Monday's Europe session extended what began in Asia: crude keeps falling on Iran diplomacy and the dollar slips across the board. The striking part is that US yields aren't following. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was crude falling on the Iran talks, tech catching its breath and the dollar slipping ahead of the Australian central bank. **The Europe session didn't change the script, it widened it**: WTI lost $100, the dollar hit its low of the day mid-morning in Europe, and the euro was the big winner.

My read: **the dollar's weakness is about flows and political premium, not Fed expectations**. Cheaper oil helps energy importers like the euro area, and the market is starting to price a split Congress or Democratic control after tomorrow's elections, which it reads as dollar-negative because of fiscal friction. But the US 2-year yield is rising and the odds of a December Fed hike haven't moved. That leaves the dollar exposed to a bounce if today's ISM comes in hot.

## Analysis

### The dollar slips, but yields don't follow

- **Dollar index:** near **101.6**, approx. 0.3 points below Friday's New York session close (⚠️ the exact figure depends on which closing reference is used). It touched approx. 101.43 mid-morning in Europe as crude broke below $109, then recovered some as oil's slide stalled.
- **US yields:** the 2-year rises to approx. **4.61%** (just over 1 basis point) and the 10-year is flat at approx. **4.93%**. That's the key signal: the dollar isn't falling because the market expects less from the Fed.
- **Midterm elections (tomorrow):** prediction markets give approx. 72–73% to a Democratic Senate and approx. 94% to a Democratic House. The president said he'll veto everything if Democrats win, which reinforces the gridlock read.
- **Stocks:** US futures bounce after last week's tech selloff, approx. 0.3–0.4% for the S&P 500 and 0.6–0.8% for the Nasdaq versus Friday's settlement (⚠️ sources differ on the size). In Europe, the regional index ended roughly flat, with energy lower (⚠️ sources differ on the sign); Spain stood out to the upside.

### The euro leads despite the data

- **EUR/USD:** up about **50–57 pips to approx. 1.121**, with a high near 1.124 mid-morning in Europe. Against the yen it gains even more: EUR/JPY is up about 126 pips to approx. 177.0.
- **Why it's rising:** cheaper oil and gas ease pressure on the euro area, and an ECB Governing Council member said the question is no longer whether more hikes are needed but how many. A December hike is priced at approx. 80–84%.
- **What didn't help:** German retail sales fell **2.0%** on the month (a rise was expected), and on Friday a rating agency put France's rating on negative outlook. The French 10-year spread over Germany sits at approx. **165 bp**.
- **Final manufacturing PMIs:** euro area **52.7** (52.4 in the flash), Germany 54.2, France 49.5, Spain 52.2 and Italy 49.4. No big surprises; Spain up and Italy down.

### Strong franc, lagging sterling, weak yen and Aussie

- **USD/CHF:** the franc is the second-strongest currency; the pair is down about **60 pips to approx. 0.819**, the low of the day.
- **GBP/USD:** up only about 28–30 pips to approx. **1.342**; sterling lags the euro (EUR/GBP approx. 0.836). Gilts were the best bond market of the day, with the UK 10-year down about 5.5 bp. Thursday's Bank of England hike is all but priced (approx. 97–99%).
- **USD/JPY:** up about 33–36 pips to approx. **157.9**, and the 158.0 area capped it again. The yen isn't joining the dollar's slide because the US 2-year yield is rising. Japan is on holiday tomorrow, so liquidity will be thinner in the Asia session.
- **AUD/USD:** the day's worst, down about 25 pips to approx. **0.700**. The RBA decides tonight and a hike to 4.85% is priced at approx. 80–90% (⚠️ sources disagree). What isn't priced is a second hike: December sits at approx. 17–23%.
- **USD/CAD:** almost unchanged (approx. 1.397). Cheap oil weighs on the Canadian dollar and the weak dollar offsets it.

### Oil and gold

**January Brent** drops about $4.4 (approx. 3.9%) to near **$109**, with a low of approx. $108.1, and **December WTI** lost $100 (approx. $99.8). The driver is diplomatic: Iranian negotiators left Doha without a breakdown, a draft proposal is circulating and another meeting is possible. There's noise the other way: Iran says its demands haven't changed and Russia says it's worried about signs of new military action (⚠️ both single-source). **Gold** rises only approx. 0.3–0.4%, near $3,900–3,910: with risk appetite back, there's little demand for a haven.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **ISM in line and no Iran news (most likely):** December Fed odds would stay at approx. 71–74% and the dollar would stay soft on pre-election flows, but without fresh momentum; the yen would keep sitting out and the Aussie would stay defensive ahead of the RBA.
2. **Hot ISM (headline 56 or higher with prices paid 80 or higher, or firm employment):** December Fed odds and the 2-year yield would rise; the dollar would win back the day's losses, especially against the euro and the yen.
3. **Soft ISM (headline 53 or lower with prices 75 or lower):** December odds would slip toward approx. 65%, yields would fall and the dollar would extend its drop; the yen might finally join in.
4. **Iran:** a verifiable deal would push oil lower, hurt the Canadian dollar and help the euro and the yen; a breakdown in talks would send crude back above $112 and reverse the day.
5. **Elections:** if the market unwinds the Democratic-sweep premium before tomorrow, the dollar could bounce without any data.
