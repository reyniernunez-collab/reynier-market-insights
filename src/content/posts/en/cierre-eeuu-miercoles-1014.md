> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 14, 2026, 3:50 PM CT:** The inflation number the whole market was waiting for wasn't a disaster in the headline, but it was in the detail. Core rose a tenth more than expected, and in a market already nervous about tech, that tenth was enough to break years of calm in the S&P 500. Chipmakers paid the bill, the dollar regained ground and the Fed's October meeting is back in play.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 finished at **7,718.94** (−1.11%), its first drop of at least 1% in more than six years. The Nasdaq closed at **26,692.87** (−1.88%) and the Dow held up at **51,621.48** (−0.09%), which makes it clear where the selling was: tech and, above all, semiconductors. The SOX lost **4.30%** to **11,684.38**. The VIX rose to **16.87**, about 10% above Tuesday.

The trigger was September CPI. Headline rose **0.6%** month over month (in line) and **3.7%** year over year (a tenth above expectations), pushed by gasoline. What changed the day was core: **+0.3%** m/m and **2.6%** y/y, versus the expected 0.2% and 2.5%. That's the reading the Fed watches, and it was exactly the one the market feared.

## Analysis

### CPI: the tenth that reopens October

Going into the release, the prevailing view was simple: energy would inflate the headline, but core needed to confirm that price pressure wasn't spreading. That was the condition for the Fed's patient message, which calmed Wall Street on Tuesday and was repeated by a governor early this morning, to stay intact. Core at +0.3% breaks that condition, though without reaching the extreme scenario of +0.4% or more.

The repricing fits what I laid out in the preview for a restrictive core. A 25 bp hike at the **October 28** meeting, which stood around **20–22%** in the morning, moves to approx. **30–31%**, with approx. 68–69% still expecting the Fed to stand pat. For **December**, the market prices approx. **78–79%** for a 25 bp hike ⚠️ (per-meeting and cumulative measures are not one-to-one comparable). October is still not the base case, but it's no longer a minor tail.

One important nuance: this week's expensive oil isn't in this CPI. It weighs on the October and November data, which is why the energy-inflation risk still points more to December than to this month's meeting.

### Stocks: tech loses its cushion

The Asia session had already shown signs of fatigue in the AI trade, and the Europe session offered no clear support. In New York, with CPI on the table, selling concentrated in the names most sensitive to rates and valuations. Chipmakers are down around 20% from their June high, with three worries at once: higher rates, stretched valuations and doubts about how much AI infrastructure really costs. Strain in the debt of some AI data-center companies, now trading at distressed levels, reinforces that reading.

The contrast with the Dow (−0.09%) shows this wasn't a broad flight from risk but a hard rotation out of tech. On the corporate front, Apple is preparing to launch its AI platform in mainland China after a regulatory delay, but it wasn't enough to change the sector's tone.

### Rates and the dollar: calm bonds, firm dollar

The odd part of the day was the bond market. With hotter core, you'd expect a jump in the 10-year, and there wasn't one: it closed at approx. **5.19%**, about 2.5 bp below Tuesday's New York close (5.217%). My read is that the pressure went into risk assets rather than the long end of the curve, and that yields were already at levels the market itself sees as restrictive.

The dollar did react. The DXY rose to approx. **102.5** and EUR/USD fell to the **1.1154–1.1158** area, more than giving back the Asia session bounce and moving away from 1.12. Sterling ended at approx. **1.3188**, USD/JPY at approx. **158.3** and USD/CAD at approx. **1.4237**. AUD/USD, which had held 0.70 after China's data, lost that level and closed at approx. **0.697**: with a firm dollar and risk under pressure, commodity support wasn't enough. Gold fell to approx. **$4,121** (−1.3%), once again without a safe-haven role when the dollar and real rates are in charge.

### Oil: high, but in the background

Brent ended the New York session at approx. **$106** (+0.3%) and WTI at approx. **$95.1** (+1%). The escalation that strengthened in the Europe session remains in place, but today it wasn't the market's engine: CPI took the spotlight. What matters is that crude holds this area while the market reprices the Fed, because that feeds inflation expectations for the coming months.

### Scenarios

- **If Thursday's data (PPI and retail sales) confirm price pressure and firm consumer spending:** October odds could keep climbing from the current approx. 30% and the dollar would keep its momentum; tech would remain very exposed.
- **If PPI is moderate and spending cools:** the market could read today's CPI as one month of noise; October would drift back toward 20% and the euro would get some breathing room.
- **If TSMC delivers solid results:** that could stabilize semiconductors, although the rates-and-valuation problem won't disappear with a single report.
- **Oil:** as long as Brent stays above $105.5, energy risk keeps the pressure on December alive; a clear loss of that area would take one argument away from the hawks.
