**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

Dallas, Texas — Nov 27, 2026, 15:45 CT

**Executive summary:** Friday's New York session was cut short for Black Friday: stocks closed at 12:00 CT and bonds at 13:00 CT, with thin liquidity. Even so, the rebound was broad: the S&P 500 rose 1.6%, the Nasdaq 2.3% and the Dow 1.2%, driven by chipmakers and big tech; Nvidia barely moved higher. The S&P 500 posted its best week since April 2025, up approx. 5.6%. The dollar firmed against the euro, which is still weighed down by the very soft French and Spanish inflation released during the Europe session. The 10-year yield rose approx. 9 bp over two sessions and the VIX fell to 23.8. October's goods trade deficit widened to $149.5 billion, the largest since March 2025, as imports hit record highs on AI investment.

**Key levels (approx. 15:45 CT; stocks at the 12:00 CT early close):**

- **S&P 500:** 7,297.49 · bullish (+1.6%, best week since April 2025)
- **Nasdaq Composite:** 24,129.50 · bullish (+2.3%, chip rebound)
- **Dollar index:** 104.0 · mildly bullish (+0.3%, on euro weakness)
- **EUR/USD:** 1.0932–1.0936 · bearish (soft European inflation)
- **US 10-year yield:** 4.94% · bullish (approx. +9 bp since Wednesday)
- **Gold:** approx. $3,650–3,669 ⚠️ · bullish (readings differ across providers)

**Central banks:**

- **Fed (Dec):** hike approx. 98% priced (morning reading); upwardly revised growth and October inflation keep the bet alive.
- **ECB (Dec):** hike approx. 95%; lower French and Spanish inflation did not shift the consensus, and one Governing Council member said two hikes might fall short.
- **BoJ (Dec):** hike approx. 78%, higher despite softer Tokyo CPI; USD/JPY remains near 160, a sensitive area.
- **BoE (Dec):** hike approx. 86% ⚠️ (other markets see it considerably lower).

**Risks:**

1. **OPEC+ and the Gulf:** the group meets on Sunday and is expected to hold its quotas; a tanker damaged by a mine in Hormuz keeps a risk premium in oil. Brent prices differ widely across providers ⚠️.
2. **France's rating:** an agency reviews the country's rating tonight, after third-quarter GDP fell 0.2%; a change would move the euro at Monday's Asia open.
3. **China PMI (Sunday, 19:30 CT):** after the strong rally in Chinese stocks, a weak reading would cool the Australian dollar and copper.
4. **Liquidity and the yen:** this week's rebound came on holiday volume; full liquidity returns Monday, and USD/JPY above 160 would raise intervention risk.
