> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 6, 2026, 15:50 CT:** Wall Street finished off the relief that started in Europe. With earnings season a week away, the market is betting that companies can handle expensive energy and high rates, and the S&P 500 marked it with its first all-time high since August.

## Executive Summary

The S&P 500 closes at **7,818.93** (+0.58%), a new record. The Nasdaq 100 rises 0.48% to 31,224 and the Dow 0.49% to 51,521. Confidence in corporate earnings and the AI trade drive the move, while the 10-year Treasury yield pulls back from its highest levels since 2002. The VIX drops to **15.01** (−3.3%). The picture has caveats, though. Small caps don't follow (Russell 2000 −0.59%), Brent settles above $100 again, and the Fed officials who spoke today made it clear that inflation is still their problem.

## Analysis

### Stocks: a record with earnings on the horizon

The market is getting ahead of earnings season, which kicks off next week, on the view that companies can absorb higher energy and funding costs. AI is the engine again. Nvidia is closing in on a $6 trillion market value, and AMD gains 2.8% after CEO Lisa Su said she expects chip demand to stay very high for the next few years. On top of that, OpenAI is in talks with UAE funds, including MGX, to anchor a funding round of about $30 billion.

The warning sign is breadth. An S&P 500 record while the Russell 2000 falls 0.59% confirms the advance still hinges on megacap tech. Smaller companies are more sensitive to high rates, and with the 10-year above 5% they aren't joining in. Options this morning priced a ~**4%** 30-day move for S&P 500 futures and ~**5.8%** for Nasdaq 100 futures, so there's room for wide swings once earnings season gets going.

### Bonds: relief at the long end, pressure at the front

The 10-year closes around **5.27–5.29%**, 2 to 4 bp lower on the day, backing away from Monday's highest level since 2002. The French relief that tightened spreads in Europe and cheaper morning oil both helped. The front end tells a different story. The $58 billion 3-year note auction cleared at **4.932%**, the highest yield since May 2006. Demand was average (bid-to-cover of 2.62), with foreign buyers taking a little less than usual. There's no sign of stress, but it's a reminder of how expensive Treasury funding has become.

### Fed: tough talk, unchanged pricing

Schmid (Kansas City) called inflation "frustrating" and said the Fed still has work to do. Daly (San Francisco) acknowledged that AI, tariffs and energy could require further tightening. Neither moved the market. Our data puts October hold odds around **77%** (hike around 23–24%), with ~**27 bp** of hikes priced by December and ~**83%** odds of a December hike. The base case is still a pause now and a data-dependent call in December. Tomorrow's FOMC minutes are the next test of that view.

### Dollar, oil and gold: a quiet finish that doesn't change the backdrop

The dollar ends the day at two-day lows (DXY ~**101.86**) and EUR/USD around **1.126**. The euro is being held up by the French relief, not by a shift in rates, which still favor the dollar. Trump defended a "strong dollar" without any market reaction. USD/JPY sits around **158.2**, near the zone where intervention risk rises. In commodities, Brent settles at **$100.58** and WTI at **$89.44**, essentially flat, after wiping out the whole morning drop on fresh attacks and incidents around Hormuz and the Black Sea. Gold hovers near **$4,197**, just shy of $4,200.

- **6:30 PM CT, Japan wages (prior 4.3%, consensus 3.7%):** with USD/JPY near 158, close to the possible intervention zone.
