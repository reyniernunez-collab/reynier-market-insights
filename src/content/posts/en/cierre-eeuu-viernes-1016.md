> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 16, 2026, 3:42 PM CT:** The New York session closes the week with what Asia and Europe already hinted at: the AI financing question is back at center stage, and oil is escalating again. Hot import-price and industrial-production prints weren't enough to rescue tech or to give the dollar back what it lost after Warsh.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 finished at **7,659.44** (−1.1%), the Nasdaq Composite at **26,140.03** (−2.1%) and the Dow at **52,002.41** (−0.3%). The Nasdaq 100 fell **2.5%** and the SOX approx. **−3.8%**, to **11,137**. The VIX rose to approx. **20**.

The morning brought a data block clearly above consensus: September import prices **+1.1%** month over month and **+8.1%** year over year (the biggest twelve-month gain since 2022), industrial production **+0.7%** and capacity utilization at **76.9%**. In practice, the market read that as more energy-driven cost pressure, but the spotlight again fell on credit and valuations in AI infrastructure. The dollar only reclaimed ground timidly: the DXY closed near **101.8** and EUR/USD near **1.124**, below the Europe session high.

## Analysis

### Tech: the financing question takes over again

After Thursday's relief — when Nvidia's CEO defended CoreWeave's financial position — the New York session undid much of that calm. CoreWeave fell approx. **12%** and its bonds remain in stress territory. Semiconductors extend to **eight** consecutive down sessions, the longest streak since 2008: the market is no longer debating only a one-off credit accident, but whether massive data-center spending will generate returns in time with rates still high.

That explains the week's rotation. The Dow held up better on Friday and still finishes the week in the green (approx. **+0.7%**), while the Nasdaq drops approx. **4.5%** — the worst week since June — and the S&P 500 approx. **2%**. Same pattern as in Europe: Europe relatively strong versus Wall Street, and inside Wall Street, defensives and less AI-exposed cyclicals better than chips and software.

### Hot data, only a timid dollar

The "hot" scenario the Europe session left open showed up in the numbers: imports higher with fuel as a driver (approx. **+4.8%** on fuel imports ⚠️ detail from a single official reading) and industrial production above the 0.4% expected. In theory, that should support the front end of the curve and help the dollar reclaim **101.85–102**.

In practice, the DXY only approached **101.8** and the euro gave back from Europe's **1.126–1.127** zone to approx. **1.124**. Sterling closed near **1.333**, the AUD near **0.695** and USD/JPY near **158**. My read: the data confirm cost inflation hasn't gone away, but this week's tech selloff and bond haven bid still weigh more than an orderly dollar bounce.

Odds of a Fed hike on **October 28** remain in the mid-teens after Warsh (approx. **12–18%** depending on the metric ⚠️; readings differ). December still carries a high probability of at least one move. October is no longer the hawkish event of the week; what matters is whether energy inflation filters through and whether AI credit stabilizes.

### Oil: from $106 in Europe to near $110 in New York

In the Europe session Brent reactivated the escalation near **$106.4**. In New York the jump was larger: settlement near **$109.7–110** (approx. **+3.6%** versus the prior close), with WTI near **$99**. Two fronts are pushing: Gulf / Hormuz supply tension and a U.S. legislative effort aimed at blocking a deal to release Russian diesel into global markets. With Thursday's PPI and today's import prices showing how much energy weighs, oil is again an argument for Fed hawks.

The 10-year closed near **5.10%**, only slightly above Thursday's New York close (approx. 5.08%). In other words: the big weekly Treasury rally isn't fully undone, but Friday was no longer a day of aggressive bond buying.

### Scenarios into the weekend

- **If AI credit fears cool in Asia on Monday:** tech can stabilize after eight down sessions in chips; the Nasdaq would enter the week looking for a floor, not an extension.
- **If fresh stress headlines hit AI infrastructure debt:** the rotation out of tech deepens and Treasuries act as a haven again.
- **Oil:** as long as Brent holds closes above **$105.5** — and it now trades near $110 — the escalation branch stays active and keeps energy inflation alive.
- **Dollar:** hot data is only partly priced; without a new hawkish catalyst, the DXY can stay in a range below **102**, vulnerable to any relief in oil or risk appetite.
