**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

# Week of Nov 23–27: PCE lands in the middle of a tech rout, with a January Fed hike a coin toss

Dallas, Texas — Nov 22, 2026, 15:50 CT

**Subtitle:** The tech and AI selloff is running the market: S&P 500 futures lost approx. 8.6% on the week and the VIX ended Friday's New York session near 36. Bonds acted as a haven, and a January Fed hike is now a coin toss (approx. 50%, up to 65% depending on the measure ⚠️). Wednesday's **PCE**, in a week shortened by **Thanksgiving**, is the test that could tip that balance.

**Pills:**

- **PCE and GDP (Wed 7:30 AM CT):** core expected 0.3% m/m (3.2% y/y); Q3 GDP approx. 3.4–3.5% ⚠️
- **Fed:** December approx. 86–93% ⚠️; January approx. 50–65% ⚠️
- **VIX (Friday New York close):** 35.89, versus 19.30 a week earlier
- **US2Y (Friday New York close):** 4.63–4.65%, approx. 20–25 bp lower on the week
- **DXY:** 103.84 (+0.4%); AUD/USD the hardest-hit currency (−2.3%)
- **Thanksgiving (Thu 26):** Wall Street closed; Friday is a half session until 12:00 PM CT

**Cards:**

**1. PCE: December is nearly priced, January gets decided here.** The market keeps a Fed hike on December 9 as its base case (approx. 86–93% ⚠️; one measure taken before Friday's slide reaches 99%), but January has slipped from approx. 59% a week ago to around 50%. On Wednesday at 7:30 AM CT, October PCE (core expected 0.3% m/m and 3.2% y/y; headline 0.3% and 3.8%) arrives together with the second estimate of Q3 GDP (consensus approx. 3.4–3.5% ⚠️), personal income and spending (0.4% and 0.4%), durable goods (approx. 0.4–0.5%) and jobless claims (201K). The core matters more than the headline: a 0.4% monthly print with firm spending would put January back on the table and push the US2Y above approx. 4.75%; a 0.2% print, alongside the drop in consumers' one-year inflation expectations (3.7% in November from 4.7% in October), would reinforce the view that the Fed is near its peak. The threshold I'm watching: if January falls below approx. 45%, the dollar loses its main support against the yen. The Beige Book (Wed 1:00 PM CT) and Williams (Mon 3:00 PM CT) round out the picture.

**2. Tech: the selloff has no clear floor yet.** On Friday, reports that the securities regulator is reviewing Nvidia's accounting sank the stock approx. 14%; the S&P 500 ended the New York session down 3.70% and the semiconductor index lost 17.1% on the week. New US restrictions on advanced chip sales to China add pressure on tech, copper and gold (gold fell 9.5% on the week). With Wall Street closed on Thursday and a half session on Friday, liquidity will be thin and moves can be abrupt. Earnings this week: Zoom and HPE (Mon, after the close), Best Buy (Tue, before the open), Dell, HP, Workday and Autodesk (Tue, after the close) and Deere (Wed, before the open). A view gaining ground among strategists: if credit and equities force the Fed to stop, the market could pull the rate peak forward to March or April.

**3. An uneven dollar: up against sterling and the Aussie, down against the yen.** The DXY rose only 0.4% on the week (103.84 at Friday's New York close). The yen acted as a haven again (USD/JPY 158.51–158.53, −0.7%), sterling fell 1.0% (1.3081–1.3082) and the euro held up better (1.0967–1.0975 ⚠️, −0.5%). Sunday's open shows no meaningful gap despite Bahrain (USD/JPY 158.56, AUD/USD 0.6618; single source ⚠️). In Europe, Monday's flash PMIs (eurozone: manufacturing 52.7, services approx. 51.8–51.9; UK: 53.0 and 53.0) and Tuesday's German Ifo (approx. 90.5–90.6) test whether industry is holding up; the ECB minutes come out on Thursday. The market sees a December ECB hike at approx. 87–97% ⚠️ and a BoE hike at approx. 74–77%. Thirty-day implied volatility, measured before the VIX jump and therefore probably too narrow, gives weekly ranges of approx. 156.90–160.15 for USD/JPY and 1.2970–1.3195 for GBP/USD.

**4. Australia, Japan and Canada: inflation with split meetings.** In Australia, October CPI (Tue 6:30 PM CT; consensus 4.8% y/y, 0.4% m/m, trimmed mean 0.3%) follows a −43.5K jobs print, with the RBA almost certainly on hold (approx. 95–97%): an above-consensus reading would clash with that calm. AUD/USD, closely tied to crude over the last 60 days, has an implied weekly range of approx. 0.6540–0.6695. Japan is on holiday Monday; Tokyo core CPI (Thu 5:30 PM CT, approx. 2.8–2.9% ⚠️ after 2.6%) is the clue for a December BoJ hike, priced anywhere from approx. 52% to 86% depending on the measure ⚠️. In Canada, Macklem speaks on Tuesday (11:25 AM CT) with a December BoC hike deeply split (approx. 40–61% ⚠️).

**5. Persian Gulf: escalation in the headlines, crude lower.** On Saturday Iran launched missiles and drones at Bahrain: Tehran says it targeted US radars, while Bahrain says a civilian industrial site was hit and two people were injured, accounts not independently verified. Between Friday and Saturday, three tankers were struck near Hormuz. Even so, Brent (January contract) ended the week at $92.57, −3.4%, supported by diplomatic signals: Iran says reopening Hormuz is "not difficult" if the US accepts its conditions, with Qatari mediators in Tehran. Implied volatility gives a weekly range of approx. $86–99; a break above the top on escalation would favor the yen and the Swiss franc as havens. On Friday, after the close, France's credit rating is up for review.
