> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 30, 2026, 01:55 CT:** Friday's Asia session was once again driven by big-tech earnings, not central banks. Amazon and Intel disappointed after the New York close and deepened doubts about whether AI spending is justified. The Bank of Japan, the main event of the night, left rates unchanged but struck a slightly firmer tone.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves a market on the defensive. **The tech selloff extends into a second day**, stocks in Japan and Korea fall hard, and money is seeking shelter in the **Swiss franc** and **Treasuries**. Overall, the dollar is little changed from the New York session close: it loses ground against the franc and the yen, but not against the more risk-sensitive currencies.

My read: markets head into Europe with two forces pulling against each other. On one side, risk aversion over tech and the Gulf; on the other, an **ECB that on Thursday all but locked in December** and a Bank of Japan that is not backing off. Today's euro-area inflation and growth data will show whether that ECB bet holds.

## Analysis

### Tech: the market punishes spending without returns

After Alphabet on Thursday, it was **Amazon and Intel's** turn. **Intel plunged more than 20% after hours** on fourth-quarter guidance well below expectations, and **Amazon fell between approx. 6% and 7%** after missing both earnings and revenue forecasts (figures vary by measurement). The common thread is the same: the market no longer rewards AI spending on its own; it wants to see when the payoff arrives.

**Nasdaq 100 futures are down between approx. 1.7% and 2%** from their Thursday reference, and **S&P 500 futures approx. 0.8%**.

### Stocks: Japan and Korea pay the bill

- **Korea** was hit hardest, down between approx. 3.8% and 4.9% depending on the time, again on chipmakers.
- **Japan**: the Nikkei is down approx. 2.5%.
- **Australia** is holding roughly flat, slightly higher.

### Bank of Japan: a pause, but still on course

The Bank of Japan **kept its rate at 1.25%**, as expected, but **with one dissent**: a board member proposed raising it to 1.50%. There were no votes for a cut, which was the risk worrying those betting on a stronger yen. In its quarterly report, the bank said **underlying inflation is now broadly consistent with its 2% target**, that price risks are tilted to the upside, and that it will **speed up sales of its exchange-traded fund holdings** from January 2027.

The reaction was clean: **USD/JPY fell to approx. 157.10** shortly after the decision and now sits at approx. **157.5**, about 18 pips below the New York session close. At his press conference, the governor repeated that the economy is growing moderately and that he is watching the currency, AI and the Middle East, but added that it is still too early to declare the 2% goal met, because inflation needs to settle at that level in a stable way. As of writing, he had given no explicit signal about December. A hike that month is still priced at approx. **80–82%**.

### Dollar: mixed, with the franc as the haven

- **USD/CHF**: the franc is the best G10 performer; the pair fell to approx. 0.819 and sits at approx. **0.822**, about 16 pips below the New York close.
- **EUR/USD**: rose to approx. 1.121 early in Asia but gave it back and sits at approx. **1.118**, almost unchanged from the New York close.
- **GBP/USD**: approx. **1.339**, slightly higher.
- **AUD/USD**: approx. **0.703**, flat despite the equity slide, and **USD/CAD** at approx. **1.397**, a little lower with crude up.
- **Dollar index**: approx. **101.8**, barely changed; it touched approx. 101.6 at its weakest.

**Treasuries** are rallying across the curve: the **2-year** yield is down to approx. **4.58%** and the **10-year** to approx. **4.92%**.

### Oil and gold: the Gulf is back in charge

December **Brent** is up to the **approx. $116** area, after touching approx. $117.8, on signs that **the US could strike Iran again**: military options were reportedly presented to the president. Add to that the attacks Yemen's rebels claimed against Saudi facilities on Thursday, with no confirmation of damage, and a **tanker attacked northeast of Oman** whose fire was put out, with the crew safe. Note: **the December Brent contract expires today**, which can add noise to prices.

**Gold** slips below **$4,000** despite the risk-off mood, and **silver** is down approx. 2%. In crypto, bitcoin eases to the approx. $77,700–78,400 area after the largest exchange suspended deposits and withdrawals over a suspected hack.

### Overnight data

- **France**: third-quarter GDP **shrank 0.1%**, versus an expected 0.1% gain. It is the first European data point of the day and does not help the euro.
- **Australia**: third-quarter producer prices **+1.1%**, slightly above the 1.0% expected, consistent with the hike the market expects from Australia's central bank on November 3.
- **Japan**: industrial production rose 2.2% month on month, above forecasts, while retail sales came in below (2.3% year on year).

- **Fed (December 9):** hike at approx. **72–75%**, below where it stood before Thursday's PCE.
- **Bank of Japan (December 18):** hike at approx. **80–82%**.
- **Reserve Bank of Australia (November 3):** hike at approx. **78–89%**, depending on the market.
- **Bank of England (November 5):** hike at approx. **98%**.

For a volatility reference, the 30-day expected move implied by options is approx. 1.7% for EUR/USD and approx. 2.0% for USD/JPY.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If flash inflation from Germany, France, Spain and Italy comes in firm**, it would reinforce the view that the ECB hikes in December and support the euro, although the "hike and then pause" message limits how far that bet can go. Soft data, on top of France's negative GDP, would do the opposite.
- **If the tech selloff spreads to Europe and the New York open**, the franc and the yen would remain the natural havens, and risk-linked currencies such as the Australian dollar would be more exposed.
- **If tensions with Iran escalate**, crude would have room to keep rising, with the caveat of the December contract expiry; any sign of diplomacy would cool it quickly.
- **If US labor costs (7:30 AM CT) surprise to the upside**, the dollar would regain some support after two days of falling short-term yields.
