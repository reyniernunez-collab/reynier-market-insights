> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 23, 2026, 08:09 CT:** Friday's Europe session left a split map: the continent disappointed on activity, with France now in contraction, while the UK surprised to the upside. Even so, the dollar ran the currency show, winning back part of what it lost on Thursday. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the focus was the scare and recovery in equities, Brent above $114 and US bonds giving back only part of their rally. The Europe session added the flash PMIs, and the picture is uncomfortable for the euro: **France slips into contraction, the euro area cools and input prices rise on energy**. That is the least pleasant mix for a central bank: less activity with more price pressure.

At the same time, US short-end yields gave back part of Thursday's drop and the dollar regained ground. European equities spent the morning in the red (the pan-European index is down approx. 0.6%), with heavy falls in specific names such as Kering (approx. −13% on weaker-than-expected Gucci sales) and Volvo Car (approx. −27%, a record low, after a loss and warnings about demand in China). My read: today decides whether Thursday's Fed repricing was a regime change or just an episode.

## Analysis

### France: from the spread to the data

Until yesterday, the French problem lived in the bond market. Today it is in the real economy too: the composite PMI fell to **48.4** (approx. 50.5 expected; prior 51.1), with both services and manufacturing below 50. The detail on business expectations at multi-year lows comes from a single report (⚠️ unconfirmed). Germany held up better (composite **52.8**) and the euro area dropped to **51.9** (52.7 expected), its lowest since June.

France's 10-year spread over Germany moved in a band of approx. **170–175 bp** during the Europe session (⚠️ single-source intraday calculation), close to the post-2011 highs we saw on Thursday. The euro looks less sensitive to France today than a few days ago, but a jump in the spread toward the 200 bp area could reconnect the two abruptly.

### The euro: under pressure, no panic

EUR/USD touched approx. **1.1183** (3:36 AM CT) and hovers around approx. 1.119 (−0.2% vs Thursday's New York session close). The drop came without panic: it is more the dollar regaining ground than a flight from the euro.

### UK: good data, weaker pound

The UK composite PMI rose to **53.1** (51.7 expected), with services and manufacturing both above forecasts. It followed very soft retail sales (−0.4% month on month) and weak consumer confidence. The UK 2-year yield rose approx. 6 bp, but GBP/USD hit the day's lows near **1.3365**. Against the euro, however, sterling is at a 17-month high (EUR/GBP approx. 0.836): the gap between the French and British PMIs shows up in that cross.

### The dollar catches its breath

- **Yields:** the US 2-year goes from approx. 4.65% at Thursday's New York session close to approx. **4.70%**, and the 10-year is back near approx. **5.00%**.
- **Dollar:** the index is up approx. 0.16%, to approx. 101.8.
- **Franc:** the day's weakest currency (USD/CHF approx. 0.824, +0.5%); it gives back part of the haven bid it gained on Thursday.
- **Yen:** gave back some strength; USD/JPY is around approx. 156.9 after touching the yen's strongest level in a month during the Asia session (approx. 156.1).
- **Credit:** corporate bond spreads widen to their highest since May 2025, on doubts about whether AI revenues justify the infrastructure spending.
- **Futures:** S&P 500 down approx. 0.3% and Nasdaq 100 approx. 0.1%, after the Nasdaq 100's worst session since June.

### Oil in event mode

Brent reached approx. **$115** in the Europe session (6:00 AM CT), its highest since early May, on new Houthi attacks against Saudi Arabia and Iranian statements about controlling traffic in Hormuz (⚠️ unconfirmed). US crude holds above $100. Gold, by contrast, still isn't acting as a haven (approx. $4,040). Oil complicates the inflation picture just as European activity cools.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Firm US PMI and short-end yields steady or higher:** the dollar would consolidate its recovery, with the euro and sterling under pressure, and the odds of a December Fed hike would drift back toward approx. 80%.
2. **Services PMI clearly below approx. 56 or another tech stumble:** Thursday's logic would return. The yen would be the main winner and the dollar would lose ground broadly, though the euro would be capped by France.
3. **Oil:** sustained new highs would reinforce the stagflation read in Europe (weak euro, firm Canadian dollar); a clear drop would take premium out of the move.
4. **France's rating:** it lands after the New York session close. A one-notch downgrade is largely priced; the tail risk is a bigger cut or a negative outlook, and its effect would show at next week's open.
