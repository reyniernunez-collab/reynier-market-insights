> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 13, 2026, 01:56 CT:** The Asia session picks up Monday's chip selloff from New York and amplifies it. Korea takes the hardest hit, US and European futures slip, investors hide in government debt and oil gives back some of the premium it built yesterday. All of it one day before the US CPI.

## Executive Summary

Monday's New York close left an uncomfortable signal: the Dow rose (~~+0.4%) while the Nasdaq fell (~~−0.4%) and the S&P finished flat (~7,807), with chipmakers leading the declines. In the Asia session that rotation turned into an outright tech selloff. Doubts about the payoff from AI spending, plus fears of Chinese competition, hit Korea, Japan and Taiwan.

The result is a textbook risk-off Tuesday: stocks down, bonds up, the yen and Swiss franc a little firmer, and a dollar that loses the momentum of recent days. Oil, which confirmed its Middle East escalation tone on Monday, eases alongside. It sits in a middle zone that doesn't settle the debate.

## Analysis

### Tech: from rotation to selloff

What looked like a sector rotation inside Wall Street on Monday became liquidation in Asia. The regional index falls ~**1.7%** and is heading for its weakest close since August. The **Kospi sinks ~6.5%**: SK Hynix and Samsung each lose around 8%, in an exit from tech that the market pins on competitive pressure from China. Japan and Taiwan tech also slide. Meanwhile, Tencent is lining up a bond sale of roughly $5 billion to fund its AI expansion, a reminder that the bill for this race keeps growing.

In futures the split between indices is clear. NQ trades near **30,650**, about 180 points below where it stood after Monday's New York close and some 415 below yesterday morning. ES barely gives ground (~**7,847**). In other words, the damage is concentrated in growth and semiconductors, not the whole market. Options reflect that asymmetry: the 30-day expected move is ~**5.5%** for NQ versus ~**3.8%** for ES, and names such as AMD, Micron or Intel carry expected moves of roughly 12% to 17% over that window.

### Bonds and the dollar: the shelter works

Government debt is doing its job as a cushion. 10-year Treasury futures have rallied clearly since Monday evening, with Australian and New Zealand debt following. Be careful with the level: the US cash market was closed yesterday for Columbus Day, and the 10-year reference being quoted (~5.26%) comes from a single source and couldn't be verified. What's solid is the direction: yields lower.

The dollar snaps a four-session winning streak: DXY ~**102.41** versus ~102.57 after the New York close. Indicative levels around **1:45 AM CT**: EUR/USD ~**1.1186** (a ~20-pip bounce), GBP/USD ~**1.3230**, USD/JPY ~**158.12** (the yen gains almost 40 pips as a haven), USD/CHF ~**0.8326**, AUD/USD ~**0.6968**, USD/CAD ~**1.4263**. The Indian rupee rises again after fresh support measures from its central bank, which is trying to offset expensive oil and capital outflows.

Gold futures are back above **$4,200** (~$4,208) on the risk-off move. Spot trades quite a bit lower (~$4,166), so don't mix the two references.

### Oil: less premium, same debate

On Monday Brent settled at **$106.65** in the New York session and never dropped below $105.5 all day, confirming the escalation tone after the attacks in Riyadh and the Gulf. In the Asia session it eases: readings put it between ~**$105.4** and ~**$105.6** (figures that don't fully agree), right at the edge of that $105.5 threshold. WTI trades near ~**$92.5** (it settled at $93.74).

The read is escalation **softened, not undone**: crude is still clearly above Friday's $104.72, the reference that would mark a containment scenario. The underlying tension continues: several Gulf airlines cancelled flights to Saudi cities, with Riyadh without service for several days. The market prices a daily move of about ±$3 in Brent.

### Overnight data: soft Japan, mixed Australia

- **Japan:** September producer prices surprised to the downside (~**6.8%** year on year versus ~7.6% expected; −0.3% month on month). With the yen already firm as a haven, the print takes some pressure off the BoJ.
- **Australia:** the RBA minutes struck a restrictive tone (concern about energy inflation and the need to keep spending in check), but NAB business confidence slumped to **−13** from −8. Net result: the AUD barely moved and Australian futures didn't add to hike bets.
- **Germany:** final September CPI came in **as expected** (0.6% month on month, 3.3% year on year).

### Rates: the market doesn't change its mind before CPI

No central bank's next meeting moved 20 pp or more, so there's **no rate alert**:

- **Fed Oct 28:** ~**19–20%** hike odds (~80% hold), with all three readings aligned. December: ~81–85% odds of at least one hike, depending on the measure.
- **ECB Oct 29:** ~**88–90%** hold (readings differ by ~2.5 pp).
- **BoE Nov 5:** ~**89–91%** hike.
- **BoC Oct 28:** ~**12–13%** hike in one reading; another shows ~25% (conflicting figures, low confidence).
- **RBA Nov 3:** ~**26–27%** hike in Australian futures; another reading is higher (~35%), conflicting figures.
- **RBNZ Oct 28:** ~**57–59%** odds of some hike.
- **BoJ Oct:** ~**90–95%** hold.

### Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If NQ doesn't stop falling** into the Europe open, the tech selloff goes from an Asian episode to a global theme, and bonds and the yen keep getting bid.
- **If Brent reclaims and holds ~$105.5**, the oil → yields → dollar chain returns, and the bond rally would have less room to run.
- **If Brent loses Friday's $104.72** on a sustained basis, the containment scenario gains ground and the euro gets some relief.
- With tomorrow's CPI ahead, any Fed commentary today (Waller, Barkin, Collins) can move October odds more than usual.
