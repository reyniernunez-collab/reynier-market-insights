## Detailed Analysis

### Data and cycle

No change in path from the morning cut: Fed Oct hike 73%, Dec 79%. ECB Oct hike 52%. BoE Nov 86%. RBA Sept 29 91%. BoJ Oct hold 69% (hike tail 32%). The US wrap is _diplomacy → oil down → equity bid_, not a dovish Fed repricing.

### Geopolitics

The "new deal / diplomacy hopes" narrative won the New York session and pushed crude lower. It coexists with Hormuz still closed and war powers intact. The market is pricing the _probability of a deal_, not a reopening.

### Policy and COT

Specs trimmed 5-year shorts (−116k to −881k) and 10-year shorts, but **added** 2-year shorts to **−907k**. Read: the front end is still hawkish. EUR and GBP are at 3-year extreme shorts: a bullish EUR/GBP squeeze only if the Hormuz deal materializes _and_ the Fed doesn't hike in October. JPY: the 48k cut in the speculative long fits USD/JPY 158 and the early-morning G10 chart.

### Correlations

30d intact: CL-EUR 0.93 / CL-GBP 0.99. If the oil-down wrap holds over the weekend, that beta starts working _in favor_ of EUR/GBP on Monday — on condition that Brent doesn't bounce back to 105–107.

### Volatility and micro

USD/JPY 1SD 155–162; Monday pin at **158.25** and a magnet at **160.50** ($1.23bn). EUR 1.1400 is the dominant expiry. AUD 0.7100 (AUD 1.08bn) just above spot ~0.702 — irrelevant if the RBA delivers.

### Sentiment

Equity funds: specs shorter in ES (−355k) while asset managers add longs. OpenAI "review months" is sector noise, not macro. MOC doesn't change the regime.
