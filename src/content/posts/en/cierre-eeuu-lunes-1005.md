> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 5, 2026, 15:20 CT:** Wall Street turned the morning around. Futures pointed lower on Europe's political noise, but big tech took charge and lifted the Nasdaq 100 to a new record, even as oil and long bonds keep flashing caution.

## Executive Summary

A Monday that started with doubts ended in the green thanks to tech. The Nasdaq 100 hits a new all-time high and the S&P 500 approaches its own, even though the backdrop isn't comfortable: crude is still elevated, long-dated Treasury yields are at multi-decade highs, and the euro remains at its lowest since May 2025 on Europe's political and fiscal crisis. WTI does ease below **$90**, while the White House prepares to loosen rules on a tax-exempt type of diesel to contain fuel prices.

## Analysis

### Equities: a narrow rally, but a strong one

Big tech is in the driver's seat again. That's good news for the indexes, but also something to watch: when gains are concentrated in a few names, the market is more exposed to any surprise in that group. With the Nasdaq 100 at a record and the S&P 500 within reach of one, the question for the coming days is whether the rest of the market joins in or the rally keeps leaning on a handful of stocks.

### Bonds: the warning stocks aren't hearing

The most striking move of the day isn't in equities but in fixed income. Long-dated yields at multi-decade highs while the Nasdaq sets records is an unusual combination. Behind it is a mix of oil-driven inflation and fiscal concern. Our daily data has the Fed at ~**82%** odds of holding in October but ~**79%** odds of a hike by December: the market isn't ruling out that the next move is up.

### Euro: Europe is still the weak spot

The euro can't bounce and stays at its lowest since May 2025. Political instability in Europe and doubts about public finances keep outweighing the data. Until that noise fades, the dollar keeps the upper hand in EUR/USD.

### Oil: some relief, same backdrop

WTI falls below **$90**, partly on the White House's plan to ease restrictions on diesel to lower its cost. It's short-term relief, but crude remains at levels that feed inflation and, with it, pressure on bonds.

## What I'm watching tomorrow

- **Nasdaq 100:** whether it holds the new high or its reliance on tech leaves it vulnerable to profit-taking.
- **Long Treasuries:** another leg higher in yields would be the main threat to the rally.
- **EUR/USD:** whether the low since May 2025 holds or European politics pushes the euro further down.
- **WTI:** whether $90 gives way decisively after the diesel measures.
