> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 16, 2026, 02:04 CT:** The night brought relief. Chips stopped falling, Seoul recovered part of Thursday's hit and Warsh avoided nudging markets toward an October hike. Traders took almost half the odds off that move and the dollar kept slipping.

## Executive Summary

After Thursday's New York close (S&P 500 +0.33%, Dow +1.08%, Nasdaq +0.08%, with a very hot core PPI at 0.7% m/m but soft underlying consumption), the Asia session **turned moderately risk-on**. Pressure on semiconductors eased: the Kospi rebounded approx. **4%** and the broad Asia-Pacific gauge rose approx. **1.6%** (both single-source). S&P 500 futures were down as much as approx. 0.6% before turning positive.

The policy catalyst was **Warsh**. Speaking in Bangkok (10:30 PM CT Thursday), the Fed Chair said he was skeptical of predicting decisions for the rest of 2026 and would assess the situation at the October and December meetings. He did not validate the hawkish read left by CPI and PPI, and markets responded: **October hike odds fall to approx. 15–16%**, from approx. 27–30% in the afternoon.

The dollar had another soft session, gold rose and **Brent failed to hold** the threshold that kept the escalation branch alive.

## Analysis

### Chips: the rebound comes from Seoul

After two sessions of punishment, semiconductors found buyers in Asia. Samsung and SK hynix each gained more than 4% and lifted the Kospi, which was coming off its **lowest close since April**. The bounce started before Warsh spoke, so it is not only a Fed story: Thursday's Wall Street rebound, helped by Nvidia's backing of CoreWeave, had already improved the tone.

It is worth reading with caution. Korean central bank data show foreign investors sold a net approx. **$14.16 billion** of local stocks in September (versus a roughly flat balance in August), and the finance ministry pledged tighter surveillance against market-disrupting behavior. On top of that comes a structural headline: **Taiwan will curb AI chip exports to China** as part of a deal with the US. The bounce is real, but the underlying flow into the sector remains fragile.

In futures, the S&P 500 trades near **7,797–7,799** and the Nasdaq 100 near **30,135–30,157**, above Thursday evening's levels.

### Fed: Warsh takes half off the October hike

On Thursday afternoon the market refused to reprice the Fed higher despite the hot PPI; Warsh tipped the balance further. Without addressing monetary policy directly, he highlighted AI's potential benefits for the economy and avoided committing to the next meeting. Result:

- **October 28:** hike odds approx. **15–16%** (from approx. 27–30%). The main readings converge in that range.
- **December:** odds of at least one hike remain high (approx. **80–92%** depending on the reading), but the tail of a second hike gets cut sharply.

Read for the rest of the day: the market moves from "October in play" to **"December as the central meeting."** Today's data (import prices and industrial production) carry less weight than CPI or PPI, but a very hot print could give back some of those odds.

Treasuries slipped somewhat during Warsh's remarks, after a broad rally in the New York session; the 10-year sits around approx. **5.08%**, practically unchanged from Thursday evening.

Gold futures rise toward approx. **$4,200** (+$18–20 versus Thursday evening). Don't mix that level with spot readings, which sit lower.

### BoJ: Ueda toughens his tone, October doesn't move

Ueda warned that underlying inflation risks **overshooting the 2% target**, that financial conditions remain accommodative and that the bank will keep raising rates in response to the economy and prices. Even so, October hike odds stay around approx. **4%**: the message is about the path rather than the next meeting.

Other central banks (indicative readings):

- **BoE Nov 5:** hike very likely, approx. **91–93%**.
- **RBA Nov 3:** hike approx. **18%** per Australian futures ⚠️ (other readings range from approx. 11% to 21%).
- **BoC Oct 28:** approx. **19%** ⚠️ (another metric sits well above and is unreliable).
- **ECB Oct 29:** hold dominant; hike approx. **5–9%** ⚠️ (gap between sources).
- **RBNZ Oct 28:** very wide range, approx. **53–81%** ⚠️; the lower reading is the more stable one.

### Brent: the escalation deflates

Thursday's New York settle left Brent at **$105.92**, with the escalation branch active (1-hour closes above $105.5 since the morning). In the Asia session that condition is lost: **since 9 PM CT every 1-hour close has been below $105.5**, and price hovers near **$105.2**. Full invalidation would require sustained closes below $104.72, which has not happened yet. The regime shifts to **sideways, with escalation degraded**.

WTI trades near approx. **$97.3** ⚠️ (quotes differ by contract; Thursday's settle: $97.97).

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If final euro-area CPI confirms the flash** (3.8% headline, 2.5% core), the ECB stays on hold and EUR/USD depends mostly on the dollar side.
- **If October Fed odds stay below 20%** after the 7:30 and 8:15 AM CT data, the soft-dollar bias from the Asia session has room to extend; if they climb back above 25%, a dollar rebound becomes the more likely scenario.
- **If the chip rebound holds into the European open and in Nasdaq futures**, Asia's relief gains credibility; if it fades, foreign selling in Korea returns to center stage.
- **If Brent regains 1-hour closes above $105.5**, the escalation branch reactivates; sustained closes below $104.72 would invalidate it fully.
