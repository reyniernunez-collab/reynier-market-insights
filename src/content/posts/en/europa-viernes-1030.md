> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 30, 2026, 08:18 CT:** Friday's Europe session extended the tech slide that began in Asia, with a twist: the dollar wasn't the haven. US yields fell and the yen, the franc and sterling gained. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was the tech selloff after Amazon's and Intel's results and a Bank of Japan that held with one dissent. The Europe session followed the same path in equities, with European tech also lower, but **the dollar ended up among the weak currencies**.

My read: **it's a haven trade without the dollar**. Money leaving stocks is buying Treasuries, and with lower yields the dollar loses appeal against the yen, the franc and sterling. On top of that, European data leave the ECB with a dilemma: the economy grew more than expected, but France and Italy are weak and their inflation is accelerating.

## Analysis

### The dollar slips across the board as yields fall

- **Dollar index:** near **101.64**, approx. 0.2 points below Thursday's New York session close, after touching approx. 101.57 mid-morning in Europe.
- **US rates:** the 2-year falls to approx. **4.57%** and the 10-year to approx. **4.91%**, about 3 basis points lower each and weekly lows.
- **Equities:** Nasdaq futures are down approx. 2.5% from Thursday's settlement and S&P 500 futures approx. 1%. In Europe, tech fell sharply, although the regional index is heading for its best month since June.

### Sterling and the yen, the winners

- **GBP/USD:** up about **54 pips to approx. 1.343** with no UK data; the move came from the dollar side. Against the euro, sterling is near its best level since January (⚠️ single-source figure). The Bank of England hike on November 5 is practically fully priced (approx. 98–100%).
- **USD/JPY:** down about **67 pips to approx. 157.0**, a weekly low. The Bank of Japan held at 1.25% with one vote to hike and said underlying inflation is now consistent with its target; the market reads it as a bank ready to hike every few months. The governor's later attempt to soften the tone (⚠️ single-source) didn't stop the yen.
- **USD/CHF:** the franc also gains, with the pair about 35 pips lower at approx. 0.821.

### Europe: better growth, worse inflation

- **Euro-area GDP (Q3):** **0.4% quarter on quarter**, against 0.2% expected, and 1.3% year on year. But the split is uneven: France contracts (−0.1%), Italy stalls (0.0%), Germany grows 0.2% and Spain 0.6%; the extra push came from Ireland and the Netherlands (⚠️ single-source figures).
- **October inflation:** in France, the harmonized measure rises to **3.8%**, the highest since early 2024, with energy surging. In Italy it reaches **5.6%** against 4.6% expected, the biggest surprise of the day. German regions, by contrast, cooled (approx. 2.6–2.9% year on year), pointing to a national figure below the 3.0% expected.
- **Euro:** **EUR/USD rises about 18 pips to approx. 1.120**, but falls against sterling, the yen and the franc. Some ECB members talked about possible further hikes if inflation doesn't ease (⚠️ single-source), and still the odds of a December hike dipped slightly. The French 10-year spread over Germany widens to approx. **161 bp**.

### Oil higher; gold isn't acting as a haven

**December Brent** reached approx. **$118** and trades about $2.7 above Thursday's settlement, on the day the contract expires. Incidents with tankers near Hormuz and mixed messages about the end of the war with Iran weigh in (⚠️ unconfirmed reports). **The Canadian dollar** benefits: USD/CAD falls to approx. **1.395**, a weekly low in the Europe session. **Gold**, by contrast, drops approx. 1.2% despite the nerves; one possible explanation is forced selling to cover equity losses, though that's a hypothesis.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Employment Cost Index (ECI) in line and tech still weak (most likely):** the dollar would stay soft, with the yen and sterling keeping the edge and the euro without a direction of its own.
2. **Hot ECI or hawkish Fed messages:** yields would bounce and the dollar would win back part of its losses, especially against the yen.
3. **Soft ECI and more tech selling:** yields would fall further and the dollar would lose across the board.
4. **German inflation below expectations:** it would take support from the euro against sterling even if the dollar stays weak.
5. **Iran:** a deal would lower oil and take strength from the Canadian dollar; an escalation would do the opposite and could restore gold's haven role.
