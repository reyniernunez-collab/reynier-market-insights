> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 14, 2026, 01:58 CT:** The Asia session delivers the China data the market was waiting for and an oil market that holds an escalation tone without adding fresh Gulf headlines. The dollar still refuses to follow crude. Everything points to this morning's US CPI.

## Executive Summary

After Tuesday's New York close, Brent had reclaimed the escalation threshold (a $105.69 settle in that session, above $105.5 and above Friday's $104.72) with the confirmed attack on an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran. In the Asia session that branch remains intact on price: no Brent hourly close fell below $105.5, but the premium is deflating (intraday high approx. $107.15 → approx. $105.9–106.0) and **there were no fresh Gulf or Hormuz headlines** in the overnight window. Nighttime energy news was Russia–Ukraine (damage to facilities in Kyiv and a strike on an oil facility in Lutsk).

What did move FX was **China**: PPI at 5.0% year on year (highest since June 2022; consensus approx. 4.4–4.5%, calendars differ), exports +36.3% and imports +31%, and a $133.53 billion surplus versus consensus around $118 billion. Chinese CPI came in soft (0.9% year on year versus 1.0%). AUD/USD broke 0.70. The dollar, by contrast, stayed soft: the oil → dollar chain **still has no price confirmation**.

## Analysis

### China: hot PPI, soft CPI, strong surplus

The China block was the Asia session's catalyst:

- **CPI:** 0.9% year on year / 0.2% month on month (consensus 1.0% / 0.3%). Mild miss; core holds at 1.0%.
- **PPI:** **5.0%** year on year / 1.2% month on month versus consensus of approx. **4.4–4.5%** (sources disagree on the decimal). A strong upside surprise, driven by crude, coal and metals.
- **Trade balance:** a surplus of **$133.53 billion** versus consensus around **$118.1 billion** (another calendar shows $116.8; conflicting figures). Exports +36.3% and imports +31% year on year, both above expectations. September crude imports fell month on month (37.10 Mt versus 37.93 Mt in August).

Net message: **cost-push inflation rising and external trade very firm**, with domestic consumer-price demand still soft. That lifts commodities and the AUD; it does not resolve the domestic-stimulus story.

In Singapore, MAS left the slope, width and midpoint of the S$NEER band unchanged: a touch more dovish than expected (some had priced a slight steepening), with a warning of elevated inflation into 2027. Advance Q3 GDP was mixed (1.1% q/q / 5.4% y/y).

### Oil: escalation intact, premium unwinding

Tuesday's New York-session reference settle (Brent **$105.69**) remains above $104.72. In the Asia session, Brent's 1-hour closes ran between approx. $105.98 and $106.92; the intraday high was near **$107.15** and by the end of the session price is back toward **$105.9–106.0**. WTI trades near $94.3–94.4 (New York settle $94.14).

Read: the escalation branch **holds on price, not on headlines**. Without new damage to Saudi export capacity or fresh Hormuz news, Tuesday afternoon's "headline premium" tends to give back. A Europe-session 1-hour Brent close below $105.5 would open a sideways-into-CPI scenario; below $104.72 would invalidate the escalation reclaim.

### Speakers: mixed ECB, BoE hawkish near-term and cautious on the tail

- **ECB:** Lagarde still sees no second-round effects but warns the bank can anticipate without waiting for obvious signals; Wunsch says the ECB will likely hike again; Moulin stresses that inflation is mostly energy-driven and that higher yields can moderate the response. Net: mixed, no material October repricing.
- **BoE Pill:** "somewhat more sceptical" of the market pricing **four hikes over the next year**, but hawkish near-term (November almost fully priced). That fits a flatter curve tail.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent closes a 1-hour bar below $105.5** in the Europe session, the escalation branch flips to "sideways into CPI" and the geopolitical premium loses force.
- **If AUD holds 1-hour closes ≥0.70** and US CPI prints soft on core, the bias favors commodities and the AUD; if it closes back below 0.70, the breakout fails.
- **If core CPI prints ≥0.3% month on month**, the dollar and yields tend higher and Fed October hike odds are more likely to reappear; core ≤0.1% pushes the other way.
- Without an upside CPI surprise, it is hard for the dollar to extend higher on oil alone: that chain **still lacks price confirmation**.
