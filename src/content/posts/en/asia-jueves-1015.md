> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 15, 2026, 01:55 CT:** The overnight session did not ease pressure on tech. Seoul and Tokyo took the chip hit, US Treasuries rallied hard and the dollar gave back much of the CPI impulse. Brent, for its part, lost the thresholds that had held the escalation branch.

## Executive Summary

After Wednesday's New York close —S&P 500 −1.11%, Nasdaq −1.88%, core CPI at 0.3% month on month / 2.6% year on year— the Asia session **extended the semiconductor selloff**. Kospi finished near **−7.4%** (with worse intraday declines and a brief trading halt); Samsung and SK hynix fell more than 10%. The Nikkei slipped approx. **4.7%**. Hang Seng approx. −1.4%; the Shanghai Composite approx. −2.4% (single-source close print). Australia was nearly flat (−0.09%).

The other side of the session was a **Treasury rally**: the 10-year moved toward **5.11%** (approx. −8 bp versus the 5.19% New York close) and the 2-year toward **4.74%** (approx. −6 bp). The dollar softened with that move: DXY touched a low near **101.88** and is back around **102.12**. EUR/USD briefly reached approx. **1.1240** and stabilizes near **1.1205–1.1207**.

Two local drivers complete the picture: **Australian employment** (−39.3k versus consensus around +20k) hits the AUD, and **Brent** breaks $105.5 and $104.72 on 1-hour closes, leaving behind the escalation branch that had held since Wednesday.

## Analysis

### Chips: Seoul and Tokyo pay the AI bill

Wednesday's New York chip selling did not stop at Wall Street. In Asia, Kospi led the damage (approx. **−7.4%** at the close, with intraday wicks near 8–9%) and forced a brief trading halt in Seoul. The Nikkei slipped approx. **4.7%**, with SoftBank, Advantest and Tokyo Electron among the more sensitive names. Hang Seng fell approx. **1.4%**; Shanghai approx. **2.4%** (single-source print).

TSMC, by contrast, posted a record third-quarter profit and spoke of “strong conviction” in the AI megatrend. The market did not treat that as a cushion: the session's doubt is not whether chip demand exists, but **how much of that investment translates into returns** when real rates and valuations tighten. Nasdaq 100 futures remain soft (approx. −1% versus the New York settle) and European futures pointed to a weak open, around −1%.

### Treasuries and the dollar: CPI fades through bonds

The Asia-session turn did not come from fresh US data, but from **haven demand** into the chip rout and a reading that the Fed may **skip October** (Wednesday afternoon's “more hikes likely, no urgency” message is still in the air). The 10-year printed a low near **5.09%** and trades around **5.11%**; the 2-year near **4.74%**.

The dollar followed: DXY from approx. **102.54** (New York close) to a low near **101.88**, now around **102.12**. Indicative levels versus that New York close:

- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1205–1.1207** (+53–55 pips; session high near 1.1240).
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3236** (+55 pips approx.).
- **USD/JPY** approx. **157.97–157.99** (−30 pips approx.; low near 157.57).
- **USD/CHF** approx. **0.8307–0.8309** (−37–39 pips approx.): the franc was the strongest major.
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.4218** (−14–15 pips approx.).

Important: **October Fed hike odds did not unwind**. Main readings converge around **29–30%** (versus approx. 20–22% before Wednesday's CPI). December still carries high odds of at least one hike. The yield move looks more like **term premium / haven** than an aggressive October repricing.

Bowman spoke in Bangkok, but the text was bank supervision, **not monetary policy**. The US speaker focus shifts to **Warsh** later in the day (evening CT).

### Australia: soft jobs, AUD against the grain

While other majors rose versus the dollar, the **AUD was the exception**. September employment printed **−39.3k** (consensus approx. +19.6–20k), with full-time +34.4k and part-time −73.7k; unemployment rose to **4.7%** (consensus 4.6%) and participation fell to 66.8%.

AUD/USD broke 0.6950 and trades near **0.6942–0.6945** (session low approx. 0.6941), below Wednesday's New York-session low (approx. 0.6949). RBA November 3 hike odds, anchored in Australian futures, fall from approx. 32% to approx. **16%** ⚠️: other readings stay higher (approx. 23–28%) and should not be mixed with the futures anchor.

### Brent: escalation branch ends

Wednesday's New York settle left Brent at **$106.06**, with every 1-hour close ≥$105.5. In the Asia session that condition breaks:

- First 1-hour close **below $105.5** (approx. **$105.05** at 7:00 PM CT).
- Then a 1-hour close **below $104.72** (approx. **$104.15** at 8:00 PM CT).
- Price now near **$104.3–104.5** (intraday low near $104.00).

WTI trades near **$94.1** (New York settle: $95.09). Under the rules we had been using, **the escalation branch is invalidated**. The price driver was not Iran-tension headlines (those continue), but the report that the US is weighing **easing sanctions on ghost-fleet vessels** to cut record freight rates and allow more Gulf flow. Crude barely reacted to Wednesday's large API draw; the official EIA print arrives at **11:00 AM CT**.

### Gold, other central banks and the UK

December gold futures rise toward approx. **$4,155–4,160** (approx. +$48–52 versus the New York settle of $4,107.90). Do not mix that print with spot readings from other windows.

Other central banks (indicative readings; flag conflicts):

- **BoE Nov 5:** hike very likely, approx. **90–91%** (main readings align after Pill: a hike “need not look like the start of a long series”).
- **BoC Oct 28:** prefer the reading near approx. **19%** ⚠️ (another metric sits clearly higher and is unreliable further out the curve).
- **ECB Oct 29:** hold dominates; hike around approx. **6–7%** on the lower readings ⚠️ (another sits near 12%).
- **BoJ October:** clear hold (approx. **4–5%** hike); Koeda sounded hawkish in tone, but the market still does not price a consecutive hike this month.
- **RBNZ Oct 28:** hike in a wide range approx. **54–66%** ⚠️ (gap across sources).

**UK August GDP** surprised to the upside: **+0.1%** month on month (consensus −0.1%), with industrial production +0.8% and manufacturing +1.2%. Sterling had already recovered ground with a softer dollar; the extra reaction to the data was contained. Bailey speaks at **2:30 AM CT**.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent holds 1-hour closes below $104.72**, the regime stays “escalation invalidated” and the geopolitical premium loses price force, even if Gulf headlines do not disappear.
- **If DXY reclaims 102.3 and EUR/USD closes a 1-hour bar back below 1.1200** without a soft PPI, the Asia-session dollar bounce stays a correction, not a post-CPI thesis change.
- **If AUD fails to reclaim 1-hour closes above 0.6950**, the post-jobs neutral-bearish bias holds into the New York session.
- **PPI and retail sales at 7:30 AM CT** are the CPI filter: a firm core PPI reinforces the restrictive read; soft PPI and weak retail open room for more dollar fade.
