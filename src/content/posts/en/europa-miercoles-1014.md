> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 14, 2026, 08:15 CT:** The Europe session reaffirms Brent's escalation after brushing the threshold and rebounding on Hormuz and Aramco headlines. The dollar recovers with oil and the euro gives back Asia's gains. This post closes the Europe-session landscape as New York opens: what was priced, how oil transmits into FX, and the conditional scenarios for how today's US inflation data gets digested.

## Executive Summary

In our Asia session post, China (5.0% PPI, strong surplus) pushed AUD through 0.70, the dollar stayed soft and Brent held the escalation branch without fresh Gulf headlines, with the premium deflating toward $105.9–106.0. The Europe session **changes the oil and dollar picture**, not the AUD's.

Brent nearly lost the technical threshold (lowest 1h close near $105.59; intraday low approx. $105.21), then rebounded toward approx. **$106.7** after a Hormuz mine headline and damage at Aramco's Dhahran headquarters — operations described as normal — and trades near **$106.2**. No hourly close fell below $105.5. In parallel, the dollar **recovers** (DXY approx. **102.22–102.24**) and EUR/USD **gives back** Asia's rally. The oil → dollar chain remains incomplete: it didn't confirm in Asia; in Europe it does, but alongside the oil rebound, not as a clean transmission.

European equities close green (Stoxx 600 approx. **+0.6%**), led by real estate and consumer, while chips stay under pressure. Into the New York open, the market digests September US inflation: the confirmed consensus was headline **0.6% m/m / 3.6% y/y** and core **0.2% / 2.5%**. Our pre-print analysis does not invent the result: only the landscape New York had to digest and the conditional scenarios already on the table.

## Analysis

### Oil: threshold brushed, rebound, escalation reinforced

The Europe-session price path tells the story. Brent's lowest 1h close sat near **$105.59** (tenths from the $105.5 threshold). There was a window of intraday lows below that level; the approximate floor was **$105.21**. The turn coincided with a Hormuz mine headline and, later, damage at Aramco's administrative HQ (operations normal). The rebound carried price toward approx. **$106.7**; toward the Europe close it trades near **$106.2**, above Tuesday's New York settle ($105.69). WTI marks **session highs** (approx. $95.8–95.9 vs $94.14 settle).

Read: the escalation branch **remains valid and is reinforced**. A Brent 1h close below $105.5 would have moved the scenario to sideways observation; below approx. $104.7 would invalidate the claim. That did not happen in Europe. Important for rates: yesterday's and today's oil is **not** in September CPI; it affects Oct/Nov expectations and implied inflation, and therefore weighs more on the **December** Fed than on the Oct 28 meeting.

### FX: soft Asia, recovered dollar in Europe

Orientative levels toward the end of the Europe session (approx. 6:30–6:45 AM CT), versus Tuesday's New York close and the Asia leg:

- **DXY** approx. **102.22–102.24** (session high near 102.27): recovers Asia's softness (approx. 102.12).
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.1195–1.1198** (low near 1.1187): gave back Asia's +12–17 pips; roughly flat vs the New York close.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.7006–0.7009**: holds 0.70 after China's PPI and +36.3% exports.
- **GBP/USD** approx. **1.3263**: firm vs the New York close.
- **USD/JPY** approx. **158.4**; **USD/CAD** in the week's range (approx. 1.4213–1.4216).
- December gold futures: **session lows** near $4,158–4,162 despite Hormuz — the dollar and pre-CPI positioning outweigh the geopolitical headline.
- Fresh US10Y near **5.20%** (avoid stale series).

The euro had returned toward 1.12 in Asia **more from dollar weakness than from own-cross strength**. In Europe, rejection in the 1.1205–1.1220 zone and the follow-through lower leave a more cautious EUR/USD bias, always conditional on the US inflation print. French stress persists: the OAT–Bund spread is around **approx. 137 bp**. Elevated European gas nudges medium-term euro-area core inflation expectations a little higher, still below what the market prices over the next few months. A reasonable read for the coming days is a possible retest of the **1.115–1.116** zone if the dollar firms with the print.

### Equities: Europe green; chips under pressure

The Stoxx 600 advances approx. **+0.6%** in the Europe session, backed by real estate and consumer; DAX and CAC in a similar range, FTSE a bit softer. Chips remain under pressure (several consecutive weak sessions for the sector). ASML rises in Amsterdam despite warning that supply will not fully meet demand over the next two years. In the US, a major bank drops sharply in early European trading after disappointing trading results, adding to sector pressure. S&P futures are slightly higher; the Nasdaq slips versus Tuesday's New York close. Risk sentiment stays relatively resilient despite oil: the market still assumes no major Middle East escalation before the midterms — a fragile read if headlines shift to capacity damage, not just headquarters.

### Conditional scenarios into New York (without inventing the print)

September CPI was the day's threshold. Consensus confirmed by two calendars: headline **0.6% m/m / 3.6% y/y**, core **0.2% / 2.5%**. **What can change the next Fed meeting is the core monthly print**, not just the headline (already expected hot on energy). Conditionals the market had on the table into the New York open:

1. **Restrictive core (≥0.3–0.4% m/m, or ≥0.3% with hot services/shelter):** October hike odds toward **30–35%**; dollar up; EUR/USD more exposed to a 1.115–1.116 retest. If Brent also holds 1h closes ≥$105.5, the dollar bias strengthens.
2. **Core in line (0.2%, headline 0.5–0.6%):** October stays near **20–22%**; FX more sideways; oil geopolitics becomes a secondary intraday driver.
3. **Soft core (≤0.1%; headline only from energy):** October toward approx. **10%**; dollar down; support for AUD/EUR **if** Brent does not fire another leg. Gold could recover from session lows.

Parallel oil branch: if Brent loses a 1h close below $105.5, escalation moves to observation; below approx. $104.7 it invalidates. Meanwhile, yesterday's/today's oil remains an **expectations** input, not a September print input.
