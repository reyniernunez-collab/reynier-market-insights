> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 6, 2026, 10:25 CT:** Europe closes Tuesday with the opposite tone to Monday. The political noise in France hasn't gone away, but the market finds room to breathe: regional bonds bounce, the euro climbs off its lows and cheaper oil takes some pressure off inflation. Wall Street takes the cue and the S&P 500 is eyeing its record again.

## Executive Summary

After a Monday that punished peripheral debt, Tuesday brings relief. Marine Le Pen's counter-budget, with a pledge to bring France's deficit below 3% of GDP, helps the spread between French and German bonds keep narrowing. European debt rebounds from multi-decade lows, led by France and Italy, and the euro moves away from its 17-month floor. Stocks climb (Stoxx 600 ~+0.7–0.8%), Brent falls ~2% toward $98 and S&P 500 futures point to fresh highs. Treasuries recover too, after the 10-year reached its highest level since 2002 on Monday.

## Analysis

### France: the spread narrows, but it's not a blank check

The key number of the session is the OAT-Bund spread. By our data it sits around **129–132 bp**, versus 138 bp on Monday and 158 bp on Friday, and the French 10-year yield is down about 11 bp. Le Pen's plan includes roughly €140 billion in savings and a primary surplus in 2028. The market reads it as a sign that the opposition doesn't want to blow up the budget, even though doubts about France's debt load remain. The government says talk of ECB intervention is premature. As long as the spread keeps falling, pressure on the euro and the periphery eases.

### Euro: sovereign relief, not a rates story

EUR/USD trades around **1.127** (+0.4% on the day, with a high near 1.128), well clear of Monday's 17-month low. The bounce comes from France, not monetary policy. In rates, the market is moving the other way. For the ECB, pricing through July 2027 drops from ~79 bp to ~**61 bp**, and the odds of a December hike fall to ~**78%**. For the Fed, the odds of an October hike rise to ~**24%** and December prices in ~~**27 bp** in total. That gap still favors the dollar. If the French relief fades, the euro is back to depending on it. Sterling looks better supported: the BoE has a November hike almost fully priced (~~**87%**), and Mann stressed that inflation "has become persistent."

### European data: ugly headlines, a less ugly core

German factory orders sink 10.6% on the month, but nearly all of it comes from a drop in large "other vehicles" orders. Excluding that line, the figure is essentially flat (−0.1%). Eurozone retail sales grow 0.1% month on month, slightly below expectations, and French industrial output falls 0.3%. The data are soft but not collapsing, consistent with a less aggressive ECB. In the UK, the construction PMI improves to 46.1, an eight-month high.

### Stocks and oil: AI pulls, crude eases

Risk appetite is backed by earnings and heavy investment in artificial intelligence. OpenAI is in talks with UAE funds for a round of roughly $30 billion, DeepSeek and Moonshot AI are raising fresh capital, and Alphabet signed a nuclear-power deal with Constellation Energy. S&P 500 futures climb toward their record, led by the Magnificent Seven. Brent drops ~**2%** to the $98 area, with WTI near $88. That eases inflation fears. Still, the tail risk points higher: a tanker was hit by a projectile in Hormuz, and outflows from the Middle East remain under close watch.

### US morning: no policy surprises

The August trade balance shows a **−$105.6 billion** deficit, slightly worse than expected, with record imports driven by AI-related goods and purchases pulled forward ahead of tariffs. It shaves a bit off third-quarter GDP but doesn't change the October meeting. New York Fed President Williams didn't discuss monetary policy, so the base case of an October pause stays intact. The dollar softens (DXY ~**101.9**, −0.3%) and the 10-year falls to ~**5.26%**.
