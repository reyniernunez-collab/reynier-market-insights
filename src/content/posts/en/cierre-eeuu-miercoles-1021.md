> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 21, 2026, 3:43 PM CT:** On Tuesday, the New York session bought everything except chips. Wednesday was the reverse: tech took over thanks to SpaceX results far above expectations, while oil, the dollar and bonds barely moved. France is still on the radar, but today it didn't hold anyone back.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the Nasdaq Composite rose **1.1%** to **26,702.86**, the S&P 500 **0.6%** to **7,757.17** and the Dow **0.7%** to **52,856.09**. The S&P 500 finished less than 1% below the all-time high it set earlier this month. Tech was the index's best sector and semiconductors rallied hard (approx. 2%, ⚠️ single-source figure), doing what they couldn't on Tuesday.

The driver was earnings season, specifically the part that matters most right now: companies tied to artificial intelligence. The question weighing on the market in recent weeks was how long the huge AI investment takes to pay off. Today the market got two favorable answers and bought them. The brake came from telecoms, which fear a new and bigger competitor.

## Analysis

### Stocks: tech takes charge again

SpaceX rose approx. **8%** after reporting, following Tuesday's close, its first profitable quarter since listing, with results well above forecasts. On top of that, Elon Musk called a possible merger with Tesla a good idea, and both companies have committees studying it. Tesla rose approx. 3.5% before releasing its own numbers.

The other push came from Super Micro, which forecast revenue and margins above expectations and gained approx. **9%**, lifting other server makers. The read of the day: AI demand remains firm and, for now, the numbers are backing it up.

Not everything was green. AT&T fell approx. 6.5% despite beating forecasts, because the market is starting to see SpaceX as a serious rival in mobile; Netflix also slipped after its results. That drop in telecoms capped the S&P 500's gain.

Meta and Tesla reported after the close. The first after-hours reactions were positive, with Meta rising sharply on a better-than-expected sales outlook (⚠️ preliminary, unconfirmed moves). The Asia session will be the first test of whether that read holds.

### Oil: lots of noise, flat close

Brent rose more than $2 at one point in the session, near five-month highs, and ended up settling at approx. **$108.8**, practically unchanged from Tuesday. December WTI closed at approx. **$97.2** (−0.7%).

Inventories pushed prices up: US crude stocks fell approx. **3.1 million** barrels, versus an expected draw of approx. 0.8 million, and gasoline also fell more than forecast. Distillates, on the other hand, rose when a drop was expected. Headlines weighed on the downside: Iran said it is open to diplomacy but sees little room for progress, and there were reports that it rejected Washington's demands (⚠️ unconfirmed). The US approved new sanctions but allowed cargo to be offloaded from some blocked vessels, which coincided with the day's lows. Washington also said Russia accepted a three-way meeting with Ukraine.

Bottom line: Brent remains inside the sideways range of recent days. The risk premium isn't going away, but it isn't growing either without a confirmed supply cut.

### Dollar and bonds: a day without data

With no relevant US data and the Fed in its quiet period until October 29, the dollar was flat: DXY approx. **101.7**. The US 10-year yield edged up to approx. **5.13–5.15%** (⚠️ readings differ), and USD/JPY stood at approx. **158.0–158.2**. Gold, near **$4,150**, still isn't acting as a safe haven.

Sterling kept what it gained in the Europe session: GBP/USD approx. **1.339** and EUR/GBP approx. **0.838**, near 17-month lows for the euro against the pound. The Bank of England governor, the big question mark for the New York leg, spoke about central bank independence and steered clear of current monetary policy. Without that test, the market still prices the November 5 hike almost fully, and the debate over how many hikes follow is left for another day. EUR/USD closed at approx. **1.123**, nearly unchanged.

### Europe: France didn't stop stocks

At the Europe close, the DAX rose **1.4%** to **25,645.04**, the CAC 40 **1.1%** to **7,921.68** and the FTSE 100 **0.2%** to **10,752.88**. The CAC stands out: it rose more than 1% on the same day the French 10-year yield touched 5% for the first time since 2002. For now, French debt stress is staying in bonds and in the euro-sterling cross, without spilling into equities. France's credit rating review arrives on Friday.

### Scenarios for the next sessions

- **If the reactions to Meta and Tesla hold into the Asia session:** tech strings together a second day of relief and Seoul and Taiwan can extend the chip rebound; if they fade, the doubt about the return on AI spending comes back.
- **If New Zealand CPI comes in firm on its domestic component:** the New Zealand central bank's October hike is locked in and the kiwi can claw back ground against the Aussie; a soft print would put that hike in doubt.
- **If Brent clearly breaks this week's high (approx. $112):** event mode returns, pressuring the yen and the euro; without a confirmed supply cut, the sideways range remains the base case.
- **If the French premium breaks above its October highs before Friday's review:** the euro could start to suffer against the dollar too, not just against sterling.
