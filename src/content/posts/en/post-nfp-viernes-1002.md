> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 2, 2026, 14:30 CDT:** September payrolls came in at **+29,000** versus ~90,000 expected —below the entire range of estimates— with July-August revised down by about 60,000 and wages at **3.0% year over year**, the lowest since 2021. Unemployment rose to **4.2%**. Swaps no longer price a full Fed hike in 2026: October "no change" moves to **~83%** and December stays at **~70%**. The dollar and yields give way; the S&P rises and the VIX falls ~5% to **15.6**. The yen is the day's strongest currency and CAD the weakest. France is still the exception: the OAT-Bund spread widens while Bunds and Treasuries are bought. Brent/WTI ease on proposals to release diesel and crude (Macron: up to 100 million barrels).

## Executive Summary

September's NFP was a miss on both quantity and price: **+29k** payrolls (12-month average: +45k), private +46k, government −17k, unemployment **4.2%**, average hourly earnings +0.1% m/m and **3.0% y/y**. The print removes the "stable labor market that allows another hike now" argument Williams had reopened: it doesn't imply cuts, it implies less urgency. Post-print: Fed **October hold ~83% / hike ~17%** and **December hike ~70%** — October is practically dead and December is the only residual hike of 2026, no longer a full hike for the year. **JPY** leads the session (rate differential + Asian safe-haven demand), **CAD** is the weakest in G10, **CHF** follows on flows out of OATs. **Brent** tactically bearish on the plan to release up to 100 mln bbl; **gold** tactically bullish with yields and the dollar lower. Traditional Fear & Greed at **32** (fear); crypto at **72** (greed): the gap is duration versus risk beta.

## Detailed Analysis

### Jobs and inflation

BLS: nonfarm payrolls **+29,000**, versus an average of **+45,000** over the prior twelve months and below the entire range of estimates. Private **+46,000**; government **−17,000**. Unemployment **4.2%** (7.1 million). Average hourly earnings +5 cents: **+0.1% m/m** and **+3.0% y/y**, the lowest since 2021. The miss is on both quantity and price: it removes the "stable labor market that allows another hike now" argument Williams had reopened. It doesn't imply cuts. It implies less urgency.

### Monetary policy

Before the NFP, the futures table had October hold at 74% and December hike at 73%. After the print, the FJ tape puts October hold at **82.8%** and December hike near **70%**. An external read (FedWatch) leaves October hike at ~22% and December still priced. The divergence between venues doesn't change the sign: **October is practically dead; December is the only residual hike of 2026 and no longer adds up to a full hike for the year**. ECB October hold 85% and December hike 70% remain relatively more hawkish than the short-term Fed, but French fiscal stress cancels that advantage. BoJ: October hold 86%, no 50 bp — the yen leads on rate differentials and safe-haven demand, not on the Summary.

### Geopolitics and energy

The US asked major European countries to release diesel (the tape mentions 800,000 kilotonnes over six months; the unit isn't confirmed by a primary source). France proposed 50 million barrels of European diesel and 50 million of crude via the IEA; Macron spoke of **up to 100 million**. WTI broke toward **88–89** and is back in the ~94 band, below recent resistance. The US–Iran diplomatic tone remains sour; an attributable incident would reopen the premium. Pakistan mediates with Riyadh on the Houthis next week: political rather than kinetic engagement is bearish for crude; a kinetic turn reverses it.

### Sentiment and flows

US, European and Asia-Pacific equities positive, with APAC leading the day before and the rest flatter. Precious metals, base metals and crypto higher. "Safe" bonds bought (prices up, yields down). OATs the other way: prices down, yields up. Moody's keeps the focus on political fragmentation and on the uncertain 2027 budget ahead of the spring elections. CNN Fear & Greed at **32** (previous close 28, a week ago 36, a month ago 46). Crypto at **72**. The gap is duration versus risk beta: equities price fewer hikes; French bonds price governance.

### Cross-asset correlations

The 60d oil-beta (CL-EUR **0.77**, CL-GBP **0.79**) is breaking down on the day: crude lower and EUR not leading. The yen leads on rate differentials and Asian safe-haven demand, not on the BoJ Summary. CHF follows on OAT flows, behind the yen. AUD and NZD recover with the rates risk-on; CAD doesn't, despite lower crude, because US jobs beta and the USDCAD level dominate.

## Asset-by-asset view

**EUR** — Relatively bearish. A weak dollar isn't enough: France and the OAT spread weigh more than the payrolls miss. Goldman keeps a ceiling bias on EUR/USD while the fiscal issue stays open.

**GBP** — Neutral / slightly bullish on the day. Little beta of its own; it benefits from the dollar, not from a domestic catalyst.

**JPY** — Bullish on the day (USD/JPY bearish). It's the session's strongest. The 1SD ceiling (~162) moves further away; the relevant floor is the post-print pullback, not a BoJ regime change.

**CAD** — Bearish. Worst in G10 on the day. Lower crude and US jobs beta.

**AUD** — Tactically bullish. First of the "hybrids" with NZD. Sensitive to a repricing of hikes that isn't confirmed on Monday.

**NZD** — Slightly bullish. Same basket as AUD, less conviction (RBNZ October 56%).

**CHF** — Bullish. OAT flows and the memory of 2010–11. SNB in no rush (December hold 85%).

**USD** — Bearish on the day, firm on the week until the print. The miss removes the October hike; it doesn't remove the 10-year term premium if France and the Gulf don't calm down.

**Gold** — Tactically bullish. Yields down and the dollar down. The previous table level was ~4,146; the day's flow has precious metals in the green.

**Brent** — Tactically bearish. Proposed releases and WTI in an 89–94 band. The military premium isn't dead.
