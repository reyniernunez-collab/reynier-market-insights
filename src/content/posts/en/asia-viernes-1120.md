**REYNIER MARKET INSIGHTS**

Dallas, Texas — Nov 20, 2026, 2:01 AM CT

**Executive summary:** The Asia session was the roughest of the week. Doubts over whether the huge investment in artificial intelligence will deliver returns that justify valuations hit the markets most exposed to chips: Japan fell between 4.7% and 5.1% ⚠️ (conflicting figures), Korea between 5.2% and 5.7% ⚠️ (conflicting figures) and Taiwan more than 4% ⚠️ (another gauge shows 6.2%). Hong Kong lost approx. 2.3%, Australia approx. 1.6% and Shanghai approx. 1.2%. The Asia-Pacific regional index is heading for its worst week since March 2020, global stocks are on a seven-session losing streak (the longest since September 2023) and emerging markets are now approx. 20% below their June highs. After the New York close, with the Nasdaq 100 at its lowest since April, futures kept sliding: S&P 500 approx. −0.7% (7,150) and Nasdaq 100 approx. −1.3%. Anthropic's market debut didn't help either: the stock slipped approx. 4.7% after hours ⚠️ (single source). What matters for currencies is that this time risk aversion did not lift the dollar: the yen acted as a haven (USD/JPY fell from 159.84 to approx. 159.1), Treasuries drew buying (US2Y approx. 4.73% ⚠️, single source) and the dollar index hit its session low. Late in Asia, sterling rose to its high (1.3122) after UK retail sales came in far above forecasts (+0.7% vs. 0.0%; core +0.9%). Gold did not work as a haven: futures lost approx. 3.6% ($3,553) ⚠️ (spot is down approx. 1.9%). The AUD rose on a conciliatory Xi–Albanese tone, and China left its benchmark lending rates unchanged.
