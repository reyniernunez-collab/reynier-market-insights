> **Dallas, Texas — Nov 5, 2026, 02:01 CT:** Thursday's Asia session left a clear message: the market is selling Asian technology on the regulatory risk it reads into a Democratic Congress, not the Federal Reserve. Korea took the worst of it, China tech rebounded, the dollar marked another month low and then recovered ground just before Europe. Today belongs to the Bank of England.

## Executive Summary

The Asia close leaves **Asian equities deeply split**, with a **Kospi in freefall**, **China tech higher**, **Australia strong** and **Wall Street futures red in the Nasdaq**. The **dollar** touched another one-month low and bounced without a clear headline; **gold** is rising again and **oil** is stabilizing after Wednesday's plunge.

My read: the night confirms Wednesday's angle, but with sharper focus. What the market is punishing is not the Fed script — which barely moves — but AI-linked stocks on the idea of a more interventionist Congress. Meanwhile, crude holds the lower zone after Wednesday's drop of more than 8%, and attention shifts to this morning's Bank of England decision.

## Analysis

### Korea: the blow to chips

South Korea had the toughest session. The **Kospi** closed down approx. **5.3%**, with heavy foreign selling. **Samsung** and **SK Hynix** fell between approx. **7% and 10%**. The story the market keeps repeating is fear that the new Congress will tighten regulation of artificial-intelligence data centers.

**Nasdaq 100 futures** are down about **1%**, in the same direction. The night's message is sectoral: AI-linked tech is being sold, not risk as a whole.

### Asian divergence: China tech and Australia

Not all of Asia was in the red. **Tencent**, **Alibaba** and **Hang Seng Tech** advanced strongly — around 5% or more for Hong Kong's tech index — with Chinese consumption and tech leading the buying. **Australia** rose more than **2.5%**, driven by gold and metals miners, consistent with another advance in the yellow metal.

In **Japan**, the **Nikkei** fell nearly **1.8%**. India, by contrast, finished higher. The Asia session map is therefore one of divergence: Korean chips and Japan lower; China tech, Australia and parts of consumption higher.

### Dollar: another low and a rebound without a clear catalyst

The dollar index fell as much as about **0.3%** in the Asian overnight, to the lowest zone in a month (low near **101.20**), and in the final hour before Europe recovered much of the slide, back toward **101.35–101.40**. There is no clear headline explaining that turn, so I flag it with caution.

Approximate levels into the Asia close / Europe open:

- **EUR/USD:** around **1.124–1.125**, after touching near **1.126–1.127** and stalling again in the same high zone of the previous two days.
- **GBP/USD:** around **1.347**, after approaching **1.350**. Sterling also gave back part of the advance hours before the Bank of England.
- **USD/JPY:** around **158.1**, after dropping about 60 pips and handing most of the move back. The yen still cannot get lasting traction despite the Asian equity slide and Bank of Japan minutes that keep a hiking bias.
- **AUD/USD:** around **0.701**, supported by Australian risk and gold.
- **USD/CHF and USD/CAD:** the dollar remains soft against most majors, although the overnight rebound cut part of the losses.

**Implied volatility** in the main currencies is falling for a second day, including sterling despite today's Bank of England meeting: the market expects little surprise in the hike.

### Gold and oil

**Gold** rose a little more than **1%** and trades around **$4,130**, with a weak dollar and demand for protection still present after the political turn.

**Oil** is stabilizing after Wednesday's plunge: **Brent** trades around **$94–95** (it marked near $93.9 on some overnight prints) and **WTI** around **$87–88**. Iran claims a tanker hit a mine and that other vessels caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz; this is an **Iranian claim with no independent verification**, and crude has not reacted aggressively to that headline.

### Overnight data

**German factory orders** rebounded hard in September (**+4.4%** month on month, in line with expectations) after August's collapse. In **Australia**, the trade surplus came in much larger than forecast. Japan's final services PMI settled at **51.7**, a touch below the flash.

### Central banks: what the market is pricing

I give ranges because the measures differ. No next-meeting probability moved by 20 points or more:

- **Fed (Dec 9):** hike at approx. **78–81%**, steady.
- **ECB (Dec 17):** hike at approx. **86–89%**, steady.
- **Bank of England (today):** hike at approx. **94–98%**; the market expects a move from 3.75% to 4.00%, with a vote split of roughly 7 for a hike and 2 for no change. For December, a second hike is around approx. **23–27%**.
- **Bank of Japan (December):** hike at approx. **79–89%** (measures differ).
- **Bank of Canada (December):** between approx. **30% and 48%**, too scattered.
- **Reserve Bank of Australia (December):** approx. **3–8%**; one official said inflation remains "too high" (single source).
- **Reserve Bank of New Zealand (December):** between approx. **58% and 74%**, with widely different measures.
- **Swiss National Bank (December):** approx. **9–11%**.

For reference, the 30-day expected move implied by options is around approx. 1.5% in the euro and sterling, approx. 1.8% in the yen and the Australian dollar, approx. 6% in gold and approx. 16% in crude. Currency volatility keeps falling; gold volatility is edging higher with the price.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If the Bank of England hikes to 4.00% with the expected vote split (approx. 7–2) and soft December guidance**, sterling could give back part of the accumulated premium: the hike is already almost fully priced. A harder tone, or fewer votes for no change, would be the upside surprise for the pound.
- **If the dollar confirms the overnight rebound** without a clear catalyst, the market would be treating the month low as an Asian-session excess. If, instead, it returns to the lows, the political theme would still be driving the exchange rate.
- **If regulatory pressure on artificial intelligence stays in the headlines**, Korea and the Nasdaq would remain the weak spot, even as Fed expectations barely move.
- **If the Hormuz incidents are independently verified**, crude could leave the $94–95 zone more forcefully than it has so far.
- **If US weekly jobless claims come in firm**, they would reinforce the idea that the market is selling tech, not the Fed script, one day before tomorrow's jobs report.
