> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 28, 2026, 08:20 CT:** Wednesday's Europe session was a waiting game. The euro defended the 1.11 area again, the dollar didn't move and US yields gave back the overnight rise. The only currency in a hurry to fall was sterling, hours before its Budget. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, the story was New Zealand's surprise pause and an Australia edging closer to a hike. The Europe session didn't change that divergence (AUD/NZD is still at approx. 1.254, highs since 2013), but **the rest of the market stood still**: three central banks are on the calendar (Canada and the Fed today, the ECB tomorrow) and nobody wanted to take risk before hearing them.

My read: **almost everything is priced, so what moves markets won't be the decisions but the tone**. A Fed on hold is priced across every source, and so is a December hike. The Bank of Canada is the exception: the market treats a hike as a done deal that economists don't see as clearly.

## Analysis

### The euro defends 1.11 for a third time

- **EUR/USD:** ends the Europe session at approx. **1.1130**, a few pips above Tuesday's New York session close and the Asia session close (approx. 1.1115–1.1119). The low, approx. **1.1111**, came early in Europe and wasn't tested again. It's the third time the 1.1100–1.1111 zone has held.
- **Dollar:** the index finished the session at approx. **102.35**, practically unchanged from Tuesday's New York session close, after tagging approx. 102.49 early on.
- **US rates:** the **10-year eased from approx. 5.03% to 5.00%** and the 2-year held at approx. 4.74%. As New York opened, the 2-year climbed back toward approx. 4.76%: the bond market is still nervous ahead of the Fed.

The key is the mix: **the dollar lost the support of yields, but didn't fall**. That's typical before a big event: positions get closed, not opened.

### Europe: soft data and the French spread back up

- **Germany:** November consumer confidence slipped to **−30.8** (prior −30.6), a bit worse than most forecasts (⚠️ consensus ranged from approx. −30.1 to −30.9). The euro didn't react.
- **Italy:** September producer prices rose **2.9% on the month and 13.8% on the year** (priors 2.4% and 10.9%), driven by energy. This is cost inflation that supports the idea of an ECB that waits now and hikes in December.
- **Bunds:** the German 10-year auction sold at **3.37%** with soft demand (cover of 1.1 versus 1.2 last time).
- **France:** the **French 10-year spread over Germany rises to approx. 159 bp**, from about 155 bp at Tuesday's New York session close (⚠️ approximate calculation using 10-year yields). Together with weak growth, it's holding back bets on a December ECB hike, which keep fading in prediction markets.
- **Switzerland:** economic expectations plunged to approx. −15 from positive territory (⚠️ single-source figure, no consensus). The franc didn't notice.
- **Stocks:** the broad European index is down approx. 0.3% on mixed bank earnings; Wall Street futures are slightly higher (Nasdaq approx. +0.4%) ahead of Microsoft and Alphabet earnings this afternoon.

### Sterling heads into the Budget weak

Sterling was the hardest hit among the majors: **GBP/USD ends the Europe session at approx. 1.3287** (about 15–20 pips below Tuesday's New York session close) and **EUR/GBP rises to approx. 0.8376**. The 10-year gilt sat at approx. 5.36%.

The Budget, which began at 7:30 AM CT, comes with **opposite readings**: some think the market already prices a friendly Budget and that the easy path for sterling and gilts is lower; others see a risk premium in gilts that could come out if the numbers convince. The leaked energy relief measures would lower inflation mechanically, which would weigh on a Bank of England hike in December. The November meeting (approx. 97% hike priced) shouldn't change. **Until we see how gilts react, sterling has no reliable direction.** This analysis closes with the Europe session and doesn't include the reaction to the speech.

### Oil: conflicting headlines on Iran

**December Brent** hit approx. **$116.35** early in Europe and fell to approx. $114.36 after a report of a new US–Iran draft deal mediated by Qatar. Shortly after, a senior Iranian official denied there was a new plan (⚠️ both headlines single-source, unconfirmed). At the Europe session close it was around approx. **$115.3**, still above Tuesday's close. Crude is still the only thing paying volatility: its 30-day expected move is approx. 17%, versus under 2% for EUR/USD.

### Bank of Canada: the least settled meeting

The market prices approx. 77–84% odds of a hike to 2.50%, but in a recent survey only 19 of 34 economists expected one. New Zealand's night was a reminder that "priced" isn't "decided". And even with a hike, a "one and see" message would trim bets on a second hike in December. It's the most asymmetric meeting of the day for the Canadian dollar.

### Conditional scenarios for the New York session

1. **Fed on hold with no new guidance (the most priced):** with December steady at approx. 77–82%, the dollar would move little and the action would be in the Canadian dollar and sterling, not the euro.
2. **A more hawkish Fed than expected:** several votes for an immediate hike or a message validating December would lift those odds to approx. 85–90% and the 2-year above approx. 4.78%; that would be the most serious test yet for the euro's 1.11 zone.
3. **A more neutral Fed:** with no dissents and no hints on December, the odds could slip toward approx. 65–70% and the 2-year below approx. 4.70%; the market could unwind part of the dollar buying of recent weeks.
4. **Bank of Canada:** a hike with guidance of more tightening would support the Canadian dollar; a pause, or a hike with a "one and see" tone, would weaken it.
5. **The Gulf:** a confirmed US–Iran deal would cheapen crude and relieve the euro; an escalation would send it back to highs, with the euro and the yen among the most exposed.
