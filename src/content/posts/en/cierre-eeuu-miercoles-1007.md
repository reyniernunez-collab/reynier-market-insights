> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 7, 2026, 15:20 CT:** Wall Street couldn't build on Tuesday's record. With Brent stuck near $100 and Fed minutes openly discussing another hike, the market was reminded that inflation is still the main risk. The drop was modest, though: a well-bid 10-year auction took pressure off yields at midday.

## Executive Summary

At the New York close, the S&P 500 ends at **7,801.77** (−0.22%), a day after setting its first all-time high since August. The Nasdaq 100 slips 0.21% to 31,160 and the Dow Jones 0.66% to 51,180. The Russell 2000 lags again (−1.31%). The VIX barely moves and closes around **15.1**. Two moments defined the day. At midday, the $39 billion 10-year note sale drew solid demand and stopped the climb in yields. An hour later, the FOMC minutes confirmed that the Fed still leans toward hiking. Expensive oil, yields near two-decade highs and a hawkish Fed add up to a market that needs strong earnings to keep climbing.

## Analysis

### Stocks: a pause after the record, and small caps struggle again

The S&P 500 opened near the bottom of its range (about 7,763 at the low) and recovered through the session without turning positive. The drop is small, but breadth is the weak spot again. The Russell 2000 loses 1.31% and has now fallen two days in a row while the S&P 500 holds near its record. Smaller companies rely more on credit and feel the 10-year above 5% more. Until they join in, the index's gains still hang on the big tech names.

On the AI front, SpaceX is reportedly in talks with banks and investors to raise about $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips. It would be one of the largest debt deals tied to AI infrastructure. It's another sign of how much capital AI is absorbing and why corporate bond supply keeps growing, which also pushes yields higher. Options were pricing a ~**3.9%** move over 30 days for S&P 500 futures early this morning, and ~**5.8%** for Nasdaq 100 futures, with earnings season about to start.

### Fed: minutes that confirm the hawkish lean

The minutes of the September 15–16 meeting show all 19 participants backed the 25 bp hike. Most see another hike in 2026 as likely appropriate, and many justify it as insurance against inflation risk. Almost all see inflation risks tilted to the upside, while they see job market risks as broadly balanced. Some participants think AI investment could push demand above supply over the medium term. Several see the current rate as not, or only mildly, restrictive. It's a clearly hawkish message, although it covers a meeting held before the weak payrolls report and the softer core PCE of recent weeks.

This morning, before the minutes, the market priced about a **21%** chance of an October hike and about **85%** for December. The tone of the minutes fits that view: hold now, with a hike likely before year-end. On top of that, the New York Fed survey lifted one-year inflation expectations to **3.9%**, the highest since May 2023. Three-year expectations rise to 3.3% and five-year expectations hold at 3.0%. Stable long-term expectations are what, for now, give the Fed room not to pull the hike forward to October.

### Bonds: the 10-year auction puts on the brakes

The 10-year Treasury yield reached about **5.36%** early and then eased. The $39 billion auction cleared at **5.30%**, the highest yield at a 10-year sale since November 2000. Even so, it came in 1.7 bp below the pre-auction level, with demand of 2.77 times the amount offered, up from last time. In other words, there are buyers at these levels. The 10-year closes around **5.28–5.29%** and the 2-year eases to about **4.77%**, so the curve steepens a bit. The underlying concern hasn't gone away. Investors are demanding more yield because of inflation, government spending and the debt being issued to fund AI. The 30-year auction is tomorrow.

### Dollar, oil and gold: energy still sets the tone

The dollar ends the day firm. The DXY rises about 0.5% to around **102.3**, near its highest since April 2025. EUR/USD closes near **1.120** after touching about 1.117 in the morning, still weighed down by the French risk premium. USD/JPY sits around **158.1**, close to the 158.5–160 area where intervention risk rises. Brent settles at **$100.20** (−0.4%) and WTI at **$88.28** (−1.3%). There were new attacks on tankers near Hormuz, and a storm in the Gulf of Mexico shut in roughly a quarter of the region's oil output, yet crude couldn't hold above $102. Gold falls 1.4% to about **$4,130** as the dollar rises. Bitcoin drops 2.4% to around $83,500.

One data note: consumer credit grew by $8.28 billion in August, well below the $15 billion expected. It's another sign that US consumers are slowing down with rates this high.
