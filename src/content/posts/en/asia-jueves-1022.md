> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 22, 2026, 01:53 CT:** Thursday's Asia session belonged to the Gulf. New attacks on ships, reports of military plans against Iran after the US elections and the departure of staff from Saudi Arabia pushed oil higher and knocked down the region's tech stocks. The striking part: the haven was not the dollar.

## Executive Summary

The night brought **classic risk aversion** in stocks and the yen, with one important twist: the dollar did not benefit. Money sought protection in **US Treasuries, the yen and the Swiss franc**, while the dollar slipped against almost every currency.

**Brent** climbed step by step to approx. **$111.6**, very close to the week's high. Asian stocks fell sharply, led by chips and with Korea at the front, on a day that combined the Gulf, mixed results from big tech names and the existing doubts about the payoff from AI spending.

## Analysis

### The Gulf is back at the center

During the Asia session, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four ships were hit and set on fire in the northern Gulf and three more struck mines west of Hormuz, and warned its response could extend to US bases. At the same time, US press reports described plans by the US and Israel for a short, intense operation against Iran after the November 3 midterm elections, including energy infrastructure. Washington also ordered non-essential staff out of Saudi Arabia.

There were also headlines about explosions in southern Iran of unconfirmed origin, and Russia says it is willing to resume Ukraine peace talks next month (single-source figure). Neither changed the tone.

The takeaway: geopolitical risk is no longer just background noise and is starting to have a **calendar**, which explains why the market is paying more premium as November approaches.

### Oil: a stepwise climb, no breakout yet

Brent went from a settlement of approx. **$108.8** on Wednesday to approx. **$111.6** (approx. +2.5%), with a high of approx. **$111.75** around 1:35 AM CT. Every hourly close overnight was higher than the one before. US crude is around **$99.4** (approx. +2.3%).

Wednesday's larger-than-expected drop in US inventories gives the move a physical base. Even so, Brent is still below the week's high (approx. $112). As long as it doesn't clearly clear it, we read this as a **high risk premium**; above it, the market would start pricing an event.

### Stocks: chips pay for the night

At the Asia session close:

- **Kospi:** approx. **−3.5%**, with the big chipmakers between −4% and −5.5%.
- **Nikkei:** approx. **−2.65%**.
- **Hong Kong:** approx. **−3%**. **Taiwan:** approx. −1.8%. **Shanghai:** approx. −1.1%.
- **Australia:** approx. −0.4%, the most resilient.

On top of the Gulf pressure came mixed results from big US tech names in after-hours trading: an electric carmaker cut its investment plans and is down approx. 3%, and a chip equipment supplier falls approx. 7% on weak guidance, while a large social media company gains approx. 2% (these figures are single-source). **Nasdaq 100** futures are down approx. **1.6%** and **S&P 500** futures approx. **0.9%**.

The Bank of Korea held its rate at 3.00% with a clearly hawkish tone, open to further hikes. It didn't stop the slide: the night was about global risk, not local policy.

### FX and metals: a haven without the dollar

Indicative readings versus Wednesday's New York session close:

- **USD/JPY** approx. **157.1** (approx. −0.65%) and **USD/CHF** approx. **0.820** (approx. −0.45%): the yen and the franc were the night's havens.
- **US 10-year** approx. **5.08%** (about −4 bp); the 2-year, approx. 4.78%.
- **DXY** approx. **101.5** (approx. −0.2%).
- **EUR/USD** approx. **1.125** (about +15–20 pips), more on dollar weakness than euro strength. **GBP/USD** approx. 1.340.
- **AUD/USD** approx. **0.701** (approx. −0.25%): risk aversion outweighs its central bank.
- **USD/CAD** approx. **1.402**: the CAD leans on oil.

**Gold** falls approx. **1.2%**, to approx. **$4,108**, and silver approx. 3%. Metals failing to act as havens on a night like this suggests a weak dollar and high real yields are still in charge of them.

### France, ahead of the European open

The French 10-year spread over Germany is trading in a range of approx. **161–163 bp** before the open (single-source calculation, with the market still closed), above the approx. 159 bp area of Wednesday's Europe session close. It needs confirming once the market opens; France's credit rating review comes on Friday.

## Scenarios for Europe and New York

- **If Brent breaks above approx. $112** with hourly closes above it, the market would shift to pricing an escalation: more demand for the yen and the franc, pressure on the euro through the energy bill and support for the CAD. If it stalls below, the premium holds without making the jump.
- **If Asia's risk aversion spreads to Europe**, European tech and banks would be the most exposed; if the open stops it, the night would stay a Gulf-driven episode.
- **If the French spread holds above 160 bp** once the market opens, the question is whether it finally weighs on the euro or the weak dollar keeps offsetting it.
- **If the dollar keeps failing as a haven**, the yen and the franc will remain the currencies to watch on every new Gulf headline.
