> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 5, 2026, 10:30 CT:** Europe takes the air out of the rally. What was an upbeat Monday in Asia turns directionless once Europe opens: political gridlock in France and an early election in Spain put the eurozone's public finances back in the spotlight, and the euro is paying for it.

## Executive Summary

Markets start the week with their eyes on Europe. Policy paralysis in France triggers selling in the region's most fragile debt, and the contagion reaches Spain, where Pedro Sánchez has called an early election amid heavy housing protests. The euro slips ~**0.4%** to a **17-month** low against the dollar; Bunds rise on haven demand while French, Italian and Spanish yields climb. In the US, S&P 500 futures are down ~**0.2%** and the Nasdaq 100 sits just below its record. Brent hovers near **$102**.

## Analysis

### Euro: politics outweighs the data

The euro extends the slide it brought from Asia and hits its lowest against the dollar in 17 months. The driver is political, not macro: gridlock in Paris and now election uncertainty in Madrid. As long as markets doubt governments' ability to balance their budgets, the euro will struggle to recover, even with the ECB still leaning toward a December hike (around **83%** odds, with October ~**85%** on hold per our daily data).

### European debt: Bunds as a haven, the periphery punished

This is the textbook fiscal-stress pattern: buy German debt, sell French, Italian and Spanish bonds. Spain joining the group is today's new element, widening the story from "France" to "the periphery." Treasuries, by contrast, barely move, with slight weakness at the long end.

### Equities: Asia rallied, Europe hesitates, the US waits

Asian stocks followed Friday's Wall Street rally, but Europe didn't buy it: the CAC 40 falls, weighed down by domestic politics. S&P 500 futures slip ~0.2% and the Nasdaq 100 stalls just shy of its all-time high. Without a fresh catalyst, European risk acts as a brake.

### Oil: supply keeps a floor under prices

Brent trades around **$102**. On one side, Saudi Arabia cut the price of its benchmark crude for Asia; on the other, low stockpiles and escalating fighting in Yemen keep the Middle East risk premium alive. Net result: the barrel holds above $100.

## What I'm watching today

- **Euro:** whether the 17-month low holds into the US session or the dollar keeps gaining as Europe's fiscal worries grow.
- **Spreads:** France, Italy and Spain versus Bunds; further widening would signal the stress is spreading.
- **Nasdaq 100:** whether it takes another run at its record high or European noise drains Wall Street's momentum.
- **Brent:** the $100 level as support while tension in Yemen persists.
