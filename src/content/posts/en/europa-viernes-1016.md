> **Dallas, Texas — Oct 16, 2026, 08:15 CT:** The Europe session closes the week with the dollar still soft after Warsh's remarks, a European CPI with no surprises and Brent back in escalation mode near $106. This post sums up the landscape Europe leaves as New York opens: what's priced, what really moved the euro and the conditional scenarios the US data block brings.

## Executive Summary

In the Asia session post, Warsh had cooled the odds of an October Fed hike, the dollar was giving ground and Brent was losing momentum with several hourly closes below $105.5. The Europe session **keeps the dollar picture and changes the oil picture**.

The dollar index extended its decline to a Europe-session low near **101.58** and trades around **101.67**. The euro ground higher toward **1.126** and sterling toward **1.334**, while AUD bounced near **0.696**. Final euro-area CPI was a non-event: it matched the flash exactly, and the euro kept rising after the release, which signals the push is coming from the dollar and oil side.

Brent, which had left the escalation on watch in Asia, strung together hourly closes above $105.5 again and trades near **$106.4**. Into New York, the key is that much of the move is already in the price: the morning US data block decides whether the dollar recovers or the weakness extends.

## Analysis

### Fed: October cools, December still stands

After Warsh's remarks, which offered no near-term guidance, the market removed roughly half of the hike probability for the October 28 meeting. In the Europe session the live metrics **diverged**: they range from approx. **12.5%** to **18%**, with a midpoint near 14–15%. We treat that figure as a range, not an exact number.

December is a different story: the odds of at least one move remain approx. **80–90%** depending on the metric. In other words, the market hasn't abandoned the idea of a more hawkish Fed; it just pushed it past October.

### FX: the dollar is in charge, not European inflation

- **EUR/USD:** climbs toward **1.126** (Europe range approx. 1.123–1.127). It is already above the large option-expiry zones at 1.1200 and 1.1225; the approx. **1.1260–1.1270** band gains weight into the 9:00 AM CT option cut.
- **GBP/USD:** firm near **1.334**. A UK growth analysis revised third-quarter GDP tracking higher (⚠️ single-source figure).
- **AUD/USD:** bounces near **0.696**; odds of an RBA hike in November remain low-conviction.
- **USD/JPY:** near **158**, a level with relevant option expiries; the BoJ's hawkish tone caps the upside.
- **USD/CAD:** lower, with CAD supported by the soft dollar and higher oil.

In the euro area, headline inflation would stay near its peak toward late 2026, with faster normalization in 2027–28 if fuel prices ease (⚠️ single-source projection). For the ECB, an October 29 pause is practically priced.

### Oil: the escalation is active again

In Asia, the rule we track (Brent hourly closes at or above $105.5) had weakened. In Europe, the last three hourly closes moved back above it and price is around **$106.4**, with a session high near $106.75. A headline about a tanker on fire near Hormuz accompanies the move, with no details on supply impact. In WTI, price series diverge; we prefer not to pin an exact level.

### Equities and bonds

S&P 500 futures are around **7,799** and Nasdaq 100 futures near **30,100**, with the VIX at approx. **16.1**. Treasuries are on track for a week of strong gains despite some rise in yields during the Europe session (⚠️ single-source reading).

A background point for the dollar: as long as US short-term rates don't break lower, the dollar's bias tends to hold, because inflation is still hot. That's why this week's weakness is tactical and vulnerable to data.

### Conditional scenarios into New York (without inventing the print)

The morning block is import prices (7:30 AM CT; consensus between 0.8% and 0.9% depending on the calendar ⚠️) and industrial production (8:15 AM CT; capacity approx. 76.5%, output approx. 0.4%).

1. **Hot data:** the dollar can regain ground and the euro can give back from the 1.126–1.127 expiry zone. A high annual pace in import prices would reinforce the persistent-inflation story (⚠️ the advance estimate of a pace near 8% is single-source).
2. **In-line data:** range trading; October Fed odds stay in the mid-teens; oil and the option cut set the tone.
3. **Weak data:** the dollar extends its slide, with the index toward or below the Europe-session low, and the euro has room above the Europe high.

Oil branch: if Brent holds hourly closes above $105.5, the escalation stays active; losing them on a sustained basis puts it back on watch.
